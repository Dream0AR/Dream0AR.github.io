The Mayer-Vietoris sequence is a classic conclusion in algebraic topology and is of great help in calculating homology groups. It certainly exists in de Rham complexes as well. Cohomological MV sequence ---------- ### Short exact sequence of complex In algebraic topology we know that if there is a short exact sequence of complex $$0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0,$$ Then according to the zig-zag lemma (graph tracking), a long exact sequence can be constructed $$...\rightarrow H^p(A)\rightarrow H^p(B)\rightarrow H^p(C)\rightarrow H^{p+\varepsilon}(A)\rightarrow ...$$ where $\varepsilon=\pm 1$, depends on whether cohomology or hypohomology is considered. Therefore, in order to obtain the MV sequence, We only need to construct the short exact sequence of the complex. Let $U, V$ be the open subset of the manifold $M$, $M=U\cup V$, $U\cap V$ is open, and there is $U$ on it, Inclusion mapping of $V$: $$\partial_0:U\cap V\rightarrow V, \quad \partial_1:U\cap V\rightarrow U,$$ The number in the subscript refers to the forgotten position, which is used to consider more general MV sequences later (multiple open sets can be listed). Let $U\sqcup V$ be the intersection-free union of the two, also called the topological sum (because when viewed in the same space, Of course they intersect). Note that it is not a Cartesian product. Then there is a map $U\sqcup V\rightarrow M$ induced by the containing map $U,V\rightarrow M$, Thus we have mapped columns $$M=U\cup V\leftarrow U\sqcup V\mathop{\leftleftarrows}\limits_{\partial_1}^{\partial_0} U\cap V,$$ The double arrow here points to selecting one of the mappings, that is, in fact we have two mapping columns. Apply the inverse functor $\Omega^\ast$ and do pullback mapping to get $$\Omega^\ast (M)\rightarrow \Omega^\ast (U)\oplus \Omega^\ast (V)\mathop{\rightrightarrows}\limits_{\partial_1^\ast }^{\partial_0^\ast }\Omega^\ast (U\cap V),$$ Each pullback above is equivalent to a restriction on the differential form. To get the exact series, We need to appropriately transform the mapping column: $$0\rightarrow\Omega^\ast (M)\xrightarrow{r}\Omega^\ast (U)\oplus\Omega^\ast (V)\xrightarrow{\delta}\Omega^\ast (U\cap V)\rightarrow 0,$$ Where $r(\omega)=(\omega|_U, \omega|_V)$, $\delta=\partial_0^\ast -\partial_1^\ast$, That is $\delta(\omega,\tau)=\tau|_{U\cap V}-\omega|_{U\cap V}$. Generally, If the definition domain is clear, it can also be abbreviated as $\tau-\omega$ for convenience. We need to verify that the above complex columns are exact. **Proof** $r$ is to limit the differential form to $U$ and $V$ respectively, so naturally it is an injective; From the special construction of $\delta$, it is easy to see that the middle term is also exact, and we only need to prove that $\delta$ is satisfied. $\,\forall\,\omega\in \Omega^q(U\cap V)$, Take the unit decomposition $\{\rho_U,\rho_V\}$ belonging to $\{U,V\}$, defined on $U$ $$\rho_V\omega= \begin{cases} \rho_V|_{U\cap V}\cdot\omega, \quad & \text{on}\quad U\cap V\\ 0, &\text{on} \quad U-U\cap V \end{cases},$$ Here the domain of $\rho_V$ is $V$, which naturally extends from zero to 0 on $U$. The domain of $\omega$ is $U\cap V$, Therefore, at this time, $\rho_V\omega$ can also be defined when expanded to $U$, and it is smooth. Similarly define $\rho_U\omega$ on $V$. Take $\xi=(-\rho_V\omega, \rho_U\omega)$, Then $\delta\xi=\omega$. Therefore, the complex sequence is exact. ### Mayer-Vietoris sequence According to the zig-zag lemma, the short exact sequence induces the long exact sequence $$\cdots\rightarrow H^q(M)\xrightarrow{r}H^q(U)\oplus H^q(V)\xrightarrow{\delta}H^q(U\cap V)\xrightarrow{d^\ast }H^{q+1}(M)\rightarrow \cdots$$ The definition of connection homomorphism $d^\ast$ here is given by the zig-zag lemma process: using graph tracking, $\omega\Rightarrow \xi=(-\rho_V\omega,\rho_U\omega)$, Then derive $r\eta=d\xi$. $r$ is the restriction mapping, so $\eta= \begin{cases} d(-\rho_V\omega) &\quad\text{on}\quad U\\ d(\rho_U\omega) &\quad\text{on}\quad V \end{cases}$. At $U\cap V$, from $\rho_U+\rho_V=1$, and $d\omega=0$, the definition is legal. So $d^\ast [\omega]=[\eta]$. Note that $\rho_U \omega, \rho_V\omega$ are not defined as a whole on $M$, There may not necessarily be $\eta=d\tau$, $\tau\in\Omega^\ast (M)$, Therefore $\eta$ is not necessarily appropriate. Compactly supported set MV sequence ---------- Let $f:N\rightarrow M$ be a smooth mapping, But $f^\ast :\Omega^\ast _c(M)\rightarrow \Omega^\ast _c(N)$ is generally not true, There is no guarantee that the differential form of the tight support will still be pulled back to the differential form of the tight support, For example, take $f$ as the projection $(x_1,x_2)\mapsto x_1$. However, a smooth inverse compact mapping is enough, Because its definition is a mapping that satisfies "the inverse image of a compact set is still a compact set". Generally, we consider more inclusion mapping: if there is $j: U\rightarrow M$, Definition $j_\ast : \Omega^\ast _c(U)\rightarrow \Omega^\ast _c(M)$, $j_\ast \omega$ is the zero extension of $\omega$** to $M-U$. Note that the mapped asterisk is marked below. 若只考虑开集间的包含映射, $\Omega^\ast _c$成为共变函子. 类似地, There is also a MV sequence for this functor, 由包含映射列$U\cap V\mathop \rightrightarrows \limits_{j_U}^{j_V} U\sqcup V\rightarrow M$诱导. 这里双箭头指分别映到$U,V$上. 由此构造出复形的短正合列: $$0\rightarrow \Omega^\ast _c(U\cap V)\xrightarrow{\delta} \Omega^\ast _c(U)\oplus \Omega^\ast _c(V)\xrightarrow{s} \Omega^\ast _c(M)\rightarrow 0,$$ Among them $\delta\omega=(-j_\ast \omega,j_\ast \omega)$, $s(\omega,\tau)=j_\ast \omega+j_\ast \tau$. In order to prove the legitimacy, we only need to prove that $s$ is full, Similarly, take $\{\rho_U,\rho_V\}$ as unit decomposition, Just construct the differential forms of $\rho_U\omega and \rho_V\omega$ on $U$ and $V$ respectively. Since $\omega$ has a compact support on $M$, the support of $\rho_U$ is in $U$, It can be proved that $\rho_U\omega$ has tight support on $U$, and the same is true for $\rho_V\omega$. Then the long exact sequence is derived from the zig-zag lemma: $$\cdots\rightarrow H^q_c(U\cap V)\xrightarrow{\delta} H^q_c(U)\oplus H^q_c(V)\xrightarrow{s} H^q_c(M) \xrightarrow{d_\ast }H^{q+1}_c(U\cap V)\rightarrow \cdots$$ In the same way, connection homomorphism can be judged. Graph tracking found $\eta=d(\rho_V\omega)|_{U\cap V}=-d(\rho_U\omega)|_{U\cap V}$, $d_\ast [\omega]=[\eta]$. 同样的在紧上同调中, $\eta$并不一定恰当. 因为无论是$\rho_U\omega$还是$\rho_V\omega$, 虽然这时有了整体定义, 但限制在$U\cap V$中不一定具有紧支集. $H^1(S^1)\simeq \mathbb{R}$ -------------------------- Let’s look at a simple example. Since $S^1$ is compact, $H_c^\ast (S^1)=H^\ast (S^1)$. At this time, this proposition can be easily proved by using cohomology and tightly cohomology MV sequences. Just cut $S^1$ in half. Or use local coordinates $\theta$, $d\theta$ to be in the $1$-form on $S^1$. $1$-When the form $\omega$ is appropriate, $\omega=dF=ad\theta \Leftrightarrow \int_{S^1}\omega=0.$ Therefore $B^1(S^1)=\ker \int_{S^1}.$ Note that $\int_{S^1}:\Omega^1(S^1)\rightarrow \mathbb{R}.$ Thus $H^1(S^1)=Z^1(S^1)/B^1(S^1)=\Omega^1(S^1)/\ker \int_{S^1}\cong \mathbb{R}$.
