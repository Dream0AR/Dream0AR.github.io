Invariants on vector bundles: tight vertical cohomology, given by integrating along the fiber. Compact cohomology of vector bundles (whole space) -------------------------- If $s:M\rightarrow E$ is zero cross-section, then it embeds $M$ into $E$. $s(M)$ is the deformation shrinkage of $E$, So $H^\ast (E)\cong H^\ast (M)$. Then inspired by Poincaré's lemma, for compact cohomology, Is there $H_c^\ast (E)\cong H_c^{\ast -n}(M)$? But this is not true for the Möbius band. Now we call manifolds with finite good coverage **finite manifolds**. **Lemma 1.1**. *$\pi:E\rightarrow M$ is a vector bundle of rank $n$. If $E, M$ are orientable finite manifolds, the formula holds.* The Poincaré duality is transformed into a cohomological duality space, and then it is obtained by homotopy invariance. If $M$ is a finite manifold, then so is $E$. In fact, if $M$ is orientable, $\pi$ is an orientable vector bundle, Then $E$ is also orientable. Just use the coordinate function $\psi_\alpha$ on $M$, And the directional trivialization $\phi_\alpha$ of $E$ can naturally construct the coordinate function on $E$. That is, the coordinate atlas of $E$ is $\{(E|_{U_\alpha},(\psi_\alpha\times \mathrm{id})\circ\phi_\alpha)\}$. It is easy to verify that it is directional, which is called local product directional. This leads to the following proposition: **Proposition 1.1**. *If $M$ is an orientable finite manifold, $\pi:E\rightarrow M$ is a rank $n$ orientable vector bundle, then $H_c^\ast (E)\cong H_c^{\ast -n}(M).$* In fact, following the Thom isomorphism proof and MV method described later, the orientability assumption of $M$ can be removed. Compact vertical cohomology and integration along fibers -------------------------- Let $\Omega^\ast _{cv}(E)$ be the differential form complex with compact support in the vertical direction, That is, $\omega\in\Omega_{cv}^\ast (E)$ if and only if for any compact set $K\subset M$, $\operatorname{supp}\omega\cap \pi^{-1}(K)$ tight. This differential form is said to have compact support in the vertical direction. $H_{cv}^\ast (E)$ is said to be compact vertical cohomology**. For such a form, the constraint has compact support on $E_x$, so it can be integrated along the fiber. Note the integral along the fiber $\pi_\ast :\Omega_{cv}^\ast (E)\rightarrow \Omega^{\ast -n}(M)$. Let $E$ be a trivial bundle. Decompose $\omega\in \Omega_{cv}^\ast (E)$ into linear combinations of terms containing $dt_1\wedge \cdots\wedge dt_n$ and other terms. Let $\pi_\ast :\pi^\ast \phi\wedge f(x,t)dt_1\wedge \cdots\wedge dt_n\mapsto \phi\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(x,t)dt_1\cdots dt_n$, Other forms are mapped to $0$. In fact, this is a high-dimensional generalization of the previous notation. For any directed vector bundle, just do $\pi_\ast \omega$ in each local trivial neighborhood. But what needs to be verified is to take two pieces of $U_\alpha, U_\beta$ and their coordinate functions and trivialization, It needs to be verified that $\pi_\ast \omega_\alpha, \pi_\ast \omega_\beta$ can be put together. In fact, this is the integral transformation formula of heavy integral. Of course, it can also be processed directly at the operational level, Use unit decomposition to divide $\omega$ into the sum of $\omega_\alpha$ supported on each $U_\alpha$, Then just integrate $\pi_\ast$ along the fiber. Next we have: **Proposition 1.2**. *$\pi_\ast$ is a chain map, thus inducing a mapping between tight vertical cohomologies.* Just verify the product bundle $E=M\times \mathbb{R}^n$, and use the definition to expand, It is almost the same as proving chain homotopy before. **Proposition 1.3** (projection formula). *$\,\forall\,\tau\in\Omega^\ast (M)$, $\omega\in \Omega_{cv}^\ast (E)$, then $\pi_\ast (\pi^\ast \tau\wedge \omega)=\tau\wedge \pi_\ast \omega$. If $M$ is an $m$-dimensional oriented manifold, let $\omega\in\Omega^{q}_{cv}(E)$, $\tau \in \Omega_c^{m+n-q}(M)$, $E$ take the local product orientation, then $\int_E\pi^\ast \tau\wedge\omega=\int_M\tau\wedge\pi_\ast \omega.$* Just do a simple verification, which is similar to the previous proof. Divide the form into two parts and verify them separately. The second half of the proposition can be decomposed into units of $M$. The proof requires the use of Fubini's theorem. That is, integrating on $E|_{U_\alpha}$ is the same as integrating along the fiber first and then integrating on $U_\alpha$. Thom isomorphism -------- Next, copy the proof of Poincaré’s lemma on compact supports to get: **Proposition 1.4** (Poincaré's lemma for compact vertical supports). *$\pi_\ast :H_{cv}^\ast (M\times \mathbb{R}^n)\cong H^{\ast -n}(M).$* Next we prove the general case: **Theorem 1.1** (Thom isomorphism). *If $\pi:E\rightarrow M$ is a rank $n$ orientable vector bundle on finite manifold $M$, then $H_{cv}^\ast (E)\cong H^{\ast -n}(M).$* The proof uses the MV sequence on $U\cup V$ and the MV sequence of the fiber $E|_{U\cup V}$ on it. They are connected by chain mapping $\pi_\ast$. According to the five lemmas, Thom isomorphism holds for $E|_U,E|_V,E|_{U\cap V}$, then it also holds for $E|_{U\cup V}$. Thus, the number of covered open sets can be summarized, which can be obtained by the MV method. Let $\mathcal{T}:H^\ast (M)\rightarrow H_{cv}^{\ast +n}(E)$ be the inverse of $\pi_\ast$, Call it Thom isomorphism. For $1\in H^0(M)$, $\Phi=\mathcal{T}(1)\in H_{cv}^n(E)$ is called the Thom class of $E$. $\mathcal{T}(\omega)=\mathcal{T}\pi_\ast (\pi^\ast \omega\wedge \Phi)=\pi^\ast \omega\wedge \Phi.$ Because $\mathcal{T}\pi_\ast =\mathrm{id}$, $\pi_\ast \mathcal{T}(1)=1.$ **Proposition 1.5**. *$\Phi$ is the only cohomology class of $H_{cv}^n(E)$, restricted to $H_c^n(E_x)$ generators on every $E_x$.* **Certificate:** $\pi_\ast \Phi=1$, $\pi_\ast \Phi(x)=\int_{E_x}\Phi(x,t)dt=1$, Thus $\Phi|_{E_x}\in H_c^n(E_x)$ is a generator. If $\Phi'$ also satisfies this property, then $\pi_\ast \Phi'=1$. $\pi_\ast (\pi^\ast \omega\wedge\Phi')=\omega$, $\mathcal{T}(\omega)=\pi^\ast \omega\wedge\Phi'$, Thus $\Phi'=\mathcal{T}(1)=\Phi.$ **Proposition 1.6**. *Suppose $\pi_1:E\rightarrow M$, $\pi_2:E'\rightarrow M$ are two directed vector bundles, Then $\Phi(E\oplus E')=\operatorname{pr}_1^\ast \Phi(E)\wedge \operatorname{pr}_2^\ast \Phi(E')$.* **Proof:** Take the right expression as $\Phi'$, and integrate it on $E_x\oplus E'_x$ to find $1$, It is obtained from the uniqueness of the previous proposition. In fact, $\Phi$ also has functor properties, that is, $f:N\rightarrow M$ is a smooth mapping, Then $\Phi(f^{-1}E)=f^\ast \Phi(E)$, the proof is left as an exercise.
