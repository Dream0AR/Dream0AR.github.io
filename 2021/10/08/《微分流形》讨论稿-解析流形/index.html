Analytic manifold -------- ### Differential Calculus Recall that the $C^\omega$ structure $[\mathcal{F}]$ means that all the coordinate cards $(U,\varphi)$ in the coordinate atlas $\mathcal{F}$ are $C^\omega$ compatible with each other, That is, each coordinate transformation $\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}\in C^\omega,$ is analytical. **Definition 1.1**. *$m$-dimensional $C^\omega$ manifold = $m$-dimensional topological manifold + $C^\omega$ structure\[$\mathcal{F}$\].* Call the $C^\omega$ manifold **analytical manifold**. Differential calculus that can be studied on smooth manifolds can also be studied on analytic manifolds. In addition, The analyticity of functions on analytic manifolds can be studied, which is guaranteed by the fact that the composition of analytic functions is still an analytic function. Just take a suitable kickoff and let the value domain of the inner function fall on the convergence domain of the outer function. ### Example #### Unanalytical smooth manifold Remember $f(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)e^{-\frac{1}{x^2} }\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}),$ Let its image be a $1$-dimensional topological manifold $M.$ Take $[\{(M,\operatorname{pr}_x),(M,\operatorname{pr}_y)\}]$ as the coordinate atlas, Then this album is from $C^\infty$ instead of $C^\omega$. Because on $\mathbb{R}$, $\operatorname{pr}_y\circ \operatorname{pr}_x^{-1}$ is $f,$ is a smooth and non-analytic function. Therefore, the transfer function is smooth and non-analytic, and the atlas is only $C^\infty$, $M$ becomes a smooth manifold under this atlas, rather than an analytic manifold. Note that in fact $M\approx \mathbb{R},$ overall Euclidean does not need a transfer function at all, Therefore, it can be any $\ast $-atlas, $\ast $ is the requirement for the transfer function. For example, project $M$ onto the $x$ axis or $y$ axis. But now when the two albums are put together, they can only be $C^\infty$. Because these two albums are not analytically compatible. Therefore $M$ is the $C^\infty$ manifold under this album, Of course it can be other differential manifolds in other atlases, but the differential structure we give it is this.
