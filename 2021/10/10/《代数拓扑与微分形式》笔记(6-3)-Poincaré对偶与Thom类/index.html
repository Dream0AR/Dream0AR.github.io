This section explains how to relate the Poincaré duality of a submanifold to the Thom class of vector bundles on the submanifold. Shang Cong ---- Say $F$ is a rank$k$**subbundle** of a rank$n$ vector bundle$E$, if $\,\forall\,x\in M$, $F_x$ is $E_x$ subspace. From this we can define the **quotient cluster**$Q=E/F$, It is a vector bundle such that $0\rightarrow F\rightarrow E\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0$ is exact. That is, $\,\forall\,x\in M$, $Q_x=E_x/F_x$ is the quotient space. First, take the natural frame in $F$, and then expand it to a set of frames in $E$, In this way, their local trivializations are compatible. Let the local trivialization transfer function of $E$ be $g_{\alpha\beta},$ $F$ is $h_{\alpha\beta}.$, then for the point on $F$, only $h_{\alpha\beta}$ works, Thus $g_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix} h_{\alpha\beta} & *\\ 0 & q_{\alpha\beta} \end{pmatrix}$. Then $q_{\alpha\beta}$ is the transfer function of $Q$. It is easy to see that if $E, F$ can be oriented, $Q$ can also be oriented, it is said that the orientation of $Q$ makes the orientation of $E$ be straight and oriented. Because we have an inner product, there is an orthogonal complementary space $F_x^\perp\subset E_x$ on the fiber. Then $F^\perp=\bigsqcup F_x^\perp$ is a subbundle of $E$ and $E=F\oplus F^\perp$, There is also an inner product inherited from $E$. Obviously $Q\cong F^\perp$, Therefore, there is also an induced inner product on $Q/E$. There is also an orientation on $F^\perp$ such that the orientation of $E$ is straight and directed. Submanifold normal bundle and tubular neighborhood lemma -------------------------- $k$-dimensional submanifold $S\subset M$, there are tangent bundles $TS$ and $i^{-1}TM=TM|_S$ on $S$, $i$ is an inclusion mapping. Obviously $TS\subset TM|_S$. Define $N_{S/M}=TM|_S/TS$, abbreviated as $N$. If $M, S$ can be oriented, Then the tangent bundle can be oriented, so $N$ can be oriented. $\,\forall\,x\in S$, there is $x$ point coordinate card $(U,\phi)$, Let $\phi|_{S\cap U}$ be the coordinate card of $x$ in $S$, and only the first $k$ coordinates have values. This induces a frame on the tangent bundle. Restricted to $U_\alpha$ or $U_\alpha\cap S$, Let $TM$ frame be $\{\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x_A^\alpha}\}_{1\le A\le n}$, The $TS$ frame is $\{\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x_i^\alpha}\}_{1\le i\le k}$, The $N$ frame is $\{\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x_a^\alpha}\}_{k+1\le a\le n}$. On $S\cap U_{\alpha\beta}$, $\frac{\partial {}x_a^\alpha}{\partial {}x_i^\beta}=0$, Therefore, the $TS$ transfer function is $h_{\alpha\beta}=(\frac{\partial {}x_j^\alpha}{\partial {}x_i^\beta})_{k\times k}$. However, $\frac{\partial {}x_j^\alpha}{\partial {}x_a^\beta}$ is obviously not necessarily $0$, But we modeled out this part when we were doing the business cluster. Therefore, the transfer function of $N$ is $q_{\alpha\beta}=(\frac{\partial {}x_b^\alpha}{\partial {}x_a^\beta})_{(n-k)\times (n-k)}$. The transfer function of $TM|_S$ is $g_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix} h_{\alpha\beta} & *\\ 0 & q_{\alpha\beta} \end{pmatrix}=(\frac{\partial {}x_A^\alpha}{\partial {}x_B^\beta})_{n\times n}$. We have the following important theorem. For proof, please refer to other differential manifold textbooks. **Theorem 1.1** (Tubular Neighborhood Theorem). *There is $j$ that embeds $N$ into $M$, and $j\circ s=i$, that is, the zero section is mapped to $S$.* The open set $j(N)\subset M$ is called a tubular neighborhood, denoted as $T$. Poincaré duality and Thom class ------------------- Let $\Phi$ be the Thom class of the $S$ method bundle $N$, then it is isomorphic to Thom, $H^\ast (S)\cong H_{cv}^{\ast +n-k}(N)\cong H_{cv}^{\ast +n-k}(T)\rightarrow H^{\ast +n-k}(M)$, The last mapping is zero continuation. **Lemma 1.1**. *Closed Poincaré duality of $S$$[\eta_S]=j_\ast \Phi\in H^{n-k}(M)$,* **Proof:** We only need to prove that $j_\ast \Phi$ satisfies the duality property. Let $l:S\rightarrow T$ be included, It is $\pi:T\rightarrow S$ with zero cross section. They are mutually inverse at the level of cohomology, Thus $\omega|_T=\pi^\ast l^\ast (\omega|_T)+d\tau$, $\tau\in \Omega^{k-1}(T)$ $$\begin{aligned} \int_M\omega\wedge j_*\Phi&=\int_T\omega|_T\wedge \Phi & j_*\text{为零延拓}\\ &=\int_T \pi^*l^*(\omega|_T)\wedge \Phi+\int_T d\tau \wedge \Phi&\\ &=\int_S l^*(\omega|_T)\wedge \pi_*\Phi&\text{Stokes定理+投射公式}\\ &=\int_S i^*\omega.&\pi_*\Phi=1\quad+\quad{\omega|_T}|_S=\omega|_S \end{aligned}$$ And because the tubular neighborhood of submanifold $S$ in $M$ can be sufficiently small, we get: **Proposition 1.1** (Localization principle). *The support of the Poincaré dual of a closed submanifold $S$ can be reduced to any given tubular neighborhood of $S$.* Application ---- ### Poincaré duality of transversal intersecting submanifolds Two submanifolds $R, S$ in $M$ are said to intersect transversely, if $\,\forall\,x\in R\cap S$, $T_xR+T_xS=T_xM$. Let $R, S$ be the closed submanifolds that intersect transversely in the manifold $M$, Then $R\cap S$ is also a closed submanifold and $\dim R\cap S=\dim R+\dim S-\dim M$. Further$\operatorname{codim}_M R\cap S=\operatorname{codim}_M R+\operatorname{codim}_M S$. $R\cap S$ is in $M$ with the French bundle $N_{(R\cap S)/M}=(N_{R/M}\oplus N_{S/M}) |_{R\cap S}$. Embedding the normal bundle into $M$ is a tubular neighborhood, Then $\Phi(N_{(R\cap S)/M})=\Phi(N_{R/M})\wedge \Phi(N_{S/M})$, Thus $\eta_{R\cap S}=\eta_R\wedge \eta_S$. **Proposition 1.2**. *The Poincaré duality of the intersection of two transversely intersecting closed submanifolds is equal to the outer product of the Poincaré duality of the two submanifolds.* ### Pullback of Poincaré duality Let $f:M'\rightarrow M$ be a smooth map, and $S$ be the closed submanifold of $M$. If $\,\forall\,x\in f^{-1}(S)$, $f_\ast (T_x M')+T_{f(x)}S=T_{f(x)}M$, Then the map $f$ is said to be transverse to the submanifold $S$. That is, $f(M')$ is either disjoint with the submanifold $S$ ($f^{-1}(S)=\varnothing$ is automatically established), Either crosswise intersection ($f_\ast (T_x M')=T_{f(x)}f(M')$). At this time, $f^{-1}(S)$ is a smooth submanifold of $M'$, and any tubular neighborhood $T$ of $S$ in $M$, $f^{-1}T$ is the tubular neighborhood of $f^{-1}(S)$ in $M'$. According to the functor property of Thom class, $f^\ast $ can be replaced with $\mathcal{T}$, $j_\ast $. At this time we have $f^\ast \eta_S=f^\ast j_\ast \Phi(T)=j_\ast \mathcal{T}f^\ast (1)=j_\ast \Phi(f^{-1}T)=\eta_{f^{-1}(S)}$. That is: **Proposition 1.3**. *Suppose $f:M'\rightarrow M$ is a smooth map, a closed submanifold $S$ transverse to $M$. If $[\eta_S]$ is the Poincaré dual of $S$ in $M$, then $f^\ast [\eta_S]$ is the Poincaré dual of $f^{-1}(S)$ in $M'$.* The article was last updated on 2021-10-10 21:10:53
