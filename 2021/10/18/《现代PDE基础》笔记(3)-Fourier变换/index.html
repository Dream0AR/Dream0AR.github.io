常义Fourier变换

基本性质

$\,\forall\,f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_x^n),$ 定义其Fourier变换为 $$F[f]=\int_{\mathbb{R}_x^n}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot \xi} dx;$$ 对$g(\xi)\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_\xi^n),$ 定义其Fourier逆变换为 $$F^{-1}g=\int_{\mathbb{R}_\xi^n}g(\xi)e^{ix\cdot \xi}\bar{d}\xi.$$ 其中$\bar{d}\xi=(2\pi)^{-n}d\xi.$ 也记$F[f]=\widehat{f}.$

$F$满足如下性质:

  1. $F[f(x+h)]=\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ih\cdot\xi};$

  2. $F[f(x)e^{-ix\cdot h}]=\widehat{f}(\xi+h);$

  3. $F[f(\delta x)]=\delta^{-n}\widehat{f}(\delta^{-1}\xi);$

  4. $F[D_jf(x)]=\xi_j\widehat{f}(\xi);$

  5. $F[x_jf(x)]=-D_j\widehat{f}(\xi).$

其中$D_j=\frac{1}{i}\partial x_j.$ 前三个命题是容易的, 第四个命题即是分部积分, 最后一个命题难点在于说明$\widehat{f}$是可微的. 对$\widehat{f}$做微商, 注意左式已经给出了我们期望的极限, 因此证明它们的差趋于零即可. 用欧拉公式与微分中值定理即可给出简单的证明. 这一性质告诉我们, $\widehat{f}\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}_\xi^n).$ 进一步有:

定理 1.1. $f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ $\Rightarrow$ $\widehat f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n).$

证: 注意到$\widehat{f}=F[f]$是有界的. 那么由前面的性质, $(-1)^{|\beta|}\xi^\alpha D^\beta\widehat{f}(\xi)=F[D^\beta(x^\alpha f(x))]$也是有界的. 因此$\widehat f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n).$

接下来我们说明$F^{-1}$确实是$F$的逆. 首先由Fubini定理, 有如下命题:

命题 1.2. $f,g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$时, 有 $$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f\widehat gdx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat fgdx.$$

回忆概率论中均值为零, 标准差为$\sigma I$的正态分布密度函数为$K_\sigma(x)={(\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma)^{-n} }e^{-\frac{|x|^2}{2\sigma^2} }.$ 记$G_\sigma(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}e^{-\frac{\sigma^2 |x|^2}{2} },$ 那么计算有$F[G_\sigma]=K_\sigma.$ 我们利用这一点证明下面的定理:

定理 1.3 (Fourier逆变换). $f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ $f=F^{-1}\circ F[f].$

证: 由前面的命题, $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f K_\sigma dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{f} G_\sigma d\xi.$ 当$\sigma\rightarrow 0$时, 左侧收敛到$f(0)$(即$K_\sigma\rightarrow \delta$), 右侧收敛到$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \widehat{f}\bar{d}\xi$(即$G_\sigma\rightarrow (2\pi)^{-n}$). 因此有$f(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \widehat{f}\bar{d}\xi=F^{-1}\widehat{f}(0).$

接下来$\,\forall\,x\in \mathbb{R},$ 取函数$g(y)=f(y+x),$ 那么 $$f(x)=g(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{g}(\xi)\bar{d}\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot \xi}\bar{d}\xi=F^{-1}\widehat{f}(x).$$ 因此$f=F^{-1}\circ F[f].$

根据定义, $F[f] (\xi)=(2\pi)^nF^{-1}[f] (-\xi),$ $F^{\pm 1}[f(x)] (\xi)=F^{\pm 1}[f(-x)] (-\xi),$ 因此 $$F[F^{-1}[f] (\xi)] (x)=(2\pi)^{-n}F[F[f] (-\xi)] (x)=F^{-1}[F[f] (-\xi)] (-x)=F^{-1}[F[f] (\xi)] (x)=f(x),$$ 即在$\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$上, $F^{-1}\circ F=F^{-1}\circ F=\mathrm{id},$ 故线性连续映照$F$建立了一个同构对应, 逆映射也是连续的.

卷积

卷积运算在Fourier变换下也有很好的性质.

命题 1.4. $\,\forall\,f,g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ $f\ast g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ 且$F[f\ast g]=F[f]F[g].$

证: $\,\forall\,l\ge 0,$ $\,\exists\,M_l>0$使得$\sup_x |x|^l|g(x)|\le M_l.$ 因此有: $$|x||g(x-y)|^{1/l}\le M_l^{1/l}\frac{|x|}{|x-y|}\le 2M_l^{1/l},\quad |x|\ge 2|y|;$$ $$\frac{|x|}{|y|}|g(x-y)|^{1/l}\le 2|g(x-y)|\le 2M_0^{1/l},\quad |x|<2|y|.$$

记$A_l=2^l(M_0+M_l),$ 那么$|x|^l|g(x-y)|\le A_l(1+|y|)^l.$ 从而 $$\sup_x|x|^l|(f\ast g)(x)|\le A_l\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(y)(1+|y|)^ldy<\infty.$$

$\,\forall\,\alpha\in \mathbb{N}^n,$ 由于$\partial^\alpha(f\ast g)=f\ast (\partial^\alpha g),$ $\partial^\alpha g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ 因此$\sup_x |x|^l |\partial^\alpha (f\ast g)(x)|<\infty,$ $f\ast g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n).$

记$F(x,y)=f(y)g(x-y)e^{-ix\cdot \xi}.$ $|F|$显然是可积的, 因此由Fubini定理, $$\widehat{f\ast g}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n\times \mathbb{R}^n}F(x,y)dydx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n\times \mathbb{R}^n}F(x,y)d(x-y)dy=\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi).$$ 即$F[f\ast g]=F[f]F[g].$

利用$F$与$F^{-1}$的转化关系, 立即可得:

推论 1.5. $F[fg]=(2\pi)^{-n}F[f]\ast F[g]$

对Fourier级数的Parseval恒等式, 我们有类似的Fourier变换版本:

定理 1.6 (Plancherel). $f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)\Rightarrow (2\pi)^{-n}\parallel\widehat{f}\parallel_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}=\parallel f\parallel_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}$

证: 取$f^\ast (x)=\overline{f(-x)},$ 那么 $$Ff^\ast (\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\overline{f(-x)}e^{-ix\cdot \xi}dx=\overline{\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(-x)e^{ix\cdot \xi}dx}=\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi)}.$$

取$h=f\ast f^\ast ,$ 那么一方面$h(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(y)\overline{f(y)}dy=\parallel f\parallel^2_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}.$ 另一方面, 由$\widehat{h}=\widehat{f}\overline{\widehat{f} }=|\widehat{f}|^2,$ $h(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{h}(\xi)\bar{d}\xi=(2\pi)^{-n}\parallel\widehat{f}\parallel^2_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}.$ 由此即得$(2\pi)^{-n}\parallel\widehat{f}\parallel_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}=\parallel f\parallel_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}.$

当然可以先证明更一般地有$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{f}\overline{\widehat{g} }\bar{d}\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f\overline{g}d x,$ 取$h=f\ast g^\ast $即可.

广义Fourier变换

基本定义

我们可以在$\mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$上定义广义Fourier变换$F[T]\in \mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n):$ $$\left<{}F[T],\varphi\right>:=\left<{}T,F[\varphi]\right>$$

当$T\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$或仅为存在Fourier变换的常义可积函数时, 由前面的命题(用Fubini定理证明), 等式在常义Fourier变换意义下成立, 因此定义是常义的推广.

类似地, 广义Fourier变换也满足如下性质: $F[D^\alpha T]=\xi^\alpha F[T],$ $F[x^\alpha T]=(-1)^{|\alpha|}D^\alpha F[T].$

定义广义Fourier逆变换为: $$\left<{}F^{-1}[T],\varphi\right>:=\left<{}T,F^{-1}[\varphi]\right>.$$

由常义Fourier变换$F:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)\cong \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ 广义Fourier变换也是广义函数空间$\mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$上的连续同构对应, 逆映射$F^{-1}$也是线性连续映照.

回忆我们定义了广义函数的卷积, 但对一般的广义函数, 卷积不见得存在. 对$\varphi\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ $T\in \mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n),$ 我们有$\varphi\ast T\in \mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n),$ 且$F[\varphi\ast T]=F[\varphi]F[T].$ $\varphi$也可替换为具紧支集广义函数$\mathscr{E}'(\mathbb{R}^n).$

由于对广义函数, $F,F^{-1}$也有如下的转化关系: $$\left<{}F[T],\varphi(x)\right>=(2\pi)^n\left<{}T,F^{-1}[\varphi(-x)]\right>=(2\pi)^n\left<{}F^{-1}[T],\varphi(-x)\right>,$$ 我们有$F[\varphi\cdot T]=(2\pi)^{-n}F[\varphi]\ast F[T].$

例子

$F[\delta(x-a)]=e^{-ia\cdot \xi},$ 特别地, $F[\delta]=1.$

具紧支集的广义函数当然在$\mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$中. 特别地, 其满足如下定理:

定理 1.7. 若$T\in \mathscr{E}'(\mathbb{R}_x^n),$ 则$F[T] (\xi)=\left<{}T_x,e^{-ix\cdot \xi}\right>\in \mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}_\xi^n)$为关于$\xi$的常义函数.

证明用广义函数的正则化即可给出.

Common sense Fourier transformation

basic properties

$\,\forall\,f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_x^n),$ Define its Fourier transform for $$F[f]=\int_{\mathbb{R}_x^n}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot \xi} dx;$$ Define $g(\xi)\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_\xi^n),$ for Inverse Fourier transform for $$F^{-1}g=\int_{\mathbb{R}_\xi^n}g(\xi)e^{ix\cdot \xi}\bar{d}\xi.$$ Among them $\bar{d}\xi=(2\pi)^{-n}d\xi.$ is also recorded as $F[f]=\widehat{f}.$

$F$ satisfies the following properties:

  1. $F[f(x+h)]=\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ih\cdot\xi};$

  2. $F[f(x)e^{-ix\cdot h}]=\widehat{f}(\xi+h);$

  3. $F[f(\delta x)]=\delta^{-n}\widehat{f}(\delta^{-1}\xi);$

  4. $F[D_jf(x)]=\xi_j\widehat{f}(\xi);$

  5. $F[x_jf(x)]=-D_j\widehat{f}(\xi).$

Among them $D_j=\frac{1}{i}\partial x_j.$ the first three propositions are easy, The fourth proposition is the integral by parts. The difficulty of the last proposition is to show that $\widehat{f}$ is differentiable. Do derivatives of $\widehat{f}$. Note that the left equation has given us the limit of our expectations, Therefore, it is enough to prove that their difference tends to zero. A simple proof can be given using Euler's formula and the differential mean value theorem. This property tells us that $\widehat{f}\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}_\xi^n).$ further has:

Theorem 1.1 . $f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ $\Rightarrow$ $\widehat f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n).$

Certificate: Note that $\widehat{f}=F[f]$ is bounded. Then from the previous properties, $(-1)^{|\beta|}\xi^\alpha D^\beta\widehat{f}(\xi)=F[D^\beta(x^\alpha f(x))]$ is also bounded. Therefore $\widehat f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n).$

Next, we show that $F^{-1}$ is indeed the inverse of $F$. First, based on Fubini's theorem, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 1.2 . When $f,g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, there is $$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f\widehat gdx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat fgdx.$$

Recall that in probability theory the mean is zero, The normal distribution density function with standard deviation $\sigma I$ is $K_\sigma(x)={(\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma)^{-n} }e^{-\frac{|x|^2}{2\sigma^2} }.$ Note$G_\sigma(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}e^{-\frac{\sigma^2 |x|^2}{2} },$ Then the calculation is $F[G_\sigma]=K_\sigma.$. We use this to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.3 (Fourier inverse transform). $f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ $f=F^{-1}\circ F[f].$

Certificate: From the previous proposition, $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f K_\sigma dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{f} G_\sigma d\xi.$ When $\sigma\rightarrow 0$, The left side converges to $f(0)$ (i.e. $K_\sigma\rightarrow \delta$), The right side converges to $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \widehat{f}\bar{d}\xi$ (i.e. $G_\sigma\rightarrow (2\pi)^{-n}$). Therefore there is $f(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \widehat{f}\bar{d}\xi=F^{-1}\widehat{f}(0).$

Next $\,\forall\,x\in \mathbb{R},$ takes the function $g(y)=f(y+x),$ then $$f(x)=g(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{g}(\xi)\bar{d}\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot \xi}\bar{d}\xi=F^{-1}\widehat{f}(x).$$ Therefore $f=F^{-1}\circ F[f].$

By definition, $F[f] (\xi)=(2\pi)^nF^{-1}[f] (-\xi),$ $F^{\pm 1}[f(x)] (\xi)=F^{\pm 1}[f(-x)] (-\xi),$ Therefore $$F[F^{-1}[f] (\xi)] (x)=(2\pi)^{-n}F[F[f] (-\xi)] (x)=F^{-1}[F[f] (-\xi)] (-x)=F^{-1}[F[f] (\xi)] (x)=f(x),$$ That is, on $\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, $F^{-1}\circ F=F^{-1}\circ F=\mathrm{id},$ Therefore, linear continuous mapping $F$ establishes an isomorphic correspondence, and the inverse mapping is also continuous.

Convolution

The convolution operation also has good properties under Fourier transform.

Proposition 1.4 . $\,\forall\,f,g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ $f\ast g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ and $F[f\ast g]=F[f]F[g].$

Certificate: $\,\forall\,l\ge 0,$ $\,\exists\,M_l>0$ makes $\sup_x |x|^l|g(x)|\le M_l.$ so we have: $$|x||g(x-y)|^{1/l}\le M_l^{1/l}\frac{|x|}{|x-y|}\le 2M_l^{1/l},\quad |x|\ge 2|y|;$$ $$\frac{|x|}{|y|}|g(x-y)|^{1/l}\le 2|g(x-y)|\le 2M_0^{1/l},\quad |x|<2|y|.$$

Note $A_l=2^l(M_0+M_l),$ then $|x|^l|g(x-y)|\le A_l(1+|y|)^l.$ thus $$\sup_x|x|^l|(f\ast g)(x)|\le A_l\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(y)(1+|y|)^ldy<\infty.$$

$\,\forall\,\alpha\in \mathbb{N}^n,$ Due to $\partial^\alpha(f\ast g)=f\ast (\partial^\alpha g),$ $\partial^\alpha g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ Therefore $\sup_x |x|^l |\partial^\alpha (f\ast g)(x)|<\infty,$ $f\ast g\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n).$

Note that $F(x,y)=f(y)g(x-y)e^{-ix\cdot \xi}.$ $|F|$ is obviously integrable, Therefore, according to Fubini’s theorem, $$\widehat{f\ast g}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n\times \mathbb{R}^n}F(x,y)dydx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n\times \mathbb{R}^n}F(x,y)d(x-y)dy=\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi).$$ That is $F[f\ast g]=F[f]F[g].$

Using the conversion relationship between $F$ and $F^{-1}$, we can immediately get:

Corollary 1.5 . $F[fg]=(2\pi)^{-n}F[f]\ast F[g]$

For the Parseval identity of the Fourier series, we have a similar Fourier transform version:

Theorem 1.6 (Plancherel). $f\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)\Rightarrow (2\pi)^{-n}\parallel\widehat{f}\parallel_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}=\parallel f\parallel_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}$

Certificate: Take $f^\ast (x)=\overline{f(-x)},$ then $$Ff^\ast (\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\overline{f(-x)}e^{-ix\cdot \xi}dx=\overline{\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(-x)e^{ix\cdot \xi}dx}=\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi)}.$$

Take $h=f\ast f^\ast ,$ So on the one hand $h(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(y)\overline{f(y)}dy=\parallel f\parallel^2_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}.$ On the other hand, by$\widehat{h}=\widehat{f}\overline{\widehat{f} }=|\widehat{f}|^2,$ $h(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{h}(\xi)\bar{d}\xi=(2\pi)^{-n}\parallel\widehat{f}\parallel^2_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}.$ From this we get $(2\pi)^{-n}\parallel\widehat{f}\parallel_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}=\parallel f\parallel_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}.$

Of course, we can first prove that $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\widehat{f}\overline{\widehat{g} }\bar{d}\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f\overline{g}d x,$ is more generally Just take $h=f\ast g^\ast $.

Generalized Fourier Transform

basic definition

We can define the generalized Fourier transform $F[T]\in \mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n):$ on $\mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$ $$\left<{}F[T],\varphi\right>:=\left<{}T,F[\varphi]\right>$$

When $T\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ or is just a normal integrable function with Fourier transformation, From the previous proposition (proven by Fubini’s theorem), the equation holds in the sense of the normal Fourier transformation, Therefore the definition is a generalization of the common sense.

Similarly, the generalized Fourier transform also satisfies the following properties: $F[D^\alpha T]=\xi^\alpha F[T],$ $F[x^\alpha T]=(-1)^{|\alpha|}D^\alpha F[T].$

Define the generalized Fourier inverse transform as: $$\left<{}F^{-1}[T],\varphi\right>:=\left<{}T,F^{-1}[\varphi]\right>.$$

By the normal Fourier transform $F:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)\cong \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ The generalized Fourier transform is also a continuous isomorphic correspondence on the generalized function space $\mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$, The inverse mapping $F^{-1}$ is also a linear continuous mapping.

Recall that we defined convolution of generalized functions, but for general generalized functions, convolution may not exist. Right $\varphi\in \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^n),$ $T\in \mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n),$ We have $\varphi\ast T\in \mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n),$ And $F[\varphi\ast T]=F[\varphi]F[T].$ $\varphi$ can also be replaced by a generalized function with compact support $\mathscr{E}'(\mathbb{R}^n).$

For generalized functions, $F,F^{-1}$ also has the following transformation relationship: $$\left<{}F[T],\varphi(x)\right>=(2\pi)^n\left<{}T,F^{-1}[\varphi(-x)]\right>=(2\pi)^n\left<{}F^{-1}[T],\varphi(-x)\right>,$$ We have $F[\varphi\cdot T]=(2\pi)^{-n}F[\varphi]\ast F[T].$

Example

$F[\delta(x-a)]=e^{-ia\cdot \xi},$ In particular, $F[\delta]=1.$

The generalized function with compact support is of course in $\mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$. In particular, It satisfies the following theorem:

Theorem 1.7 . If $T\in \mathscr{E}'(\mathbb{R}_x^n),$, then $F[T] (\xi)=\left<{}T_x,e^{-ix\cdot \xi}\right>\in \mathscr{S}'(\mathbb{R}_\xi^n)$ is a normal function about $\xi$.

The proof can be given using regularization of generalized functions.
