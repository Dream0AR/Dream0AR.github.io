《现代PDE基础》笔记(4)-Sobolev空间 "Basics of Modern PDE" Notes (4)-Sobolev Space

basic definition -------- By default $\Omega\subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is the bounded area or outer area (the complement of the bounded area closure), The boundary is $C^\infty$ submanifold or $C^m$ submanifold. The smoothness requirement of the boundary is determined by the implicit function theorem, Equivalent to requiring $\,\forall\,x\in \partial \Omega,$ $\,\exists\,$neighborhood $U\ni x,$ $U\cap \partial\Omega$ can be represented by $x_j=\varphi(\widehat{x}_j)\in C^\infty/C^m$, $1\le j\le n.$ For $m\in \mathbb{N},$ $1\le p\le \infty,$ Define the Sobolev space $H^{m,p}(\Omega)$ to satisfy the condition $D^\alpha u\in L^p(\Omega),$ $\quad |\alpha|\le m$ generalized function $u$ ensemble. Equipped with norm $$\parallel u\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\le m}\parallel D^\alpha u\parallel_{L^p(\Omega)}^p\right)^{\frac{1}{p} },\quad 1\le p<\infty,$$ $$\parallel u\parallel_{H^{m,\infty}(\Omega)}=\max_{|\alpha|\le m}\parallel D^\alpha u\parallel_{L^\infty(\Omega)}.$$ Since $L^p(\Omega)$ is a Banach space, it is easy to prove that $H^{m,p}(\Omega)$ is also a Banach space. Recall that $C^\infty$ is dense in $L^p$, Through a similar method, it can be proved that $C^\infty(\overline{\Omega})$ is dense in $H^{m,p}(\Omega)$ (of course $C^m(\overline{\Omega})$ is also dense in $H^{m,p}(\Omega)$). Therefore $H^{m,p}(\Omega)$ can be regarded as the complete space of $C^\infty(\overline{\Omega})$ equipped with $\parallel\cdot\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)}$ module. Equivalent modular theorem ---------- For $f\in H^{m,p}(\Omega),$ define the model based on $H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ $$\parallel f\parallel_{\widetilde{H}^{m,p}(\Omega)}=\inf_{\widetilde{f}|_{\Omega}=f}\parallel\widetilde{f}\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)},$$ $\widetilde{f}$ is the continuation of $f$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Obviously for any continuation $\widetilde{f},$ $\parallel f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)}\le \parallel\widetilde{f}\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)},$ Therefore $\parallel f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)}\le \parallel f\parallel_{\widetilde{H}^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}.$ Next we prove that we can find constants that make the converse inequality true, so we have the following theorem: **Theorem 1.1**. *$H^{m,p},\widetilde{H}^{m,p}$ are equivalent modules.* **Proof:** Just construct a continuation $\widetilde{f},$ Such that $\parallel\widetilde{f}\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}\le C\parallel f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)},$ $C=C(m,p,\Omega)$ has nothing to do with $f$. In this way, we have $$\parallel f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)}\le\parallel f\parallel_{\widetilde{H}^{m,p}(\Omega)}\le \parallel\widetilde{f}\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}\le C\parallel f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)},$$ The theorem is proved. Since $C^m(\overline\Omega)$ is dense in $H^{m,p}(\Omega)$, Let us first discuss the case of $f\in C^m(\overline{\Omega})$. We use the Lions continuation method. $\,\forall\,x\in \overline{\Omega},$ If $x\in \Omega,$ take $O_\delta(x)\subset \Omega;$ if $x\in \partial\Omega,$ Take $O_\delta(x)$ so that $O_\delta(x)\cap \partial\Omega$ can be represented by the $C^m$ function. Then $\{O_\delta(x)\}$ constitutes the open coverage of $\overline{\Omega}$, There is a finite subcover $\{O_{\delta_i}(x_i)\}.$ Take the unit decomposition that belongs to $\{\varphi_i\}.$ Then since $f=\sum_i \varphi_if,$ Just do an extension $F_i.$ for each $\varphi_i f$. Since $\varphi_i$ is only related to $\Omega$, $\,\exists\,C>0$ such that $$\parallel\varphi_i f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)}\le C\parallel f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)}.$$ Just let the continuation $F_i$ satisfy $$\parallel F_i\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}\le C_i\parallel\varphi_i f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)},$$ In this way, just take $\widetilde{f}=\sum_i F_i$. If $x_i\in \Omega,$ then take $F_i$ as the zero continuation of $\varphi_i f$; If $x_i\in \partial \Omega,$ Flatten $\partial \Omega\cap O_{\delta_i}(x_i)$, Let $\Omega\cap O_{\delta_i}(x_i)$ be contained in the upper half-plane $\mathbb{H}$, During the process, $H^{m,p}$ remains unchanged. When determining $O_{\delta}(x)$, additional conditions may be required: After $\Omega^c\cap O_{\delta}(x)$ is flattened, the lower half plane contains the mirror symmetry of $\Omega\cap O_{\delta}(x)$. Otherwise, the part of $O_{\delta}(x)$ in $\Omega$ can be reduced, Although $O_{\delta}(x)$ is no longer a spherical neighborhood. Still use the same symbols to record the flattened elements. Let’s assume that the flattening is about $x_1$, Remember $y=(x_2,\cdots,x_n),$ Then define the continuation supported on $O_{\delta_i}(x_i)$ (zero continuation can be done) $$F_i(x_1,y)=\begin{cases} \varphi_i f(x_1,y)&x_1\ge 0\\ \sum_{j=0}^m C_j\varphi_if(-\lambda_j x_1,y)&x_1<0 \end{cases}.$$ It is $C^m$ in the upper and lower half-planes respectively, and only needs to verify the regularity in the $x_1$ direction when $x_1=0$. Note that you only need to ensure that $\sum_{j=0}^m (-\lambda_j)^kC_j=1,$ $\,\forall\,0\le k\le m,$ That is, there is $F_i\in C^m(\mathbb{R}^n)\subset H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n).$ And since $\det(-\lambda_j)^k$ is the Vandermonde determinant, As long as $\lambda_j>0$ are mutually different, there is a solution $\{C_j\}.$ Therefore, the continuation $F_i\in C^m(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is achievable. Pull it back to the initial $O_{\delta_i}(x_i), $ just do zero extension. In this way, $f\in C^m(\Omega),$ We found a suitable continuation $\widetilde{f}\in C^m(\mathbb{R}^n),$ Satisfies $\parallel\widetilde{f}\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}\le C\parallel f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\Omega)}.$ Generally, for $f\in H^{m,p}(\Omega),$ there is $f_i\xrightarrow{H^{m,p} } f.$ At this time, follow the same method to construct the continuation, It is not difficult to prove that $\widetilde{f_i}\xrightarrow{H^{m,p} } \widetilde{f}.$ Therefore there is still $\parallel\widetilde{f}\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}\le C\parallel f\parallel_{H^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}.$ The proposition is proved. In fact, in the proof, just let $j$ start counting from $1$. In this way, although the continuation is $C^{m-1}$, But since there are $C^m$ on both sides of $x_1=0$, the continuation $F_i$ is still generalized differentiable of order $m$, And the derivative falls in $L^p$ space. Sobolev's inequality ------------- Also known as **embedding theorem**. Define homogeneous Sobolev modules $$|f|_{\dot{H}^{m,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\parallel\partial^\alpha f\parallel_{L^p(\Omega)}^p\right)^{\frac{1}{p} }.$$ Do dimensional analysis on $x$: Replace $x$ with $kx,$ and there will be an extra constant $k^{\frac{n}{p}-m}.$ The dimensions of the elements matching this formula with respect to $x$ should also be $\frac{n}{p}-m.$ **Theorem 1.2** (Sobolev’s inequality (embedded theorem)). *When $\frac{n}{p}=\frac{n}{q}-m$, there is* $$\parallel f\parallel_{L^p(\Omega)}\le C|f|_{\dot{H}^{m,q}(\Omega)},\quad \,\forall\,f\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$$ **Proof:** First make basic observations to make the conclusion of the proof as simple as possible. Assume $m\ge 2,$ For all inequalities below $m-1$, then $$\parallel f\parallel_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\le C_{m-1}|f|_{\dot{H}^{m-1,q_1}(\Omega)}.$$ Remember $g_\alpha=\partial^\alpha f,$ then $$\parallel g_\alpha\parallel_{L^{q_1}(\Omega)}\le C_1|g_\alpha|_{\dot{H}^{1,q_2}(\Omega)}.$$ thus getting $$\parallel f\parallel_{L^p(\Omega)}\le C_1C_{m-1}\sum_{\alpha}|g_\alpha|_{\dot{H}^{1,q_2}(\Omega)}\le C_m|f|_{\dot{H}^{m,q_2}(\Omega)},$$ $C_m$ can be taken as $nC_1 C_{m-1}.$ due to $\begin{cases} \frac{n}{p}=\frac{n}{q_1}-m+1,\\ \frac{n}{q_1}=\frac{n}{q_2}-1. \end{cases},$ found that $q_2=q,$ satisfies $\frac{n}{p}=\frac{n}{q}-m.$ Therefore, it only needs to be proved for the case $m=1$. Next, assume that $q=1,$ $p=\frac{n}{n-1}$ has been proved. Then for general $p,q,$ Taking $g=|f|^{\frac{p(n-1)}{n} },$ we have: $$\parallel f\parallel_{L^p}^{\frac{p(n-1)}{n} }=\parallel g\parallel_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1} } }\le C|g|_{\dot{H}^{1,1} }=\widetilde{C}\sum_{j=1}^n\parallel|f|^{\frac{p(n-1)}{n}-1}\partial_jf\parallel_{L^1}\le \widetilde{\widetilde{C} }|f|_{\dot{H}^{1,q} }\parallel|f|^{\frac{p(n-1)}{n}-1}\parallel_{L^{q^\ast } }.$$ Among them, $q^\ast $ satisfies $\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^\ast }=1.$ Combining $\frac{n}{p}=\frac{n}{q}-1,$ we have $q^\ast [\frac{p(n-1)}{n}-1]=p.$ Therefore $$\parallel|f|^{\frac{p(n-1)}{n}-1}\parallel_{L^{q^\ast } }=\parallel f\parallel_{L^{p} }^{\frac{p(n-1)}{n}-1},$$ By moving this term to the left side of the inequality, the proof in the general case is given. So we only need to consider $q=1,$ The case of $p=\frac{n}{n-1}$. When $n=1$, $p=\infty,$ Certificate required$\parallel f\parallel_{L^\infty}\le C|f|_{\dot{H}^{1,1} }.$ Since the function $f\in C_c^\infty(\Omega),$ on $\mathbb{R}^1$, the conclusion is obvious, Just take $C=1$ or even $\frac{1}{2}$. For general $n,$ assuming $n-1$ is correct, Remember $y=(x_2,\cdots,x_n),$ then we have: $$\begin{aligned} \parallel f\parallel_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1} } }&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R} }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1} }|f(x_1,y)|^{\frac{n}{n-1} }dx_1dy\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n} }\\ &\le\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1} }\int_{\mathbb{R} }|f(x_1,y)|dx_1\left(\int_{\mathbb{R} }|\partial_1 f(y_1,y)|dy_1\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1} }\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n} }\\ &\le \left|\left|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R} }|\partial_1 f(y_1,y)|dy_1\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1} }\right|\right|_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_y)}^{\frac{n-1}{n} }\left|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R} }|f(y_1,y)|dy_1\right|\right|_{L^q(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_y)}^{\frac{n-1}{n} } \end{aligned}$$ Taking $q=\frac{n-1}{n-2},$ then $p=n-1.$ Applying the assumption, we have: $$\begin{aligned} \parallel f\parallel_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1} } }&\le C\left|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R} }|\partial_1 f(y_1,y)|dy_1\right|\right|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_y)}^{\frac{1}{n} }\left(\sum_{j=2}^m\left|\left|\partial_j\int_{\mathbb{R} }|f(y_1,y)|dy_1\right|\right|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_y)}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n} }\\ &\le C\parallel\partial_1 f\parallel_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)}^{\frac{1}{n} }\left(\sum_{j=2}^m\parallel\partial_j f\parallel_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)}\right)^\frac{n-1}{n}\\ &\le \widetilde{C}\sum_{j=1}^m \parallel\partial_j f\parallel_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)}=\widetilde{C}|f|_{\dot{H}^{1,1} } \end{aligned}$$ The inequality sign in the last line is obtained from Young's inequality. This gives the final proof. Let $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ be completed by $H^{m,q}$ norm and the space obtained is $H_0^{m,q}(\Omega),$ Then the inequality is also true for the functions in $H_0^{m,q}(\Omega)\cap L^p(\Omega)$. In particular, When $\Omega=\mathbb{R}^n$, $H_0^{m,q}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is $H^{m,q}(\mathbb{R}^n).$ The article was last updated on 2021-10-25 18:35:52
