On $\mathbb{R}^n$, take $\varphi(x)=\begin{cases} e^{\frac{1}{|x|^2-1} },&|x|<1\\ 0,&|x|\ge 1. \end{cases},$ Let $\alpha(x)=\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \varphi dx}\varphi(x).$ Then $\alpha(x)\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n),$ $\operatorname{supp}\alpha=B_1,$ satisfies $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\alpha dx=1.$ Take $\alpha_\varepsilon=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^n}\alpha(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n),$ Then $\operatorname{supp}\alpha_\varepsilon=B_\varepsilon,$ Also satisfies $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\alpha_\varepsilon dx=1.$ Define the convolution operation $f\ast g:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(y)g(x-y)dy.$ Then for any open set $E,$ $f=1_E\ast \alpha_\varepsilon=\int_E \alpha_\varepsilon(x-y)dy,$ Satisfy the following properties: 1. $f\in [0,1];$ 2. $\,\forall\,x$ satisfies $d(x,E^c)< \varepsilon,$ $f(x)=1;$ 3. $\operatorname{supp}f=\overline{O_\varepsilon(E)};$ 4. $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n).$ Then on the manifold coordinate card $(U,\varphi),$ if there is an open set $V\Subset W\Subset U,$ Then $\varphi(V)\Subset \varphi(W)\Subset \varphi(U).$ Remember $\delta=\min(d(\overline{\varphi(V)},\varphi(W)^c),d(\overline{\varphi(W)},\varphi(U)^c))>0,$ Then take $f=(1_{\varphi(W)}\ast \alpha_{\frac{\delta}{2} })\circ \varphi,$ Satisfy $f\in [0,1],$ $f|_{V}=1,$ and $f\in C_c^\infty(U).$ embedding of compact manifolds ---------- The basic idea is to embed the coordinate neighborhoods into their respective $\mathbb{R}^m$ for each coordinate card, Just leave one more dimension for identification. **Theorem 1.1**. *Suppose $M$ is an $m$-dimensional compact smooth manifold, then there are positive integers $n,$ and smooth maps $\varphi:M\rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n,$ such that $(M,\varphi)$ is a regular submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$. * **Certificate:** Take the coordinate atlas of $M$$\{(U_i,\varphi_i;u_i^j)\}_{i=1}^k,$ Since it is a compact manifold, a finite coordinate atlas can be obtained. Take $M\setminus\bigcup_{j=2}^k U_j\subset V_1\Subset W_1\Subset U_1,$ Inductively take $M\setminus(\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}V_j\cup\bigcup_{j=i+1}^k U_j)\subset V_i\Subset W_i\Subset U_i,$ Obtain the detailed $\{V_i\}$ to cover $M,$ and for each $V_i$ according to the previous description, There exists $f_i\in C_c^\infty(U_i),$ $f_i|_{V_i}=1.$, and $f_i$ zero can be extended to $M$. Define $n=k(m+1)$ smooth functions on $M$: $$\begin{aligned} x_i^0&=f_i\\ x_i^j&=f_i \cdot u_{i}^j\\ \end{aligned}$$ where $i=1,\cdots,k,$ $j=1,\cdots,m.$ Although each function is only defined on $U_i$, it can also be extended to the entire $M$. This gives a smooth map $\Phi:M\rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n,$ Assert that $(M,\Phi)$ is the regular submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$. First of all, it is indeed a submanifold: first verify the rank, since $\,\forall\,p\in M,$ $\,\exists\,V_i\supset O_p\ni p,$ then $f_i|_{O_p}\equiv 1,$ Thus $\det \frac{\partial(x^{1}_i,\cdots,x^m_i)}{\partial (u_i^1,\cdots,u_i^m)}=1,$ $r(\Phi)=m.$ Therefore $(M,\Phi)$ is an immersed submanifold. Next if $\Phi(p)=\Phi(q),$ then if $V_i\supset p,$ $f_i(p)=1=f_i(q)\Rightarrow q\in V_i.$ Thus $\varphi_i(p)=\varphi_i(q),$ Since $\varphi_i$ is a homeomorphism, $p=q.$ therefore $\Phi$ is an injective, $(M,\Phi)$ is a submanifold. Since $M$ is compact, according to the theorem of the previous lesson, $(M,\Phi)$ is automatically an embedded submanifold, And another theorem shows that $(M,\Phi)$ is also a regular submanifold. Dimension theory ------ The embedding obtained in this way is obviously a bit violent and has too many redundant dimensions. Take $S^2$ as an example, At least two coordinate cards must be used to describe, At this time, according to the above method, it needs to be embedded into the $n=2\times(2+1)=6$ dimensional space. If you use standard six coordinate cards to describe it, it will even be embedded in an $18$ dimensional space. In fact, we can use dimension theory to reduce the dimension of the peripheral space.
