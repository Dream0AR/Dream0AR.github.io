Compatibility ------ **Proposition 1.1**. *Suppose $\mathcal{D}$ is a smooth distribution on $M$. If $\mathcal{D}$ is integrable, then $\,\forall\,X,Y\in \chi(\mathcal{D}),$ is $\,\forall\,p\in M,$ $X_p,Y_p\in \mathcal{D}_p,$ then $[X, Y]\in \chi(\mathcal{D}).$* **Proof:** Take any coordinate system $(U,\varphi;x^i),$ containing $p$ To prove $[X,Y]_p\in \mathcal{D}_p.$ According to the integrability, there exists an integrator manifold $i:N^k\hookrightarrow M,$ through the $p$ point. From a local perspective, according to the rank theorem, It can be regarded as a slice. Therefore, $X,Y$ only has smooth components on the first $k$ natural bases. Calculated, $[X,Y]$ also only has smooth components on the first $k$ natural bases, Therefore $[X,Y]\in \chi(\mathcal{D}).$ **Note 1.2**. *It can be proved that if $X_1,X_2$ and $Y_1,Y_2$ are $F$ related, that is, $F_\ast It can be proved that under the problem setting, $[X|_N,Y|_N]$ and $[X,Y]$ are related to $i$, that is, $i_\ast [X|_N,Y|_N]=[X,Y],$ and thus $[X|_N,Y|_N]=[X,Y]|_N.$* **Definition 1.3**. *Suppose $\mathcal{D}$ is a smooth distribution on $M$. If $\,\forall\,X,Y\in \chi(\mathcal{D}),$ $[X,Y]\in \chi(D),$, then $\mathcal{D}$ is said to be **involution**.* From the previous proposition, integrability $\Rightarrow$ involution. In fact, to prove the involution, Just explain the local frame field $X_1,\cdots,X_k,$ of $\,\forall\,\mathcal{D}$ $[X_i,X_j]\in \chi(\mathcal{D}),$ $\,\forall\,1\le i,j\le k.$ The original condition for satisfying involutivity can be explained by using the frame property. Therefore, these two conditions are equivalent. Frobenius theorem ------------- **Theorem 1.4** (Frobenius). *Suppose $\mathcal{D}$ is a $k$-dimensional distribution on $M^m$. If $\mathcal{D}$ is involutive, then $\,\forall\,p\in M,$ $\,\exists\,$ contains $p$ coordinate system $(U,\varphi; x^i)$ makes $k$-dimensional slices all integral manifolds of $\mathcal{D}$.* **Proof:** When $k=1$, according to the Flow box theorem, each point has a local coordinate system, Let any $x^1$-curve be the integral curve of $X$, That is, $1$-dimensional slices are all $1$-dimensional integral molecule manifolds of $\mathcal{D}$. Inductively, if the proposition holds for $k-1$ dimensional distribution, Then let $\mathcal{D}$ be the $k$-dimensional distribution involuted on $M$, Take the local standard field $X_1,\cdots, X_k,$ then according to the involution property, $$[X_i,X_j]\equiv 0 \mod \{X_1,\cdots,X_k\}.$$ According to the Flow box theorem, The coordinate system can be obtained such that $X_k=\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}y_k},$ Then for $1\le \lambda,\mu,\nu\le k-1,$ Let $X_\lambda'=X_\lambda-X_\lambda(y^k)X_k,$ then $X_{\lambda}'(y_k)=0,$ $X_k(y_k)=1,$ Thus $\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{span}\{X_1',\cdots,X_{k-1}',X_k\},$ It is still a local benchmark field. Calculated, The $k-1$ dimensional distribution $\mathcal{D}':=\operatorname{span}\{X_1',\cdots,X_{k-1}'\}$ alone satisfies the involution property. At this time, by the inductive assumption, $\mathcal{D}'$ is an integrable distribution, There is a coordinate system $(V,\psi;z^i)$ such that $k-1$ dimensional slices are all integrator manifolds of $\mathcal{D}'$. At this time $\frac{\partial {}y^k}{\partial {}z^\lambda}=0,$ $\,\forall\,1\le \lambda\le k-1.$ $\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{span}\{\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}z^1},\cdots,\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}z^{k-1} },X_k\}.$ Effect$y^k,$ Calculate $[\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}z^\lambda},X_k]\equiv 0\mod \{\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}z^1}\cdots\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}z^{k-1} }\}.$ Express $X_k$ as $\sum_{A=1}^m \xi^A \frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}z^A},$ Then from the previous formula, $\sum_{A=k}^m\frac{\partial {}\xi^A}{\partial {}z^\lambda}\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}z^A}=0,$ This shows that $\xi^A=\xi^A(z^k,\cdots,z^m),$ $\,\forall\,A\ge k.$ Let $X_k'=X_k-\sum_{\lambda=1}^{k-1}\xi^\lambda\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}z^\lambda},$ Only the following components are retained. According to the Flowbox theorem, The new coordinate system can be obtained such that $X'_{k}=\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^k},$ It satisfies $x^\lambda=z^\lambda,$ $1\le \lambda\le k-1.$, that is, the first $k-1$ coordinates remain unchanged. Then at this time, we have $\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{span}\{\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^1},\cdots,\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^{k} }\},$ $k$-dimensional slices are all integral manifolds. unit decomposition -------- **Theorem 1.5**. *Suppose there is a compact set $\overline{V}\subset U,$ on the smooth manifold $M^m$, then there exists $f\in C_c^\infty(M),$ such that $f\in[0,1],$ $f|_V\equiv 1,$ $f|_{M\setminus U}\equiv 0.$* - Suppose $M$ is a topological space, $\{A_\alpha\}$ is a family of subsets of $M$. Such as $\bigcup_{\alpha}A_\alpha=M,$ Then $\{A_\alpha\}$ is said to be a **cover** of $M$. - Let $\{A_\alpha\}$ be the cover of $M$, if $\,\forall\,p\in M,$ There exists $U\ni p$ such that $U$ intersects only a limited number of $A_\alpha$, Then $\{A_\alpha\}$ is said to be a **locally finite** coverage of $M$. - Let $\{U_\alpha\},$ $\{V_\beta\}$ be two covers of $M$. If $\,\forall\,\beta,$ $\,\exists\,\alpha$ makes $V_\beta\subset U_\alpha,$ Then $\{V_\beta\}$ is said to be the **refined** of $\{U_\alpha\}$. **Definition 1.6**. *$M$ is a topological space. If any open covering $\{U_\alpha\}$ of $M$ has local finite thinning, then $M$ is said to be **quasi-compact**.* Now suppose $M$ is the $m$-dimensional $C^\infty$ manifold, and $\{A_\alpha\}$ is the open cover of $M$. According to the second countable axiom, $\{A_\alpha\}$ exists countably plus thin $\{U_j\},$ And each open set is a coordinate graph $(U_j,\varphi_j).$ The coordinate graph can be further transformed into a locally finite one, And $\varphi_j(U_j)=B_1^m(0),$ $\{V_j=\varphi_j^{-1}(B_{1/2}^m(0))\}$ is also an open covering of $M$. **Definition 1.7**. *Suppose $M$ is a smooth manifold. If $\{f_i\}\in C^\infty(M)$ satisfies $f_i\in [0,1],$ $\sum_i f_i\equiv 1,$ and $\{\operatorname{supp}f_i\}$ is a local finite cover of $M$, then $\{f_i\}$ is called the **unit decomposition** of $M$.* **Theorem 1.8** (Unit decomposition). *Assume $M^m$ is a smooth manifold, then there is unit decomposition $\{f_i\}.$* **Proof:** $\,\forall\,\bigcup_\alpha A_\alpha\supset M,$ From the above lemma, There exists local finite addition $\{(U_i,\varphi_i)\},$ $\varphi_i(U_i)=B_1^m(0).$ and then $\,\exists\,\{\widetilde{f}_i\}\in C_c^\infty(U_i),$ $\widetilde{f}_i\in[0,1],$ $\widetilde{f}_i|_{V_i}\equiv 1.$ Finally, take $f_i=\frac{\widetilde{f}_i}{\sum_i \widetilde{f}_i}$. In fact there is $\operatorname{supp}f_i\subset U_i\subset A_\alpha,$ It is said that $\{f_i\}$ is **subordinate** to the unit decomposition of $\{A_\alpha\}$.
