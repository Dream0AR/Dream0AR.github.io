Large scale Ricci curvature on graphs - Mark Kempton · Gabor Lippner · Florentin Münch Many curvatures on the graph are only limited to the vicinity of points and their neighbors, and the local range is too small. This may result in the inability to derive interesting results. This article introduces the neighborhood radius $R,$ Define various concepts with scale $R$ and derive curvature. mark ---- For the weighted graph $G(V,w,m),$ $$\Delta f(x):=\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_yw(x,y)(f(y)-f(x)),$$ From this derives the Dirichlet form for energy methods: $$Q(f,f):=\left<{}-\Delta f,f\right>,$$ Then derive Bakry-Emery(carré du champ) operator: $$\Gamma(f,g):=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta(fg)-f\Delta g-g\Delta f),$$ Calculated: $$\Gamma(f,g)(x)=\frac{1}{2m(x)}\sum_y w(x,y)[f(y)-f(x)][g(y)-g(x)].$$ Remember $\Gamma f:=\Gamma(f,f),$ Then $\Gamma f=\frac{1}{2}\parallel\nabla f\parallel^2.$ The thermal semigroup generated by $\Delta$ is denoted as $\{P_t=e^{t\Delta}\},$ such that $P_tf$ satisfies the heat equation $$\partial_t P_tf=\Delta P_tf.$$ Curvature export -------- For the Riemannian manifold $M, $ Ricci curvature with $K$ as the lower bound is equivalent to $$|\nabla P_tf|\le e^{-Kt}P_t|\nabla f|,\quad \,\forall\,C_c^\infty(M).$$ We define $R$-gradient to generalize this concept to graphs on a large scale. Let $B_R(x)=\{y:d(x,y)\le R\}.$ for $f:V\rightarrow \mathbb{R},$ $x\in V,$ Definition: $$|\nabla_R f|(x):=\max_{y\in B_R(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|.$$ Next we consider the order $$|\nabla_R P_tf|\le e^{-Kt}P_t|\nabla_R f|$$ What are the necessary conditions for its establishment. Since both sides are equal when $t=0$, the derivative on the right side should be larger than the derivative on the left when $t=0$. Note that the curvature condition $K$ can be put forward by deriving the derivative on both sides, and we perform the derivation. Since $P_tf$ changes continuously, $\,\exists\,y_0\in B_R(x)$ makes $$|\nabla_RP_tf|(x)=|P_tf(y_0)-P_tf(x)|,\quad \,\forall\,t\in[0,\varepsilon].$$ Then for $y\in B_R(x),$ which satisfies $|\nabla_R f|(x)=|f(y)-f(x)|$ $$|\nabla_RP_tf|(x)=|P_tf(y_0)-P_tf(x)|\ge |P_tf(y)-P_tf(x)|,\quad \,\forall\,t\in[0,\varepsilon],$$ And when $t=0$, both sides are equal, so when $t=0$, the derivative on the left side is also larger than the derivative on the right side. When calculating $t=0$, the derivative on the right side is $$\partial_t (P_tf(y)-P_tf(x))\operatorname{sgn}(P_tf(y)-P_tf(x))=(\Delta f(y)-\Delta f(x))\operatorname{sgn}(f(y)-f(x)),$$ Therefore, for any $y\in B_R(x),$ that satisfies $|\nabla_R f|(x)=|f(y)-f(x)|$ $$\begin{aligned} (\Delta f(y)-\Delta f(x))\operatorname{sgn}(f(y)-f(x))&\le \partial_t |\nabla_RP_tf|(x)\le \partial_t (e^{-Kt}P_t|\nabla_Rf|)\\ &\le -K|\nabla_R f|(x)+\Delta |\nabla_R f|(x). \end{aligned}$$ So we have the inequality for curvature: $$K|\nabla_R f|(x)\le\Delta |\nabla_R f|(x)-(\Delta f(y)-\Delta f(x))\operatorname{sgn}(f(y)-f(x)).$$ The dimensions about $f$ are $1,$. Let $|\nabla_R f|=1,$ The following curvature definition method is derived from this: **Definition 1.1**. *Arbitrarily specify $R\in \mathbb{N},$ $x,y\in V,$ to define **Gradient-Ollivier curvature**:* $$K_R(x,y):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c} f:V\rightarrow \mathbb{R}\\ |\nabla_Rf|(x)=1\\ |f(y)-f(x)|=|\nabla_R f|(x) \end{subarray} }\Delta|\nabla_R f|(x)-(\Delta f(y)-\Delta f(x))\operatorname{sgn}(f(y)-f(x)).$$ Contact Ollivier Curvature ------------------ **Theorem 1.2**. *$\,\forall\,x\sim y,$ We have:* $$K_1^{Ol}(x,y)\ge K_1(x,y).$$ **Certificate:** By definition, $$K_1^{Ol}(x,y)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c} \parallel\nabla f\parallel_{\infty}=1\\ f(y)-f(x)=1 \end{subarray} }\Delta f(x)-\Delta f(y).$$ Let $f$ be the function that achieves the minimum value, then $|\nabla_1 f|(x)=1,$ and $\,\forall\,z\sim x,$ $|\nabla_1 f|(z)\le 1.$ This means $\Delta |\nabla_1 f|(x)\le 0.$ Therefore: $$K_1(x,y)\le \Delta |\nabla_1f|(x)+K_1^{Ol}(x,y)\le K_1^{Ol}(x,y).$$ Gradient estimation -------- Next, it will be shown that this way of defining curvature is actually sufficient. First, the following lemma is required: **Lemma 1.3**. *If $K_R(x,y)\ge K,$ $\,\forall\,d(x,y)\le R.$ Given $t>0,$ take the function* $$G_s:=e^{-Ks}P_s|\nabla_R P_{t-s}f|,\quad s\in[0,t]$$ *Then $G_s$ is an increasing function.* **Certificate:** Just prove that the lower derivative of $G_s$$\underline{\partial}_sG_s:=\varliminf\limits_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{G_{s+h}-G_s}{h}\ge0.$ Calculated: $$\underline{\partial}_sG_s=-KG_s+e^{-Ks}(P_s(\underline\partial_s|\nabla_RP_{t-s}f|)+\Delta P_s|\nabla_R P_{t-s}f|).$$ In the same way as when exporting curvature, $\,\forall\,y\in B_R(x)$ satisfies $|\nabla_R P_{t-s}f|(x)=|P_{t-s}f(y)-P_{t-s}f(x)|,$ There are: $$\begin{aligned} \underline\partial_s|\nabla_RP_{t-s}f|(x)&\ge \partial_s|P_{t-s}f(y)-P_{t-s}f(x)|\\ &=(\partial_s P_{t-s}f(y)-\partial_s P_{t-s}f(x))\operatorname{sgn}(P_{t-s}f(y)-P_{t-s}f(x))\\ &=-(\Delta P_{t-s}f(y)-\Delta P_{t-s}f(x))\operatorname{sgn}(P_{t-s}f(y)-P_{t-s}f(x))\\ &\ge |\nabla_R P_{t-s}f|(x)K_R(x,y)-\Delta |\nabla_RP_{t-s}f|(x)\\ &\ge K|\nabla_R P_{t-s}f|(x)-\Delta |\nabla_R P_{t-s} f|(x) \end{aligned}$$ Since $P_s\Delta=\Delta P_s$ and for the non-negative function $f\ge 0,$ $P_sf\ge 0,$ we have $\underline{\partial}_s G_s\ge 0,$ The lemma is proved. Comparing $G_0$ with $G_t,$, we immediately get the following theorem: **Theorem 1.4**. *If $K_R(x,y)\ge K,$ $\,\forall\,d(x,y)\le R,$ then* $$|\nabla_R P_tf|\le e^{-Kt}P_t|\nabla_R f|.$$ attenuation ------ When $K=0$, the above gradient estimate no longer gives good decay properties. We wish to prove the following estimate: **Theorem 1.5**. *If $\,\forall\,x,y\in V,$ $d(x,y)\le R,$ has $K_R(x,y)\ge 0.$ then* $$|\nabla_R P_tf|^2\le \frac{C}{t}\parallel f\parallel^2_{\infty},$$ We hope to use $\Gamma f$ to estimate $|\nabla_R f|^2,$ to give its upper bound. However, for $R\ge 2, $ $\Gamma$ only involves one-step neighborhood, So the basic idea is to sum $\Gamma f$ over a large enough neighborhood to control $|\nabla_R f|.$ We need the following tools. **Definition 1.6**. *For the graph $G=(G,w,m),$, write $Q(x,y):=\frac{w(x,y)}{m(x)},$ Then $\Delta f(x)=\sum_y Q(x,y)(f(y)-f(x)).$* *Remember $\deg(x):=\sum_y Q(x,y),$ $Q_{\min} :=\inf_{x\sim y}Q(x,y). $ Definition $A:=\Delta+\deg_{\max},$ is called **average operator**.* It is easy to see that it satisfies some simple properties: 1. $f\ge 0\Rightarrow Af\ge 0;$ 2. $\parallel Af\parallel_{\infty}\le \deg_{\max}\parallel f\parallel_{\infty};$ 3. $P_t A=AP_t.$ **Lemma 1.7**. *$\,\forall\,f:V\rightarrow \mathbb{R},$ We have* $$|\nabla_R f|^2\le \frac{2R}{Q_{\min}^R}A^{R-1}\Gamma f.$$ **Certificate:** First estimate $A^{R-1}\Gamma f.$ and note that $\,\forall\,g\ge 0,$ $$Ag(x)\ge \sum_y Q(x,y) g(y)\ge Q_{\min}\sum_{y\sim x}g(y).$$ Therefore, $$A^{R-1}\Gamma f\ge Q_{\min}^{R-1}\sum_{x\sim x_1\sim\cdots\sim x_{R-1} } \Gamma f(x_{R-1}).$$ Similarly, $\Gamma f$ itself satisfies $$\Gamma f(x)\ge \frac{1}{2}Q_{\min}\sum_{y\sim x}(f(z)-f(x))^2.$$ Therefore, $$A^{R-1}\Gamma f(x)\ge \frac{1}{2}Q_{\min}^{R}\sum_{x\sim x_1\sim\cdots\sim x_{R} } (f(x_{R})-f(x_{R-1}))^2.$$ Then let $|\nabla_R f|(x)=|f(x)-f(y)|,$ $y\in B_R(x).$ Take $x=y_0\sim \cdots\sim y_r=y,$ $r\le R.$ Then from the basic inequality $\frac{a_1+\cdots+a_n}{n}\le \sqrt{\frac{a_1^2+\cdots+a_n^2}{n} },$ There are: $$\begin{aligned} A^{R-1}\Gamma f(x)&\ge \frac{1}{2}Q_{\min}^{R}\sum_{k=1}^r(f(y_k)-f(y_{k-1}))^2\\ &\ge \frac{1}{2}Q_{\min}^R\frac{1}{r}(f(y_r)-f(y_0))^2\\ &\ge \frac{1}{2}Q_{\min}^R\frac{1}{R}|\nabla_R f|^2. \end{aligned}$$ The lemma is proved. Now we can prove the previous theorem. In fact we can prove the following stronger version: **Theorem 1.8**. *Suppose $K_R(x,y)\ge K,$ $\,\forall\,x,y\in V,$ $d(x,y)\le R.$ Then* $$\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}|\nabla_R P_tf|^2\le R\frac{\deg_{\max}^{R-1} }{Q_{\min}^{R} }\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}^2.$$ *When $K=0$, $\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}:=t,$ is the previous theorem.* **Proof:** Let $H_s:=A^{R-1}P_s[(P_{t-s}f)^2],$ $G_s:=e^{-Ks}P_s|\nabla_R P_{t-s} f|.$ Then take the derivative of $H$ and apply the previous lemma: $$\begin{aligned} \partial_s H_s&= P_s A^{R-1}\Delta(P_{t-s}f)^2-2P_s A^{R-1}P_{t-s}f \cdot \Delta P_{t-s} f\\ &=2P_sA^{R-1} \Gamma (P_{t-s}f)\\ &\ge \frac{Q_{\min}^R}{R}P_s|\nabla_R P_{t-s}f|^2\\ &\ge \frac{Q_{\min}^R}{R}(P_s|\nabla_R P_{t-s}f|)^2\\ &=\frac{Q_{\min}^R}{R}e^{2Ks}G_s^2. \end{aligned}$$ Integrate and use $G_s$ to be monoincreasing, we get: $$H_t-H_0\ge \frac{Q_{\min}^R}{R}G_0^2\int_0^te^{2Ks}ds=\frac{Q_{\min}^R}{R}G_0^2\frac{e^{2Ks}-1}{2K}.$$ Then since $\parallel Ag\parallel_{\infty}\le \deg_{\max}\parallel g\parallel_{\infty},$ $H_0\ge 0,$ $$\begin{aligned} \deg_{\max}^{R-1}\parallel f\parallel_\infty^2&\ge A^{R-1}(P_tf)^2=H_t\\ &\ge \frac{Q_{\min}^R}{R}G_0^2\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}\\ &=\frac{Q_{\min}^R}{R}|\nabla_R P_tf|^2\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}. \end{aligned}$$ The theorem is established after sorting it out. It should be noted that this theorem only applies when $\deg_{\max}<\infty,$ $Q_{\min}>0,$ That is, the degree of graph $G$ has a consistent upper bound, and $Q$ has a non-zero consistent lower bound. exponential curvature -------- Ollivier curvature is characterized by the following gradient estimate with $K$ as the lower bound: $$\parallel\nabla_R P_tf\parallel_{\infty}\le e^{-Kt}\parallel\nabla_R f\parallel_{\infty}.$$ The idea of exponential Ollivier curvature is to enhance the gradient estimate for $f>0,$ as follows: $$\parallel\nabla_R \log P_tf\parallel_{\infty}\le e^{-Kt}\parallel\nabla_R \log f\parallel_{\infty}.$$ This is indeed a stronger form, Because $\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log(1+\varepsilon f)\rightarrow f,$ $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.$ Therefore, the following inequality holds and the above gradient estimate can be deduced by taking the limit. Then $\,\forall\,x,y\in V$ satisfies $d(x,y)\le R,$ And $\log f(y)-\log f(x)=\parallel\nabla_R \log f\parallel_{\infty},$ Similar to the previous derivation method, we take the derivative on both sides. When $t=0$, we have $$\partial_t (\log P_t f(y)-\log P_t f(x))=\frac{\Delta f(y)}{f(y)}-\frac{\Delta f(x)}{f(x)}\le -K\parallel\nabla_R \log f\parallel_{\infty}.$$ Let $f=\exp(g),$ have the following definition: **Definition 1.9**. *Arbitrarily specify $R\in \mathbb{N},$ $x,y\in V,$ to define **Exponential Gradient-Ollivier Curvature**:* $$K_R^e(x,y)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c} r>0\\ \parallel\nabla_R g\parallel_{\infty}=r\\ g(y)-g(x)=r \end{subarray} }\frac{1}{r} \left(\frac{\Delta \exp(g)}{\exp(g)}(x)-\frac{\Delta \exp(g)}{\exp(g)}(y)\right)$$ Similarly, the way it is defined is adequate. **Theorem 1.10**. *If $K_R^e(x,y)\ge K,$ $\,\forall\,d(x,y)\le R,$ and $G$ is a finite graph.
