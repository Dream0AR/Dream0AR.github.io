《微分流形》第五章-张量积 "Differential Manifolds" Chapter 5-Tensor Product

The reflective nature of everything ---------- Let $V, W, U$ be vector spaces, $\psi:V\times W\rightarrow U$ be bilinear mapping. If $\,\forall\,$ vector space $Z,$ bilinear mapping $f:V\times W\rightarrow Z,$ There exists a unique linear mapping $g:U\rightarrow Z,$ such that $f=g\circ\psi,$ is said to be $(U,\psi)$ has the **universal mapping property**. **Lemma 1**. *$(U,\psi)$ is unique in the following sense: if $(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{\psi})$ has the universal mapping property, then $\,\exists\,g:U\cong \widetilde{U}$ is a linear isomorphism, and $\widetilde{\psi}=g\circ\psi.$* **Proof:** Just state $\,\exists\,g,\widetilde{g},$ such that $$\widetilde\psi=g\circ\psi=(g\circ \widetilde{g})\circ\widetilde\psi=\mathrm{id}\circ\widetilde\psi;$$ $$\psi=\widetilde{g}\circ\widetilde\psi=(\widetilde g\circ g)\circ\psi=\mathrm{id}\circ\psi.$$ It can be explained by using the uniqueness of the universal mapping property that $g\circ\widetilde{g}=\mathrm{id}_{\widetilde U},$ $\widetilde{g}\circ g=\mathrm{id}_{U}.$ **Proposition 2**. *Suppose $V, W$ are vector spaces, then $(V\otimes W,h=\otimes)$ has the universal mapping property.* **Proof:** For any bilinear $f:V\times W\rightarrow Z,$ $f$ is determined by the value $f(e_i,\sigma_\alpha)$ on the basis. It only needs to be stated that such $$f(e_i,\sigma_\alpha)=g\circ h(e_i,\sigma_\alpha)=g(e_i\otimes \sigma_\alpha)\eqno{(\ast )}$$ The linear mapping $g$ of exists uniquely. Note that $e_i\otimes \sigma_\alpha$ is the basis of $V\otimes W$, Therefore, the $(\ast )$ formula determines $g$, which illustrates the uniqueness. As for existence, just define $g$ according to $(\ast )$ formula, because the condition is satisfied on the basis, The mapping is linear, so it naturally satisfies $f=g\circ h.$ on all elements. **Note 3**. *Due to uniqueness, $(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{\psi})\cong (V\otimes W,h),$ therefore the tensor product can always be used to express the universal mapping property.* **Corollary 4**. *$\mathcal{L}(V,W;Z)\cong \mathcal{L}(V\otimes W;Z),$ is connected by $f\mapsto g$ in the property. In particular, $\mathcal{L}(V,W;\mathbb{R})=V^\ast \otimes W^\ast \cong (V\otimes W)^\ast .$* tensor product ------ Generalized, let $\varphi\in \mathcal{L}(V_1,\cdots,V_r;\mathbb{R}),$ $\psi\in \mathcal{L}(W_1,\cdots,W_s;\mathbb{R}),$ Definition $$(\varphi\otimes \psi)(v_1,\cdots,v_r,w_1,\cdots,w_s):=\varphi(v_1,\cdots,v_r)\cdot\psi(w_1,\cdots,w_s).$$ Then $$h=\otimes:\mathcal{L}(V_1,\cdots,V_r;\mathbb{R})\times \mathcal{L}(W_1,\cdots,W_s;\mathbb{R})\rightarrow \mathcal{L}(V_1,\cdots,V_r,W_1,\cdots,W_s;\mathbb{R}),$$ It satisfies bilinearity, that is, the tensor product satisfies the distributive law. It is easy to verify that it also satisfies the associative law. But generally, The tensor product does not satisfy the commutative law. Furthermore, $\varphi_i\in V_i^\ast =\mathcal{L}(V_i;\mathbb{R}),$ Define the tensor product $\varphi_1\otimes \cdots\otimes \varphi_r\in \mathcal{L}(V_1,\cdots,V_r;\mathbb{R}).$ specific, $$(\varphi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes \varphi_r)(v_1,\cdots,v_r)=\varphi_1(v_1)\cdots\varphi_r(v_r),$$ i.e. we get $$\begin{aligned} \otimes^r:V_1^*\times\cdots\times V_r^*&\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(V_1,\cdots,V_r;\mathbb{R}),\\ (\varphi_1,\cdots,\varphi_r)&\mapsto \varphi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes \varphi_r, \end{aligned}$$ is an $r$-heavy linear function, $\otimes$ satisfies the distributive law. And let $\omega_{(i)}^{j_{i} }$ be the basis of $V_i^\ast $, Then $\{\omega_{(1)}^{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes \omega_{(r)}^{j_r}\}$ forms the basis of $V_1^\ast \otimes \cdots\otimes V_r^\ast =\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{Im}\otimes^r\}$. Therefore $\dim V_1^\ast \otimes \cdots\otimes V_r^\ast =\dim V_1\cdots\dim V_r.$ Inductively, the same argument can be proved $$V_1^\ast \otimes \cdots\otimes V_r^\ast =\mathcal{L}(V_1,\cdots,V_r;\mathbb{R}).$$ Due to mutual duality, we can also define $v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_r,$ $v_i\in V_i,$ And $V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_r=\mathcal{L}(V_1^\ast ,\cdots,V_r^\ast ;\mathbb{R}).$ If $\{e^{(i)}_{j_i}\}$ is the basis of $V_i$, Then $\{e^{(1)}_{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e^{(r)}_{j_r}\}$ is the basis of $V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_r$, $\dim V_1\otimes \cdots\otimes V_r=\dim V_1\cdots \dim V_r.$ For the universal mapping property, there are also multiple forms of generalization: that is, let $V_1,\cdots,V_r,U$ be a vector space, $h:V_1\times \cdots \times V_r\rightarrow U$ is a $r$-heavy linear mapping. If $\,\forall\,r$-heavy linear mapping $f:V_1\times \cdots\times V_r\rightarrow Z,$ There exists a unique $g:U\rightarrow Z,$ such that $f=g\circ h.$ Then it is said that $(U,h)$ has the **universal mapping property**. Similarly, such $(U,h)$ is unique in the sense of linear isomorphism, And $(V_1\otimes \cdots\otimes V_r,h)$ has the property of universal mapping. From this it follows that $(V_1\otimes \cdots\otimes V_r)^\ast \cong V_1^\ast \otimes \cdots\otimes V_r^\ast .$ For the general $r$-heavy linear mapping, we have $$\mathcal{L}(V_1,\cdots,V_r;Z)=V_1^\ast \otimes \cdots\otimes V_r^\ast \otimes Z.$$ Define $(r+1)$-heavy linear mapping $$\begin{aligned} f:V_1^*\times\cdots\times V_r^*\times Z&\rightarrow \mathcal{L}(V_1,\cdots,V_r;Z),\\ (v_1^*,\cdots,v_r^*,z)&\mapsto v_1^*\otimes \cdots\otimes v_r^*\cdot z, \end{aligned}$$ Just use the universal mapping property to show that $g$ is isomorphic. Tensor ---- Suppose $V$ is an $n$-dimensional vector space on $\mathbb{R}$, let $$V^r_s=(V\otimes \cdots\otimes V)^r\otimes (V^\ast \otimes\cdots\otimes V^\ast )_s=\mathcal{L}(V^\ast ,\cdots,V^\ast ,V,\cdots,V;\mathbb{R}),$$ It is called $(r,s)$-type tensor space, where the elements are called $(r,s)$-type **tensors**. For example, $V_0^1=V,$ $V_1^0=V^\ast ,$ $V_0^0=\mathbb{R}.$ Let $\{e_i\}$ be the basis of $V$, $\{\omega^i\}$ be the dual basis, Then $\{e_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_r}\otimes \omega^{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes \omega^{j_s}\}$ constitutes the basis of $V^r_s$, $\dim V_s^r=n^{r+s}.$ $\,\forall\,\Phi\in V^r_s,$ Components of $\Phi$$\Phi^{k_1\cdots k_r}_{l_1\cdots l_s}=\Phi(\omega^{k_1},\cdots,\omega^{k_2},e_{l_1},\cdots,e_{l_s}).$ If we take another basis $\{\widetilde{e}_i\}$ and a dual basis $\{\widetilde{\omega}^i\},$ Then we have $\widetilde{e}_i= a_i^je_j,$ $\widetilde{\omega}^i=b_j^i\omega^j,$ Among them $a_i^jb_j^k=\delta_i^k.$ Then after transformation, the change in the components is: $$\widetilde{\Phi}^{i_1\cdots i_r}_{j_1\cdots j_s}=b_{k_1}^{i_1}\cdots b_{k_r}^{i_r} a_{j_1}^{l_1}\cdots a_{j_s}^{l_s} \Phi_{l_1\cdots l_s}^{k_1\cdots k_r}.$$ It is called $r$-order contravariant and $s$-order covariant.
