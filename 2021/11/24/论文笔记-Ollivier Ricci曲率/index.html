Ollivier Ricci Curvature For General Graph Laplacians: Heat Equation, Laplacian Comparison, Non-explosion And Diameter Bounds - Florentin Münch, RadosŁaw K. Wojciechowski Discuss the local finite connected graph $G(V,w,m),$ and the basic definition will not be introduced again. Ollivier curvature ---------- ### Wasserstein distance Remember $f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1),$ if $|f(x)-f(y)|\le d(x,y),$ $\,\forall\,x,y\in V.$ for two given probability measures $\mu,\nu,$ on $V$ Define Wasserstein distance: $$ W(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap \ell_\infty(V)}\int f d\mu-\int f d\nu=\sup_{f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap \ell_\infty(V)}\sum_{x\in V}f(x)(\mu(x)-\nu(x)). $$ Note that the difference constant of $f$ does not change the value. It has an equivalent expression: $$ W(\mu,\nu):=\inf_\rho \sum_{x,y\in V}\rho(x,y)d(x,y), $$ Among them, $\rho:V^2\rightarrow[0,1],$ satisfies $\sum_{y}\rho(x,y)=\mu(x),$ $\sum_{x}\rho(x,y)=\nu(y),$ That is, $\rho(x,y)$ is a joint distribution of $\mu$ and $\nu$, It is called the pairing of $\mu$ and $\nu$. Define $\varepsilon>0,$ $$ m_x^\varepsilon(y):=1_y(x)+\varepsilon\Delta 1_y(x)= \left\{ \begin{aligned} &1-\varepsilon\deg(x),&&y=x\\ &\varepsilon\frac{w(x,y)}{m(x)},&&y\neq x. \end{aligned} \right. $$ Then when $\varepsilon$ is sufficiently small, It is easy to see that it is a finitely supported (non-negative) probability measure. In particular, we have: $$ \int f dm_x^\varepsilon=\sum_y f(y)m_x^\varepsilon(y)=(f+\varepsilon\Delta f)(x). $$ For the standard Laplace operator, the definition of $m_x^\varepsilon$ is consistent with the definition in Lin-Lu-Yau curvature, Let $\varepsilon=1-\alpha.$ Similarly, define $$ \kappa_\varepsilon(x,y):=1-\frac{W(m_x^\varepsilon,m_y^\varepsilon)}{d(x,y)}, \quad \kappa(x,y):=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\kappa_\varepsilon(x,y)}{\varepsilon}. $$ The introduction of $\varepsilon$ is a method of reducing the neighborhood on the imitation manifold. In this way, when $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0$, the point is more likely to stay at $x$, Somewhat similar to blast analysis. ### Free limit expression Define gradient $$ \nabla_{xy}f:=\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{d(x,y)},\quad \,\forall\,x\neq y\in V. $$ definition $$ \Vert\nabla f\Vert_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\neq y}|\nabla_{xy} f|=\sup_{x\sim y}|\nabla_{xy} f|\in [0,\infty]. $$ For $K\ge 0,$ Let $\operatorname{Lip}(K)=\{f\in C(V):\Vert\nabla f\Vert_{\infty}\le K\}.$ The elements are functions with Lipschitz constant $K$. **Theorem 1**. *For $x\neq y,$ we have a limit-free Laplacian representation of Ollivier curvature:* $$ \kappa(x,y)=\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c} f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap C_c(V)\\ \nabla_{yx}f=1 \end{subarray} }\nabla_{xy}\Delta f. $$ **Proof:** By definition, since the measure is finitely supported, the requirement of $\ell^\infty$ can be removed, then: $$ \begin{aligned} W(m_x^\varepsilon,m_y^\varepsilon)&=\sup_{f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)}\sum_z f(z)(m_y^\varepsilon(z)-m_x^\varepsilon(z))\\ &=d(x,y)\sup_{f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)}\nabla_{yx}(f+\varepsilon\Delta f). \end{aligned} $$ Therefore $$ \begin{aligned} \frac{\kappa_\varepsilon(x,y)}{\varepsilon}&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1-\sup_{f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)}\nabla_{yx}(f+\varepsilon\Delta f))\\ &=\inf_{f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)} \nabla_{xy}\Delta f+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1-\nabla_{yx}f)\\ &\le \inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c} f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap C_c(V)\\ \nabla_{yx}f=1 \end{subarray} }\nabla_{xy}\Delta f. \end{aligned} $$ conversely, It is necessary to find the minimum $f_\varepsilon\in \operatorname{Lip}(1).$ that makes $\nabla_{xy}\Delta f+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1-\nabla_{yx}f)$ reach a minimum. Since we hope to obtain the smallest formula, it is only related to $B_1(x)\cup B_1(y)$, You can always transform $f$ into a function supported on $B_{2d(x,y)+2}(x)$ and add or subtract constants to make $f(x)=0,$ without affecting the value. At this time $\operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap C_c(V)\cap \{f(x)=0\}$ is a compact set, Minimizer $f_\varepsilon$ exists, and $f_\varepsilon\in C_c(V),$ $f_\varepsilon(x)=0.$ At this time $\{f_\varepsilon\}$ has a sub-column $\{f_{\varepsilon_n}\}$ that converges to $f_0,$ $\varepsilon_n\rightarrow 0+,$ $n\rightarrow \infty.$ due to $$ \frac{\kappa_{\varepsilon_n}(x,y)}{\varepsilon_n}=\nabla_{xy}\Delta f_{\varepsilon_n}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_n}(1-\nabla_{yx}f_{\varepsilon_n})\rightarrow \kappa(x,y)<\infty, $$ There must be $\nabla_{yx}f_{\varepsilon_n}\rightarrow 1=\nabla_{yx}f_0.$ At the same time $f_{\varepsilon_n}\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)$ $\Rightarrow$ $f_0\in \operatorname{Lip}(1).$ So, $$ \begin{aligned} \kappa(x,y)&=\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty} \nabla_{xy}\Delta f_{\varepsilon_n}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_n}(1-\nabla_{yx}f_{\varepsilon_n})\\ &\ge \nabla_{xy}\Delta f_0 \ge \inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c} f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap C_c(V)\\ \nabla_{yx}f=1 \end{subarray} }\nabla_{xy}\Delta f. \end{aligned} $$ In summary, the proposition is proved. **Note 2**. *In fact, when taking the exact bound, only the integer-valued Lipschitz function can be considered. In particular, on the combination graph, the Ollivier curvature $\kappa(x,y)$ is always an integer value, $\,\forall\,x\sim y. $* **Theorem 3**. *For $x_0\neq y_0,$ we have a limit-free transportation cost representation for Ollivier curvature:* $$ \kappa(x_0,y_0)=\sup_{\rho}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c} x\in B_1(x_0)\\ y\in B_1(y_0) \end{subarray} }\rho(x,y)\left[1-\frac{d(x,y)}{d(x_0,y_0)}\right] $$ *The pairing $\rho:B_1(x_0)\times B_1(y_0)\rightarrow[0,\infty)$ satisfies* $$ \sum_y \rho(x,y)=\frac{w(x_0,x)}{m(x_0)}, \quad \,\forall\,x\in S_1(x_0); $$ $$ \sum_x \rho(x,y)=\frac{w(y_0,y)}{m(y_0)}, \quad \,\forall\,y\in S_1(y_0). $$ **Certificate:** Remember $F(\rho)=\sum_{x,y}\rho(x,y)\left[1-\frac{d(x,y)}{d(x_0,y_0)}\right],$ Need to explain $\kappa(x_0,y_0)=\sup_\rho F(\rho).$ First by definition, $$ \frac{\kappa_\varepsilon(x_0,y_0)}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(1-\frac{W(m_{x_0}^\varepsilon,m_{y_0}^\varepsilon)}{d(x_0,y_0)}\right)=\sup_{\widetilde{\rho} } F(\widetilde{\rho}), $$ Pairing $\widetilde{\rho}$ satisfies $$ \sum_y \widetilde\rho(x,y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}m_{x_0}^\varepsilon(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}1_x(x_0)+\Delta 1_x(x_0), \quad \,\forall\,x\in B_1(x_0); $$ $$ \sum_x \widetilde\rho(x,y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}m_{y_0}^\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}1_y(y_0)+\Delta 1_y(y_0), \quad \,\forall\,y\in B_1(y_0). $$ Note that for any $\widetilde\rho,$ that satisfies the condition, it also satisfies the condition of $\rho$. Therefore $\kappa(x_0,y_0)\le \sup_{\rho} F(\rho);$ In turn, given any $\rho,$ we construct: $$ \widetilde{\rho}_\varepsilon(x_0,y)=\rho(x,y)+1_x(x_0)1_y(y_0)\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\sum_{u,v}\rho(u,v)\right). $$ Then, $$ \sum_y \widetilde{\rho}_\varepsilon(x,y)=\sum_y \rho(x_0,y)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\sum_{u,y}\rho(u,y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\Delta 1_{x_0}(x_0), $$ Similarly, the sum of $x$ also satisfies the property. Note that $F(\rho)=F(\widetilde{\rho}_\varepsilon),$ Because they only differ at $(x_0,y_0)$, but this point does not contribute to $F$. This explains $\kappa(x_0,y_0)\ge \sup_\rho F(\rho).$ Gradient estimation -------- ### Permanent truncation method Perpetual cutoff method. There are two different ways. Perpetual cutoff method and discrete Ricci curvature Consider Bakry-Emery curvature in bounds with exceptions, truncate in space: $$ P_t^Wf:=\sup_{t_1+\cdots+t_n=t}Q_{t_1}^W\cdots Q_{t_n}^Wf,\quad Q_t^Wf:=S^WP_tf,\quad S^Wf:=f\vee \sup_{\overline W}f. $$ This article considers time truncation: taking the non-negative function $\phi\in C_c(V),$ $f\in [0,\phi],$ Definition: $$ Q_t^\phi f:=P_tf\wedge \phi,\quad P_t^\phi f:=\inf_{t_1+\cdots+t_n=t}Q_{t_1}^\phi\cdots Q_{t_n}^\phi f. $$ In this way, we let $\phi\rightarrow 1$. Once $P_t^\phi f\rightarrow P_t f$ is indeed established, Then the properties of $P_tf$ can be derived from the properties of $P_t^\phi f$. And $P_t^\phi f(x)$ has compact support about $x$, and many properties are easy to obtain. **Theorem 4**. *$\{P_t^\phi\}$ forms a nonlinear contraction semigroup, about $L^p$ module. For $f,g\in [0,\phi],$ $s,t\ge 0,$ we have the following properties:* (i) $P_t^\phi P_s^\phi=P_{t+s}^\phi,$ (ii) $\Vert P_t^\phi f-P_t^\phi g\Vert_p\le \Vert f-g\Vert_p,$ (iii) $P_0^\phi f=f,$ (iv) $P_t^\phi f\ge P_t^\psi g,$ if $\phi\ge \psi \ge f\ge g.$ (v) $e^{-t\deg}f\le P_t^\phi f\le P_t f,$ (vi) $P_t^\phi f$ is the Lipschitz function about $t$, (vii) $\overline{\partial^{\pm}_t}P_t^\phi f\le \Delta P_t^\phi f,$ (viii) $\partial_t P_t^\phi f(x)=\Delta P_t^\phi f(x),$ If $P_t^\phi f(x)<\phi(x),$ (ix) $P_t^{1_W} f=e^{t\Delta_W}f,$ $W$ is limited, $\Delta_W f=1_W\Delta(1_W f).$ **Certificate:** (i) From the definition, it naturally follows $$ P_t^\phi P_s^\phi f\le \inf_{\begin{subarray}{c} t_1+\cdots+t_n=t\\ s_1+\cdots+s_m=s \end{subarray} }Q_{t_1}^\phi\cdots Q_{s_m}^\phi f \ge P_{s+t}^\phi f. $$ The reverse of the first inequality, using $\,\forall\,\phi\ge g\ge f\ge 0,$ Just have $Q_t^\phi g\ge Q_t^\phi f$; the reverse of the second inequality, Just use $Q_t^\phi Q_s^\phi f\le Q_{t+s}^\phi f$. (ii) Just use $$ \Vert Q_t^\phi f-Q_t^\phi g\Vert_p\le \Vert P_tf-P_tg\Vert_p\le \Vert f-g\Vert_p, $$ Using the triangle inequality, we have $\Vert P_t^\phi f-P_t^\phi g\Vert_p\le \Vert f-g\Vert_p.$ (iii) $P_0f=f,$ $f\wedge \phi=f.$ (iv) $a\ge c$ and $b\ge d$ $\Rightarrow$ $a\wedge b\ge c\wedge d.$ (v) Just note $$ P_tf(x)\ge p_t(x,x)f(x)=e^{t\Delta}1_x(x)f(x)\ge e^{-t\deg(x)} f(x), $$ This is because $$ e^{t(\Delta+\deg(x))}1_x(x)\ge 1. $$ Thus $$ e^{-t\deg} f\le P_t f\wedge \phi=Q_t^\phi f\le P_t f\,\Rightarrow\, e^{-t\deg} f\le P_t^\phi f\le P_tf. $$ (vi) Using $\phi\in C_c(V),$ and the above estimation, we have: $$ -C_\phi t\le 1_{\operatorname{supp}\phi}(e^{-t\deg}-1) f\le P_t^\phi f-f\le 1_{\operatorname{supp}\phi}(P_t-1)f\le C_\phi t,\quad \,\forall\,f\in [0,\phi], t\ge 0. $$ From the semigroup properties, we immediately obtain $P_t^\phi f$ and $C_\phi$ as the Lipschitz constant. (vii) By doing one more first-order expansion, we have $$ \frac{1}{t}(P_t^\phi f-f)\le \frac{1}{t}1_{\operatorname{supp}\phi}(P_tf-f)\le \Delta f+C_\phi't. $$ Note that we used $1_{\operatorname{supp}\phi}\Delta f\le \Delta f,$ $f\in[0,\phi].$ Therefore, we calculate: $$ \overline{\partial_t^+}P_t^\phi f=\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+} \frac{1}{\varepsilon}(P_\varepsilon^\phi P_t^\phi f-P_t^\phi f)\le \Delta P_t^\phi f. $$ $$ \overline{\partial_t^-}P_t^\phi f=\varlimsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0-} \frac{1}{-\varepsilon}(P_{-\varepsilon}^\phi P_{t+\varepsilon}^\phi f-P_{t+\varepsilon}^\phi f)\le \varlimsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0-} \Delta P_{t+\varepsilon}^\phi f-C_\phi'\varepsilon\le \Delta P_t^\phi f. $$ In summary, $\overline{\partial_t^\pm}P_t^\phi f\le \Delta P_t^\phi f.$ (viii) We need to make some preparations. Define $\Delta^x:C(V)\rightarrow C(V),$ $$ \Delta^x f(y):=\left\{ \begin{aligned} &\Delta f(x)& y=x\\ &-\deg(y)f(y)& y\neq x \end{aligned}\right. $$ Let $P_t^x:=e^{t\Delta^x},$ then it is also sign-preserving, Because $P_t^xf(y)=e^{-t\deg(y)}f(y),$ $P_t^xf(x)=e^{t\Delta}f(x)=P_tf(x).$ **Lemma 5**. *$t>0,$ $f\in[0,\phi].$ If $P_sf(x)\le \phi(x),$ $\,\forall\,s\in[0,t],$ then $P_t^xf\le P_t^\phi f.$* **Proof:** Obviously $P_s^xf\le P_sf.$ and $P_s^xf(y)\le f(y)\le \phi(y),$ $P_s^xf(x)\le P_sf(x)\le \phi(x),$ Therefore $P_t^xf\le Q_t^\phi f.$ Inductively, for $s_1+\cdots+s_n=t,$ we have $$ P_t^xf=P_{s_1+\cdots+s_n}^x f=P_{s_1}^x\cdots P_{s_n}^x f\le Q_{s_1}^\phi\cdots Q_{s_n}^\phi f. $$ Taking off the exact bound means $P_t^xf\le P_t^\phi f.$ Back to the proposition, we only need to prove that when $P_t^\phi f(x)<\phi(x)$, $\underline{\partial_t}P_t^\phi f(x)\ge \Delta P_t^\phi f(x).$ For any $g\in[0,\phi],$ $\varepsilon>0,$ $\,\exists\,\delta\in [0,\varepsilon],$ such that $$ \frac{1}{\varepsilon}(P_\varepsilon^xg-g)(x)=\Delta^xg(x)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\partial_s^2P_s^xg(x)|_{s=\delta}\ge \Delta g(x)-C_\phi''\varepsilon. $$ Take $g=P_t^\phi f,$ then $g(x)<\phi(x),$ And $\,\exists\,\varepsilon>0$ makes $P_sg(x)\le \phi(x),$ $\,\forall\,s\in [0,\varepsilon].$ From the previous lemma, $P_\varepsilon^\phi g\ge P_\varepsilon^xg,$ Therefore $$ \underline{\partial_t^+}P_t^\phi f(x)\ge \varliminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(P_\varepsilon^xg-g)(x)\ge \Delta g(x)=\Delta P_t^\phi f(x). $$ Similarly, there exists $-\varepsilon>0$ such that $P_{t-s}^\phi f(x)\le \phi(x),$ $\,\forall\,s\in [0,-\varepsilon].$ $$ \underline{\partial_t^-}P_t^\phi f(x)\ge \varliminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0-}\frac{1}{-\varepsilon}(P_{-\varepsilon}^xP_{t+\varepsilon}^\phi f(x)-P_{t+\varepsilon}^\phi f(x))\ge \Delta P_t^\phi f(x). $$ To sum up, $\partial_t P_t^\phi f(x)=\Delta P_t^\phi f(x).$ (ix) Let $\phi=1_W.$ If $W\neq V,$ then $P_t^\phi f|_W<1,$ $\,\forall\,t>0.$ From the previous property, we immediately get $P_t^\phi f=e^{t\Delta_W}f,$ is the only solution to the Dirichlet problem on $W$. ### Truncated semigroup and Ricci curvature For general $f\in C(V),$ define $P_t^\phi f:=P_t^\phi(f\wedge \phi),$ In particular, $P_0^\phi f=f\wedge \phi.$ **Lemma 6**. *Suppose the graph $G=(V,m,w)$ satisfies $\kappa\ge K,$ $f:V\rightarrow [0,1]$ non-constant, $T>0,$ $\phi:V\rightarrow [0,1]$ compactly supported, and $\Vert\nabla\phi\Vert_\infty<\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty(1\wedge e^{-KT}). $ Then $\,\forall\,t\in [0,T],$* $$ \Vert\nabla P_t^\phi f\Vert_\infty\le e^{-Kt}\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty. $$ **Proof:** We only need to discuss the case of $\kappa>K$, and consider taking the limit of $\kappa\ge K$. Definition $$ F(t,x,y):=e^{Kt}\nabla_{yx}P_t^\phi f, $$ Just prove that $F\le \Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty.$ disproof, by compact support, Assume $F(t_0,x_0,y_0)=F_{\max}>\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty.$ $$ F(0,x_0,y_0)=\nabla_{y_0x_0}(f\wedge \phi)\le \Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty \vee\Vert\nabla \phi\Vert_\infty=\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty0.$ Note that $P_{t_0}^\phi f(x_0)<\phi(x_0),$ otherwise $$ \nabla_{y_0x_0}P_{t_0}^\phi f\le \nabla_{y_0x_0}\phi<\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty(1\wedge e^{-KT})\le \Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty e^{-Kt_0}\,\Rightarrow\, F(t_0,x_0,y_0)<\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty. $$ Therefore, by the property (*viii*), $\partial_t P_t^\phi f(x_0)|_{t=t_0}=\Delta P_{t_0}^\phi f(x_0),$ And from the properties (*vii*), $\overline{\partial_t^-}P_t^\phi f(y_0)|_{t=t_0}\le \Delta P_{t_0}^\phi f(y_0).$ Therefore, the difference is $\underline{\partial_t^-} \nabla_{x_0y_0} P_t^\phi f|_{t=t_0}\ge \nabla_{x_0y_0}\Delta P_{t_0}^\phi f.$ Note that $\Vert\nabla P_{t_0}^\phi f\Vert_\infty\le F_{\max}e^{-Kt_0},$ And $\nabla_{y_0x_0}P_{t_0}^\phi f=F_{\max} e^{-Kt_0},$ Defined by Ollivier curvature, $$ F_{\max} e^{-Kt_0}K0$. Therefore, $F\le \Vert\nabla f\Vert_{\infty},$ The proposition is proved. **Lemma 7**. *Suppose the graph $G=(V,m,w)$ satisfies $\kappa\ge K,$ $f:V\rightarrow [0,1]$ non-constant. Then $\,\forall\,t>0,$* $$ \Vert\nabla P_t f\Vert_\infty\le e^{-Kt}\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty. $$ **Proof:** Just take $T>0,$ and prove the conclusion for $t\in[0,T]$. Let $\{W_n\}$ be the exhaustive sequence of $V$, $\phi_n:V\rightarrow [0,1]$ has a compact support set, Satisfy $\phi_n|_{W_n}\equiv 1,$ $\Vert\nabla \phi_n\Vert_\infty<\Vert\nabla f\Vert_{\infty}(1\wedge e^{-KT}).$ Then $\,\forall\,x\neq y\in V,$ From the above lemma, $$ \nabla_{xy}P_t^{\phi_n}f\le e^{-Kt}\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty. $$ From the truncated semigroup property (*ix*), $e^{t\Delta_{W_n} }=P_t^{1_{W_n} }$ on $[0,1_{W_n}]$. Since $1_{W_n}\le \phi_n,$ property (*iv*) means: $$ e^{t\Delta_{W_n} }f=P_{t}^{1_{W_n} }f\le P_t^{\phi_n}f\le P_tf. $$ Recall the definition of hot semigroup, $e^{t\Delta_{W_n} }f\rightarrow P_tf,$ $n\rightarrow \infty,$ We have: $$ \nabla_{xy}P_tf=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow \infty}\nabla_{xy}P_t^{\phi_n}f\le e^{-Kt}\Vert\nabla f\Vert_{\infty}. $$ **Lemma 8**. *Suppose the graph $G=(V,m,w)$ satisfies $\kappa\ge K,$, then the graph $G$ is statistically complete.* **Certificate:** Take $\eta_i\rightarrow 1,$ $\Vert\nabla \eta_i\Vert_\infty\rightarrow 0,$ Then $\,\forall\,x\neq y\in V,$ $t>0,$ $$ \nabla_{xy}P_t1=\lim\limits_{i\rightarrow \infty}\nabla_{xy}P_t\eta_i\le \lim\limits_{i\rightarrow \infty}e^{-Kt}\Vert\nabla\eta_i\Vert_\infty=0. $$ Thus $\Vert\nabla P_t 1\Vert_\infty=0,$ $\partial_t P_t1=\Delta P_t1=0\Rightarrow$ $P_t1\equiv P_01=1.$ ### Semigroup characteristics **Theorem 9** (Gradient estimation). *Taking the graph $G=(V,m,w),$ $K\in \mathbb{R},$ then the following statements are equivalent:* (1) $\kappa\ge K;$ (2) $\,\forall\,f\in C_c(V),$ $t>0,$ $\Vert\nabla P_tf\Vert_\infty\le e^{-Kt}\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty;$ (3) $\,\forall\,f\in \ell_\infty(V),$ $t>0,$ $\Vert\nabla P_tf\Vert_\infty\le e^{-Kt}\Vert\nabla f\Vert_\infty;$ (4) $G$* is statistically complete, and *$\,\forall\,x,y\in V,$ $t>0,$ $W(p_t^x,p_t^y)\le e^{-Kt}d(x,y),$ $p_t^x:=\frac{m}{m(x)}P_t1_x$* is a hot core.* **Certificate:** $(3)\Leftrightarrow (4):$ Just note, $\,\forall\,f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap \ell^\infty(V),$ $$ \int fdp_t^x-\int fdp_t^y=\sum_{z}f(z)(p_t^x(z)-p_t^y(z))=P_tf(x)-P_tf(y). $$ $(1)\Rightarrow (3):$ The lemma we proved. $(3)\Rightarrow (2):$ Trivial. $(2)\Rightarrow (1):$ Just prove that $\,\forall\,x\sim y,$ $$ \inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap C_c(V)\\\nabla_{yx}f=1\end{subarray} }\nabla_{xy}\Delta f\ge K. $$ Take any $f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)\cap C_c(V)$ and $\nabla_{yx}f=1,$ by $(2),$ $\nabla_{yx}P_tf\le e^{-Kt}.$ Taking the derivative at $t=0$, we have: $$ \nabla_{xy}\Delta f=-\partial_t^+\nabla_{yx}P_tf|_{t=0}=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{t}(\nabla_{yx}f-\nabla_{yx}P_tf)\ge \lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\frac{1}{t}(1-e^{-Kt})=K. $$ Laplacian comparison principle ------------------ ### Basic concepts On the Riemannian manifold $M$ whose Ricci curvature is lower bounded by $K$, Taking the model space $H,$ with Ricci curvature constant $K$, we have **Laplacian comparison principle**: $$ \Delta^Md(x_0^M,\cdot)\le \Delta^Hd(x_0^H,\cdot). $$ In the discrete case, Take the Birth-Death Chain as the model space for discrete analogy. **Theorem 10** (Laplacian comparison principle). *Get the graph $G=(V,w,m),$ and take $x_0\in V,$ Let $\kappa(x_0,\cdot)\ge K,$ then* $$ \Delta d(x_0,\cdot)\le \deg(x_0)-Kd(x_0,\cdot). $$ **Certificate:** $\,\forall\,y\neq x_0\in V,$ take $f:=d(x_0,\cdot).$ Then $f\in \operatorname{Lip}(1)$ and $\nabla_{yx_0}f=1.$ Therefore, Expressed by Ollivier curvature equivalent, $$ K\le \kappa(x_0,y)\le \nabla_{x_0y}\Delta f=\frac{\Delta f(x_0)-\Delta f(y)}{d(x_0,y)}=\frac{\deg(x_0)-\Delta f(y)}{d(x_0,y)}. $$ For the chain of birth and death, that is, the graph $G=(\mathbb{N}_0,w,m)$ satisfies $w(m,n)=0,$ $\,\forall\,|m-n|\neq 1.$ Take $f(r):=d(0,r)=r,$ and calculate the curvature calculation method as: $$ \kappa(r,R)=\nabla_{rR}\Delta f=\frac{\Delta f(r)-\Delta f(R)}{R-r}. $$ For the constant curvature birth-death chain, take $r=0,$ and we have $$ K=\frac{\Delta f(0)-\Delta f(R)}{R}\,\Rightarrow\,\deg(0)-Kd(0,R)=\Delta f(R), $$ Therefore, this theorem is a discrete generalization of the Laplacian comparison principle. ### Diameter estimate **Proposition 11**. *Get the graph $G=(V,w,m),$ and get $x\neq y\in V.$ If $\kappa(x,y)>0,$ then* $$ d(x,y)\le \frac{\deg(x)+\deg(y)}{\kappa(x,y)} $$ **Proof:** For sufficiently small $\varepsilon,$ There is $W(1_x,m_x^\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\deg(x).$ Therefore, $$ W(m_x^\varepsilon,m_x^\varepsilon)\ge W(1_x,1_y)-W(1_x,m_x^\varepsilon)-W(1_y,m_y^\varepsilon)=d(x,y)-\varepsilon(\deg(x)+\deg(y)) $$ So, $$ \kappa_\varepsilon(x,y)=1-\frac{W(m_x^\varepsilon,m_y^\varepsilon)}{d(x,y)}\le \varepsilon\frac{\deg(x)+\deg(y)}{d(x,y)}. $$ Since $\kappa(x,y)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\kappa_\varepsilon(x,y)}{\varepsilon},$ Take the limit and sort it out to get the conclusion. **Corollary 12**. *Get the graph $G=(V,w,m),$ If $\kappa\ge K>0,$ then* $$ \operatorname{diam}(G)\le \frac{2\deg_{\max} }{K}. $$ Applying the Laplacian comparison principle, a better diameter estimate can be demonstrated by giving a new definition, And the attenuation related properties are given.
