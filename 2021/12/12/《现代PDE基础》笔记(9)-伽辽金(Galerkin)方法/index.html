《现代PDE基础》笔记(9)-伽辽金(Galerkin)方法 "Basics of Modern PDE" Notes (9)-Galerkin Method

Elliptic equation -------- Recall from the previous section that the existence of $Ax=b$ is equivalent to the uniqueness of $A^\ast y=0$. If $A$ is an $n\times n$ matrix, then the uniqueness of $A^\ast y=0$ is equivalent to the uniqueness of $Ay=0$. Therefore, the existence of $Ax=b$ is equivalent to uniqueness. For elliptic equations, consider the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary problem $Lu=f\in H^{-1}(\Omega).$ We have Garding's inequality: $$ \left<{}-Lu,u\right>_{L^2}\ge C_1\Vert u\Vert_1^2-C_2\Vert u\Vert^2. $$ When $C_2\le 0$, we can illustrate the uniqueness of the problem. As for the existence, According to the original method, it is necessary to consider whether there is $C_2^\ast \le 0,$ in the Garding inequality corresponding to the dual operator $L^\ast $ Thus, the existence of the original problem is explained through the uniqueness of the dual problem. But in fact, $C_2\le 0$ can directly illustrate the existence of the solution to the original problem. Consider the **Galerkin method**. The method of deriving existence from the uniqueness of the dual problem only explains the theoretical existence. The construction of the solution cannot be given specifically. However, this method can give the construction method of the solution, It is the theoretical basis of the **finite element method** in the numerical solution of differential equations. Recall that $H^{m,p}$ is a separable space, because it can be equidistantly embedded in $(L^p)^N$, The latter is a separable space. Therefore, $H_0^1(\Omega)$ is a separable Hilbert space, There is a set of complete orthonormal bases $\{\phi_i\}.$ Theoretically, The characteristic function system can be taken $-\Delta \phi_j=\lambda_j\phi_j,$ $\phi_j|_{\partial\Omega}=0.$ When the operator is self-conjugated, the characteristic function system is of course orthogonal. Since $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ is dense in $H_0^1(\Omega)$, Of course we can take $\{\phi_i\}\subset C_c^\infty(\Omega).$ Consider $u_m=\sum_{i=1}^ma_i\phi_i,$ $f=\sum_{i=1}^\infty f_i\phi_i,$ $f_m=\sum_{i=1}^m f_i\phi_i\rightharpoonup f.$ To understand $Lu=f,$ One expectation is to get $u_m$ and give $u.$ by solving $Lu_m=f_m$, but this expectation is often disappointed. Because for the equation $$ Lu_m=\sum_{i=1}^m a_iL\phi_i=\sum_{i=1}^mf_i\phi_i=f_m, $$ When $\{\phi_i\}$ does not take the characteristic function system, this will be an infinite-dimensional equation, It cannot be solved for a finite number of unknown quantities. Galerkin's approach is that we consider the following questions: $$ \left<{}Lu_m,\phi_j\right>=\left<{}f_m,\phi_j\right>,\quad j=1,\cdots,m. $$ That is $\sum_{i=1}^m a_i\left<{}L\phi_i,\phi_j\right>=\sum_{i=1}^mf_i\left<{}\phi_i,\phi_j\right>,$ $j=1,\cdots,m.$ This is the form of the matrix equation $Ax=b$. As stated at the beginning, In order to prove the existence of the solution, it is only necessary to explain the uniqueness of the solution. For this purpose, elliptic estimation can be used. Consider $\left<{}Lu_m-f_m,u_m\right>=0,$ by Garding inequality, when $C_2\le 0$, $$ \begin{aligned} C_1\Vert u_m\Vert^2_1&\le \left<{}-f_m,u_m\right>\le \Vert f_m\Vert_{-1}\Vert u_m\Vert_1\le \frac{1}{2C_1}\Vert f_m\Vert_{-1}^2+\frac{C_1}{2}\Vert u_m\Vert_1^2\\ \frac{C_1}{2}\Vert u_m\Vert^2_1&\le \frac{1}{2C_1}\Vert f_m\Vert_{-1}^2\\ \Vert u_m\Vert_1&\le \frac{1}{C_1}\Vert f_m\Vert_{-1}\le \frac{1}{C_1}\Vert f\Vert_{H^{-1} }+M \end{aligned} $$ The last inequality sign is because $f_m\rightharpoonup f$ is weakly $\ast$ convergent, It is derived that $\Vert f_m-f\Vert_{H^{-1}}$ is bounded (resonance theorem). Substitute $f_m=0$ to get the uniqueness of the solution to the equation, so the solution also exists. The matrix $A$ is invertible, We can find $u_m$ as the solution to the problem. In particular, according to the last line, we can see that $\{u_m\}\subset H_0^1(\Omega)$ is bounded. It is a Hilbert space, which is of course the dual space (reflexive) of a certain space (self), Therefore $\{u_m\}$ is weakly compact, that is, it has sub-sequences that are weakly convergent. We might as well assume that it is weakly convergent to $u.$ Assert that $u$ is a weak solution. $\,\forall\,v\in H_0^1(\Omega),$ take $v_m=\sum_{i=1}^m\xi_i\phi_i,$ Then $v_m\xrightarrow{H^1}v.$ Since $\left<{}Lu_m,\phi_j\right>=\left<{}f_m,\phi_j\right>,$ $j=1,\cdots,m.$ We have $\left<{}Lu_m,v_m\right>=\left<{}f_m,v_m\right>.$ Note that $\left<{}f_m,v_m\right>=\left<{}f_m,v_m-v\right>+\left<{}f_m,v\right>\rightarrow \left<{}f,v\right>.$ Among them $$ |\left<{}f_m,v_m-v\right>|\le \Vert f_m\Vert_{-1}\Vert v_m-v\Vert_1\le (\Vert f\Vert_{-1}+M)\Vert v_m-v\Vert_1\rightarrow 0. $$ At the same time, $$ \left<{}Lu_m,v_m\right>=-\sum_{i,j=1}^n\int_{\Omega}\partial_ju_m\partial_i(a_{ij}(x)v_m)dx-\sum_{i=1}^n \int_\Omega u_m\partial_i(b_i(x)v_m)dx+\int_\Omega c(x)u_mv_mdx. $$ Similarly, $\left<{}Lu_m,v_m\right>=\left<{}Lu_m,v_m-v\right>+\left<{}Lu_m,v\right>,$ Among them, according to strong convergence and integral by parts, $$ |\left<{}Lu_m,v_m-v\right>|\le \alpha\Vert u_m\Vert_1\Vert v_m-v\Vert_1\rightarrow 0; $$ $$ \left<{}Lu_m,v\right>=\left<{}u_m,L^\ast v\right>\rightarrow \left<{}u,L^\ast v\right>=\left<{}Lu,v\right>. $$ Therefore $\left<{}Lu_m,v_m\right>\rightarrow \left<{}Lu,v\right>.$ So $\left<{}Lu,v\right>=\left<{}f,v\right>,$ $\,\forall\,v\in H_0^1(\Omega).$ This shows that $u$ is a weak solution, This proves the existence of the solution to the original problem and provides a specific method to obtain the solution. hyperbolic equation ---------- Consider the hyperbolic equation $$ Mu=\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}-\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^n\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\left(a_{ij}(t,x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_j}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^nb_i(t,x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_i}+c(t,x)u\right)=f(t,x). $$ Among them, suppose $a_{ij}$ satisfies the **consistent ellipticity condition**: $\,\exists\,\alpha>0,$ such that $$ \sum_{i,j=1}^n a_{ij}(t,x)\xi_i\xi_j\ge \alpha\sum_{i=1}^n \xi_i^2, \quad\,\forall\,\xi\in \mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\}. $$ We consider the Galerkin method: For the Dirichlet homogeneous boundary problem, Take a set of $C_c^\infty$ complete orthonormal bases $\{\phi_i(x)\},$ of $H_0^1(\Omega)$ Then write $u_m(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i(t)\phi_i(x),$ and solve the equation given the initial value $$ \left<{}Mu_m,\phi_j\right>=\left<{}f,\phi_j\right>, \quad j=1,\cdots,m, $$ Find the coefficient $a_i(t).$ This is a system of ODE equations composed of $m$ second-order ordinary differential equations, which is always solvable. In order to illustrate that $\{u_m\}$ has subsequences that weakly converge to weak solutions, we only need to discuss its boundedness. The difference method can be considered. Here we give the energy inequality and use the energy method to solve it. Using integrals by parts, it can be deduced that $u_t$ also has a homogeneous boundary from $u$. Consider $$ \int_\Omega u_tMudx=\frac{1}{2}\partial_t \int_\Omega u_t^2dx+\int_\Omega \sum_{i,j=1}^nu_{it}a_{ij}u_jdx-\int_\Omega\sum_i u_tb_iu_idx-\int_\Omega u_tcudx=\int_\Omega u_tfdx. $$ Using symmetry, we can get $$ \int_\Omega \sum_{i,j=1}^nu_{it}a_{ij}u_jdx= \frac{1}{2}\partial_t\int_\Omega \sum_{i,j=1}^n a_{ij}u_iu_jdx-\frac{1}{2}\int_\Omega \sum_{i,j=1}^n\partial_t a_{ij}u_iu_jdx. $$ Therefore, $$ \frac{1}{2}\partial_t \int_\Omega u_t^2dx+\frac{1}{2}\partial_t\int_\Omega \sum_{i,j=1}^n a_{ij}u_iu_jdx=\int_\Omega u_tfdx+\frac{1}{2}\int_\Omega \sum_{i,j=1}^n\partial_t a_{ij}u_iu_jdx+\int_\Omega\sum_i u_tb_iu_idx+\int_\Omega u_tcudx. $$ Consider on $\overline{\Omega_T}=[0,T]\times \overline{\Omega}$, It can be obtained that $C>0,$ makes $$ \partial_t \int_\Omega u_t^2+\sum_{i,j=1}^n a_{ij}u_iu_j dx\le C(\Vert u\Vert_1^2+\Vert u_t\Vert^2+\Vert f\Vert^2) $$ Integrate both sides, then at time $t$, the initial value is recorded as $u=g,$ $u_t=h,$ then $$ \Vert u_t\Vert^2+\Vert u\Vert^2_1\le \Vert g\Vert_1^2+\Vert h\Vert^2+C\int_0^t\Vert u_t\Vert^2+\Vert u\Vert_1^2+\Vert f\Vert^2d\tau $$ Remember $E(t)=\Vert u_t\Vert^2+\Vert u\Vert^2_1,$ $E_0=\Vert g\Vert_1^2+\Vert h\Vert^2+C\int_0^T\Vert f\Vert^2d\tau,$ then $$ E(t)\le E_0+C\int_0^tE(\tau)d\tau. $$ To summarize the above methods, We transform PDE into ODE by taking modulo, and obtain an ordinary differential inequality. Then use Gronwall's inequality to get the energy inequality. **Lemma 1** (Gronwall inequality). *Suppose non-negative $E(t)\in C[0,T],$ satisfies $E(t)\le E_0+C\int_0^tE(\tau)d\tau,$ $C,E_0>0,$ then* $$ E(t)\le E_0e^{Ct}. $$ **Certificate:** Remember $I(t)=\int_0^tE(\tau)d\tau,$ Then the original formula is equal to $\partial_t I(t)\le E_0+CI(t).$ Therefore, $$ \partial_t(e^{-Ct}I(t))\le E_0e^{-Ct}\,\Rightarrow\, e^{-Ct}I(t)\le \frac{E_0}{C}(1-e^{-Ct})\,\Rightarrow\, I(t)\le \frac{E_0}{C}(e^{Ct}-1). $$ Substituting into the above formula, we have $$ E(t)=\partial_t I(t)\le E_0e^{Ct}. $$ In particular, we have $E(t)\le E_0e^{CT},$ $\,\forall\,t\in [0,T].$ Therefore locally, $u$ is bounded and therefore weakly compact. Furthermore, similarly to the elliptic equation, $$ \left<{}f,v\right>\leftarrow \left<{}Mu_m,v\right>=\left<{}u_m,M^\ast v\right>\rightarrow \left<{}u,M\ast v\right>=\left<{}Mu,v\right> $$ Therefore, $u$ that $\{u_m\}$ weakly converges to is a weak solution. Of course, The uniqueness can also be explained through the energy estimation of the dual problem, thereby giving the existence of the solution to the original problem. But again, this can only give a theoretical explanation. Let $E_0=0$ to illustrate the uniqueness, A stability statement is given for the general $E_0,$ energy method. parabolic equation ---------- Parabolic equations are similar to hyperbolic equations, and only the derivation of energy estimates is done here. $$ \left<{}Mu,u\right>=\frac{1}{2}\partial_t\int_\Omega u^2dx+\int_\Omega \sum_{i,j=1}^n a_{ij}u_iu_jdx-\int_\Omega \sum_{i=1}^n b_iu_iudx-\int_\Omega cu^2dx=\int_\Omega fudx $$ It can be explained by $ab\le \varepsilon{a^2}+\frac{b^2}{\varepsilon},$: $$ \frac{1}{2}\partial_t\Vert u\Vert^2\le \frac{1}{2}\partial_t\Vert u\Vert^2+\frac{\lambda}{2} \Vert\nabla u\Vert^2\le C(\Vert u\Vert^2+\Vert f\Vert^2) $$ Therefore, assuming the initial value $u=g,$ then $$ \Vert u\Vert^2\le \Vert g\Vert^2+C\int_0^T\Vert f\Vert d\tau + C\int_0^t \Vert u\Vert^2d\tau. $$ Remember $E(t)=\Vert u\Vert^2,$ $E_0=\Vert g\Vert^2+C\int_0^T\Vert f\Vert d\tau,$ Then similarly from Gronwall's inequality, we have $$ E(t)\le E_0e^{Ct}. $$ Therefore, the weak solution can be obtained through the Galerkin method, and the uniqueness and stability of the solution are also discussed. operator semigroup -------- Another very practical method is the operator semigroup method. For example, for parabolic problems, Consider solving the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary initial value problem: $$ \left\{\begin{aligned} &u_t=Au=\sum_{i,j=1}^n\partial_j(a_{ij}u_i)\\ &u(0,x)=g(x)\\ &u|_{\partial\Omega\times [0,T]}=0 \end{aligned}\right. $$ So formally, $u(t,x)=e^{At}g(x)$ gives the solution to the problem, because $u_t=Ae^{At}g(x)=Au,$ And satisfy the initial value and boundary conditions. Here $e^{At}:=\sum_n \frac{A^n}{n!}t^n.$ In order to give a strict explanation, take modulo the operator, then $$ \Vert e^{At}\Vert\le \sum_n\frac{\Vert A^n\Vert}{n!}t^n\le \sum_n\frac{\Vert A\Vert^n}{n!}t^n $$ The compatibility condition $\Vert AB\Vert\le \Vert A\Vert\Vert B\Vert.$ is used here. Thus if $\Vert A\Vert\le 1,$ then $\Vert e^{At}\Vert\le e^t,$ The operator is meaningful. However, in general, it is obvious that $\Vert A\Vert\le 1$ is not necessarily true. Recall that $(\lambda-A)^{-1}$ is a bounded linear operator for sufficiently large $\lambda$, Therefore, discussion on it is promising. Imitate $a=\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lambda a(\lambda-a)^{-1},$ definition $$ A_\lambda=\lambda A(\lambda-A)^{-1}=\lambda(A-\lambda+\lambda)(\lambda-A)^{-1}=-\lambda+\lambda^2(\lambda-A)^{-1}. $$ We can expect the following limits to hold: $$ e^{At}=\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow \infty}e^{A_\lambda t}=\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow \infty}e^{-\lambda t}e^{\lambda^2(\lambda-A)^{-1}t} $$ At this time $\Vert e^{A_\lambda t}\Vert=e^{-\lambda t}\Vert e^{\lambda^2(\lambda-A)^{-1}t}\Vert.$ And $\Vert(\lambda-A)^{-1}\Vert\le \frac{1}{\lambda},$ Therefore $\Vert e^{\lambda^2(\lambda-A)^{-1}t}\Vert\le e^{\lambda t},$ $\Vert e^{A_\lambda t}\Vert\le 1,$ operator is meaningful.
