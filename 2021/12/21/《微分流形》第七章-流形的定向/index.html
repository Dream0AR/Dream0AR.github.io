《微分流形》第七章-流形的定向 "Differential Manifolds" Chapter 7 - Orientation of Manifolds

Differential formal properties ---------- **Theorem 1**. *Suppose $f\in C^\infty(M,N),$ then $f^\ast \circ d=d\circ f^\ast .$* **Certificate:** $\,\forall\,\omega\in \mathcal{A}^r(N).$ When $r=0$, $$ (f^\ast du)(X)=(du)(f_\ast X)=(f_\ast X)u=X(u\circ f)=X(f^\ast u)=d(f^\ast u)(X). $$ When $r>0$, do the induction. Let's set $\omega=udy^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy^{i_r},$ Then $d\omega=du\wedge dy^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy^{i_r}.$ we have: $$ f^\ast d\omega=f^\ast [du\wedge dy^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy^{i_r}]=df^\ast u\wedge df^\ast y^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge df^\ast y^{i_r}=d[(f^\ast u) df^\ast y^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge df^\ast y^{i_r}]=df^\ast \omega. $$ Orientation of the manifold ---------- **Definition 2**. *If there is a continuous unit normal vector $\vec{n},$ on $S$, then $S$ is said to be **orientable**.* In general, given an abstract $C^\infty$-manifold $M,$ it is inconvenient to talk about normal vectors due to the lack of peripheral space. But the tangent plane always exists. We determine the normal vector by selecting a basis. For any $m$ dimensional vector space, The two sets of basis are said to be equivalent if the determinant of the transfer matrix is positive. At this time, they determine the same normal vector. It is easy to see that all basis sets fall into two equivalence classes. Choosing an equivalence class gives $V$ an orientation. In order to discuss continuity, we randomly choose $0\neq \Omega\in \Lambda^m(V^\ast ).$ At this time, for two sets of equivalent bases, $\{\widetilde{e_j}\}=\{e_i\}\det(a^i_j),$ $\Omega(\widetilde{e_1},\cdots,\widetilde{e_m})=\Omega(e_1,\cdots,e_m)\det(a^i_j).$ Therefore $\Omega$ acts on the equivalence basis with the same sign. Then selecting $0\neq \Omega\in \Lambda^m(V^\ast )$ is equivalent to giving the orientation of $V$. **Definition 3**. *Suppose $M^m$ is an $m$-dimensional $C^\infty$-manifold. If there is a continuous everywhere non-zero $m$ sub-external differential form $\Omega,$ on $M$, then $M$ is said to be **orientable**, and $(M,\Omega)$ is called an **oriented manifold**.* $\Omega$ is the continuous reflection of $M\rightarrow \Lambda^m T^\ast M$. Since $\dim \Lambda^m(T_p^\ast M)=1,$ $\Omega=fdx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^m,$ $f\in C^0(U),$ $\,\forall\,(U,x^i)\subset M.$ If $\Omega_1,\Omega_2$ are both orientations of $M$, then $\Omega_2=f\Omega_1,$ $f$ is non-zero and continuous everywhere. They have the same orientation if and only if $f>0.$ Assume $M$ is connected, Then $f$ is either positive everywhere, or negative everywhere. This gives two orientations of $M$. $dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^m\in \mathcal{A}^m(U)$ on the coordinate system $(U,x^i)$ gives the local orientation on $U$. Assume $(M,\Omega)$ is a directional manifold, then $\Omega|_U=fdx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^m,$ $f\in C^0(U).$ If $f>0,$, then the orientation of $(U,x^i)$ is said to be consistent with $\Omega$. **Theorem 4**. *Suppose $M$ is a $m$-dimensional $C^\infty$ manifold, then it is an orientable $\Leftrightarrow$ and exists in the coordinate atlas $\{(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha; x^i_{(\alpha)})\},$ makes $\,\forall\,\alpha,\beta,$ have $\det[D(\varphi_\alpha\circ\varphi_\beta^{-1})]>0.$* on $U_\alpha\cap U_\beta$ **Certificate:** $\Rightarrow:$ Take any coordinate atlas $\{(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha;x_{(\alpha)}^i)\}.$ Then $\Omega|_{U_\alpha}=f_\alpha dx^1_{(\alpha)}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{(\alpha)}^m.$ If $f_\alpha>0,$, the coordinate system does not move; if $f_\alpha<0,$ Then the coordinate system can be adjusted to make $f_\alpha$ positive. At this time $\,\forall\,p\in U_\alpha\cap U_\beta,$ $$ \Omega|_{U_\alpha\cap U_\beta}=f_\alpha{}dx_{(\alpha)}^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx_{(\alpha)}^{ {}m}=f_\beta{}dx_{(\beta)}^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx_{(\beta)}^{ {}m}. $$ Therefore $\det[D(\varphi_\alpha\circ\varphi_\beta^{-1})]=\frac{f_\beta}{f_\alpha}>0.$ $\Leftarrow:$ refines the coordinate atlas so that it is locally finite and maintains the properties of the problem. Take $f_i$ as the unit to decompose. Then let $\omega=\sum_i f_i{}dx_{(i)}^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx_{(i)}^{ {}m}$. At this time $\,\forall\,p\in M,$ $$ \omega(p)=\sum_i f_i(p){}dx_{(i)}^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx_{(i)}^{ {}m}=\sum_i f_i(p)\det[D(\varphi_i\circ \varphi_{j_0}^{-1})]{}dx_{(j_0)}^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx_{(j_0)}^{ {}m}. $$ From the unit decomposition and the properties of the question, there is $\omega\neq 0.$ It can be seen from the process that the constructed $\omega\in \mathcal{A}^m(M).$ Therefore, the external differential form that is non-zero everywhere can be required to be smooth.
