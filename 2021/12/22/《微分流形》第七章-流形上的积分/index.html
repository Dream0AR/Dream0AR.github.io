Integral in external differential form ---------------- ### Definition We are familiar with the **integral variable substitution formula**: Let $f\in C_c(\mathbb{R}^m),$ $h:\mathbb{R}^m\approx \mathbb{R}^m$ is a diffeomorphism, then $$ \int_{\mathbb{R}^m}f(x)dx^1\cdots dx^m=\int_{\mathbb{R}^m}(f\circ h)(y)|\det Dh|dy^1\cdots dy^m. $$ If $h:\mathbb{R}^m\rightarrow \mathbb{R}^m$ satisfies $\det Dh(y)>0,$ Then there is $\det Dh>0.$ everywhere, which is called a **direction-preserving diffeomorphism**. From the content of the previous lesson, $\Omega={}dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx^{ {}m}\in \mathcal{A}^m(\mathbb{R}^m)$ gives $\mathbb{R}^m$ a natural orientation. Now $(\mathbb{R}^m,\mathrm{id};x), (\mathbb{R}^m,h^{-1};y)$ are two different coordinate systems, Let $\omega=f(x)dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^m$ be the continuous $m$ times external differential form with compact support, Then due to $$ dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^m=\det(Dh)(y){}dy^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dy^{ {}m}, $$ We know that $\omega=f\circ h(y)\det(Dh){}dy^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dy^{ {}m}$ is another representation. Tips for the above formula: $$ \int_{\mathbb{R}^m}\omega:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^m}f(x)dx^1\cdots dx^m. $$ Let $(M^m,\Omega)$ be the oriented $C^\infty$-manifold, $\omega$ is the external differential form of $m$ with compact support on $M$, That is $\operatorname{supp}\omega=\overline{\{q|\omega(q)\neq 0\} }\Subset M.$ Let's define the integral of $\omega$ on $M$. When $\operatorname{supp}\omega\subset (U,\varphi;x^i),$ When the coordinate system is consistent with the orientation $\Omega$, $\omega=f dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^m,$ $f\in C_c(U).$ is defined by the previous description $$ \int_M \omega:=\int_U \omega=\int_{\varphi(U)}(f\circ\varphi^{-1})(x)dx^1\cdots dx^m. $$ Generally speaking, take $\{(U_i,\varphi_i)\}$ as the coordinate atlas consistent with the orientation, And the unit decomposition subordinate to it. Then $\omega=\sum_i f_i\omega,$ $\operatorname{supp}(f_i\omega)\subset U_i.$ has a compact support set by $\omega$, $\{\operatorname{supp}f_i\}$ is locally finite, and the sum is always meaningful. So we define $$ \int_M \omega:=\sum_i \int_M f_i\omega, $$ Where $\int_M f_i\omega$ is defined. ### Good quality We need to verify the soundness of this definition: Is it independent of the coordinate system/atlas and unit decomposition selection? If $\operatorname{supp}\omega \subset (U,\varphi;x)\cap (V,\psi;y),$ $f{}dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx^{ {}m}=\omega=g{}dy^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dy^{ {}m}.$ Need to verify: $$ \int_{\psi(U)}(g\circ\psi^{-1})(y)dy^1\cdots dy^m=\int_M\omega=\int_{\varphi(U)}(f\circ\varphi^{-1})(x)dx^1\cdots dx^m. $$ Let $h=\varphi\circ\psi^{-1},$ $h(y)=x.$ Then substitute the formula by the integral variable, And $g=f\det(Dh),$ the combined coordinate system is consistent with the orientation $\Omega$, that is, the equation is established. There are two unit decompositions $\{f_i\},\{g_j\},$ Then $\{f_ig_j=g_jf_i\}$ is also a unit decomposition. Therefore $$ \sum_j\int_M g_j\omega=\sum_{i,j}\int_M f_ig_j\omega=\sum_i\int_M f_i\omega. $$ ### Other properties It is easy to see, $\int_{(M,\Omega)}\omega=-\int_{(M,-\Omega)}\omega.$ Define $\mathcal{K}$ as the continuous $m$ form of all compact supported sets, forming a vector space, And $\int_M:\mathcal{K}\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a linear functional. Let $\varphi:N^k\rightarrow M$ be a $k$-dimensional submanifold. If $\varphi^\ast \omega$ has compact support in $N$, then $$ \int_N \varphi^\ast \omega:=\int_{\varphi(N)} \omega. $$ Stokes formula ---------- Recalling the multivariable calculus on $\mathbb{R}^m$, we can convert the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green's formula, Gauss formula and Stokes formula are unified as: $$ \int_D d\omega=\int_{\partial D}\omega,\quad D\subset \mathbb{R}^m. $$ Let $M^m$ be the $m$-dimensional $C^\infty$ manifold. If the subset $D$ in $M$ consists of the following two types of points: 1. Interior points: $p\in D,$ $\,\exists\,V\ni p$ is an open neighborhood, $V\subset D.$ 2. Boundary point: $p\in D,$ $\,\exists\,(U,\varphi;x^i)$ makes $\varphi(p)=0,$ $\varphi(U\cap D)=\{q\in D|x^m(q)\ge 0\}.$ Such a coordinate system is called an adaptive coordinate system. Then $D$ is called the **edge region** in $M$. Use $\partial D$ to represent the entire boundary. It is easy to see that all interior points $D^\circ$ are open subsets of $M$, $U\cap \partial D=\{x^m(q)=0\}$ is a $(m-1)$ dimensional slice. Thus $\partial D$ is a regular hypersurface in $M$. The overall adaptive coordinate system constitutes a cover of $\partial D$, It constitutes the coordinate atlas of $\partial D$. It determines the $C^\infty$ structure of $\partial D$. Now $M$ is an oriented manifold, then each adaptive coordinate system of $\partial D$ can be required to be consistent with the orientation $\Omega$. On each $(U_\alpha\cap \partial D;x_{\alpha}^1,\cdots,x_{\alpha}^{m-1})$, Local definition $(-1)^m{}dx_{(\alpha)}^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx_{(\alpha)}^{ {}m-1}.$ The following proves that it is consistent with the $(-1)^m{}dx_{(\beta)}^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx_{(\beta)}^{ {}m-1}$ orientation, Therefore there is an induced orientation on $\partial D$. **Theorem 1**. *Suppose $D$ is an edge region in $C^\infty$-manifold $M$. If $(M,\Omega)$ is orientable, then $\partial D$ is also orientable.* **Proof:** Take $(U,\varphi;x^i),(V,\psi;y^j)$ as the adaptive coordinate system of $\partial D$, Consistent with orientation $\Omega$, The sign of $\det\frac{D(y^1,\cdots,y^{m-1})}{D(x^1,\cdots,x^{m-1})}$ needs to be considered. It is known that $\det D(\psi\circ \varphi^{-1})>0.$ Since $\frac{\partial {}y^m}{\partial {}x^{i} }=0,$ $\,\forall\,i\neq m;$ $\frac{\partial {}y^m}{\partial {}x^m}\ge 0,$ Immediately push $\det\frac{D(y^1,\cdots,y^{m-1})}{D(x^1,\cdots,x^{m-1})}>0.$ Therefore $\partial D$ is orientable.
