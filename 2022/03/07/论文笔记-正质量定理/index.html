物理意义

以下为本非物理系学生粗略阅读物理文献, 查阅网站后对该定理物理意义的理解, 可能具有不专业之处, 请见谅.

我们熟知有万有引力, 空间中的物体会对周围的物体产生力的作用, 这种力场称为引力场. 场本身理应具有能量, 称为ADM质量, 由三位物理学家定义, 以其首字母取名.

直观来看, 在一张平坦的布上放置一个物体, 会使得整张布扭曲(物体附近下沉, 无穷远处轻微下沉, 效果随距离缩小), 这便是引力场的一个直观上的模型. 注意这张布扭曲后便构成了一个二维非平凡曲面, 我们可以根据其上诱导度量讨论几何结构, 黎曼几何知识由此引入.

为了探讨一个孤立系统的能量, 假设空间中只有这一个物体, 那么它的引力场理应在无穷远处几乎没有作用, 物理上称之为渐近平直时空. 仍以上述的布为例, 它在无穷远处是和平面接近的, 因此数学上也符合空间渐进平坦的定义.

物理上正质量定理的表述为: 一个孤立体系若其物质分布满足主能量条件, 则其ADM质量非负. 数学上, 可以建立物理量与几何量间的联系. 在特定情形下, 主能量条件可以推出空间数量曲率非负的条件. 类似于Gauss定理, 球内的能量应可在球面上检测, 定义ADM质量:

$$ E=\frac{1}{4(n-1)\omega_{n-1} }\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow \infty}\int_{S_\sigma}\sum_{i,j}(g_{ij,i}\nu_j-g_{ii,j}\nu_j)d\xi(\sigma). $$

这里$S_\sigma=\{|x|=\sigma\}.$ 逗号表示偏导, 如$g_{ij,k}:=\partial_kg_{ij}.$ $\nu=\sigma^{-1}x$为$S_\sigma$单位外法向量. 该式可积由渐进平坦的假设保证, 由此得到引力场的能量.

数学上的正质量定理, 就是在如上假设下, 证明$E\ge 0.$ 特别地, $E=0$仅在欧氏空间取到, 物理意义上即若引力场能量为零, 则对空间不产生扭曲. 按前文的例子, 没有物体放在布上.

该定理在物理意义上并没有想象中的显然, 因为对于孤立体系, 物质之间的引力结合能是负的. 在Newton引力理论中, 一个孤立点质量产生的引力场的能量不仅是负的, 而且可以趋于负无穷, 这样的孤立体系的总能量也完全可以是负的. 因此正质量定理物理方面不仅不是显而易见的结果, 还是广义相对论有别于Newton引力理论的一个重要特征.

另一方面, 特殊情形$E=0$推出空间平凡也具有重大的意义. 对于三维空间, 若ADM质量为零, 则必定是欧氏空间, 时空本身必定是Minkowski时空. 这表明Minkowski时空不仅是所有时空中能量最低的, 而且是具有最低能量值的唯一时空. 这一点对于确保Minkowski时空稳定性有重要的意义, 也意味着所有非平凡渐近平直时空的ADM质量都是正的. 这是正质量定理中”正”字的含义所在.

渐进平坦流形

取黎曼流形$(M^n,g),$ 称它是渐进平坦的, 若存在紧集$K\subset M,$ 使得有$\Phi:M\setminus K\approx \mathbb{R}^n\setminus \overline{B}_1$为微分同胚. 同时, 在由$\Phi$决定的坐标系下, $g_{ij}(x)=\delta_{ij}+O(|x|^{-p}),$ $p>\frac{n-2}{2};$ $|R(g)|=O(|x|^{-q}),$ $q>n.$ 此外还需要求:

$$ |x||g_{ij,k}(x)|+|x|^2|g_{ij,kl}(x)|=O(|x|^{-p}). $$

这里逗号指偏导. 这样便可以定义$M$的总能量. 回忆数量曲率表达式: $$ \begin{aligned} R(g)&=g^{ij}R_{ij}=g^{ij}g^{kl}R_{ikjl}\\ &=g^{ij}g^{kl}\left<{}D_{\partial_k}D_{\partial_i}\partial_j-D_{\partial_i}D_{\partial_k}\partial_j,\partial_l\right>\\ &=g^{ij}(\Gamma_{ij,k}^k-\Gamma_{kj,i}^k+\Gamma_{kl}^k\Gamma_{ij}^l-\Gamma_{il}^k\Gamma_{kj}^l) \end{aligned} $$ 联络系数表达式:

$$ \Gamma_{ij}^k=\frac{1}{2}g^{km}(g_{im,j}+g_{jm,i}-g_{ij,m})=O(|x|^{-p-1}), $$

这里注意对逆阵的估计同样是$g^{ij}(x)=\delta^{ij}+O(|x|^{-r}).$ 对逆阵求导有如下公式:

$$ \partial_kg^{ij}=-g^{ir}g_{rs,k}g^{sl}=O(|x|^{-p-1}), $$

含$g^{ij}$的项在对$i,j$求和时, 只有$i=j$占主要部分, 因此有如下估计成立: $$ \begin{aligned} g^{ij}(\Gamma_{ij,k}^k-\Gamma_{kj,i}^k)&=\frac{g^{ij}g^{km} }{2}(g_{im,jk}+g_{jm,ik}-g_{ij,mk}-g_{km,ji}-g_{jm,ki}+g_{kj,mi})+O(|x|^{-2p-2})\\ &=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,k}(g_{ik,ik}-g_{ii,kk}-g_{kk,ii}+g_{ki,ki})+O(|x|^{-2p-2})\\ &=\sum_{i,j}(g_{ij,ij}-g_{ii,jj})+O(|x|^{-2p-2}) \end{aligned} $$ 结合$\Gamma_{kl}^k\Gamma_{ij}^l-\Gamma_{il}^k\Gamma_{kj}^l=O(|x|^{-2p-2}),$ 我们得到$R(g)=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij,ij}-g_{ii,jj}+O(|x|^{-2p-2}).$ 由于$2p+2>n,$ 余项在无穷远处绝对可积. 而$R(g)$本身是可积的, 因此$\sum_{i,j}g_{ij,ij}-g_{ii,jj}$可积. 从而由分部积分, 如下极限存在:

$$ \lim_{\sigma\rightarrow \infty}\int_{S_\sigma}\sum_{i,j}(g_{ij,i}\nu_j-g_{ii,j}\nu_j)d\xi. $$

这里$S_\sigma=\{|x|=\sigma\},$ $\nu=\sigma^{-1}x$为$S_\sigma$单位外法向量. 依下式定义总能量$E=E(M,g),$

$$ E=\frac{1}{4(n-1)\omega_{n-1} }\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow \infty}\int_{S_\sigma}\sum_{i,j}(g_{ij,i}\nu_j-g_{ii,j}\nu_j)d\xi. $$

正质量定理的基本内容即是, 若处处有$R(g)\ge 0,$ 则$E\ge 0.$ 同时, $E=0$当且仅当$(M,g)$同构于欧氏空间.

Yamabe问题

共形类

对度量$g,$ 定义其共形类:

$$ [g]=\{e^ug,u\in C^\infty(M)\}. $$

1960年, Yamabe声称证明了如下命题: 给定紧黎曼流形$(M,g),$ $n\ge 3,$ 存在与$g$共形的度量, 具常数量曲率. 不过证明中出现了较大的问题, 从而该命题成为了一个问题, 称为Yamabe问题. 在24年后, Schoen给出了证明.

若$\widetilde{g}=e^ug,$ 我们来计算几何量的变化. 首先容易得到:

$$ \widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^m=\Gamma_{ij}^m+\frac{1}{2}(\delta_j^m \partial_i u+\delta_i^m\partial_ju-g^{mk}g_{ij}\partial_k u). $$

取度量$g$下$x$点处法坐标系, 回忆

$$ \widetilde{R}_{ijkl}=\widetilde{g}_{lm}(\partial_j \widetilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^m-\partial_i\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^m+\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^r\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jr}^m-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^r\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ir}^m). $$

由于$\widetilde{g}(x)_{lm}=e^{u(x)}\delta_{lm},$ $\partial_k g_{ij}(x)=0,$ $$ \begin{aligned} \delta_{lm}(\partial_j \widetilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^m(x)-\partial_i\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^m(x))=&\delta_{lm}(\partial_j\Gamma_{ik}^m(x)-\partial_i\Gamma_{jk}^m(x))\\ &+\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{il}\partial_{jk}u(x)+\delta_{jk}\partial_{il}u(x)-\delta_{jl}\partial_{ik}u(x)-\delta_{ik}\partial_{jl}u(x)) \end{aligned} $$

$$ \begin{aligned} \delta_{lm}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^r\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jr}^m-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^r\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ir}^m)=&\frac{\delta_{lm} }{4}\left\{(\delta_{i}^r\partial_k u+\delta_k^r\partial_iu-\delta^{rs}\delta_{ik}\partial_su)(\delta_{j}^m\partial_r u+\delta_r^m\partial_ju-\delta^{mt}\delta_{jr}\partial_tu)\right.\\ &\left.-(\delta_{j}^r\partial_k u+\delta_k^r\partial_ju-\delta^{rs}\delta_{jk}\partial_su)(\delta_{i}^m\partial_r u+\delta_r^m\partial_iu-\delta^{mt}\delta_{ir}\partial_tu)\right\}\\ =&\frac{1}{4}\left\{(\delta_{i}^r\partial_k u+\delta_k^r\partial_iu-\delta_{ik}\partial^ru)(\delta_{lj}\partial_r u+\delta_{lr}\partial_ju-\delta_{jr}\partial_lu)\right.\\ &\left.-(\delta_{j}^r\partial_k u+\delta_k^r\partial_ju-\delta_{jk}\partial^ru)(\delta_{li}\partial_r u+\delta_{lr}\partial_iu-\delta_{ir}\partial_lu)\right\}\\ =&\frac{1}{4}\left\{(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}-\delta_{jl}\delta_{ik})\partial_ru\partial^ru+(\delta_{jl}\partial_ku\partial_iu+\delta_{ik}\partial_ju\partial_lu-\delta_{il}\partial_ku\partial_ju-\delta_{jk}\partial_iu\partial_lu)\right\} \end{aligned} $$ 最终得到

$$ \widetilde{R}=e^{-u}\left(R-(n-1)\Delta_gu-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{4}|\nabla u|_g^2\right). $$

$n=2$时, Yamabe问题即为是否存在$\lambda\in \mathbb{R},$ $u\in C^\infty(M),$ 使得

$$ -\Delta_gu+R=\lambda e^u. $$

这个方程并不是特别困难. $n\ge 3$时, 它是一个流形上非线性分析的很好的问题. 令$e^u=v^{\frac{4}{n-2} },$ $v$为光滑正函数, 则等式化为如下形式:

$$ -\Delta_g v+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}Rv=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\widetilde{R}v^{2^\ast -1}. $$

其中$2^\ast =\frac{2n}{n-2}.$ Yamabe问题即化为是否存在$\lambda\in \mathbb{R}$以及$M$上光滑正函数$u,$ 使得

$$ -\Delta_g u+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}Ru=\lambda u^{2^\ast -1}. $$

这样就将纯几何问题化为了纯分析问题.

共形Laplace算子

由前面的式子启发, 我们定义共形Laplace算子:

$$ L:=-\Delta+c_nR,\quad c_n=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}. $$

它是共形不变的, 具体表述为如下命题:

命题 1. 设$(M,g)$为紧黎曼流形. 令$\varphi\in C^\infty(M),$ 则$n\ge 3$时, 对于$\widetilde{g}=e^{\varphi}g=\psi^{\frac{4}{n-2} }g,$

$$ L_{\widetilde{g} }u=\psi^{-(2^\ast -1)}L_g(u\psi). $$

证: $$ \begin{aligned} \Delta_{\widetilde{g} }u&=\frac{e^{-\frac{n\varphi}{2} } }{\sqrt{g} }\partial_i(e^{\frac{n-2}{2}\varphi}g^{ij}\sqrt{g}\partial_ju)\\ &=e^{-\varphi}\left(\Delta_gu+\frac{n-2}{2}\partial_i\varphi g^{ij}\partial_ju\right) \end{aligned} $$ 其中$\partial_i\varphi g^{ij} \partial_j u=\left<{}D\varphi,Du\right>_g.$ 从而, $$ \Delta_{\widetilde{g} }u=\psi^{-\frac{4}{n-2} }\left(\Delta_gu+2\frac{\left<{}D\psi,Du\right>_g}{\psi}\right)=\psi^{-(2^*-1)}(\psi \Delta_g u+2\left<{}D\psi,Du\right>_g) . $$ 从而, $$ \begin{aligned} L_{\widetilde{g} }u&=\psi^{-(2^*-1)}\left(-\psi\Delta_gu-2\left<{}D\psi,Du\right>_g+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}Ru\psi -u\Delta_g\psi\right)\\ &=\psi^{-(2^*-1)}(-\Delta_g(u\psi)+c_nRu\psi)=\psi^{-(2^*-1)}L_g(u\psi).\\ \end{aligned} $$

接下来我们定义共形平坦的概念. 称黎曼流形$(M,g)$是共形平坦的, 若$\,\forall\,x\in M,$ 存在$x$的开邻域$V,$ $u\in C^\infty(M),$ 使得$e^ug$在$V$上是平坦的.

正质量定理

度量改造

有了这些准备工作后, 便可以开始正质量定理的证明了. 首先我们将度量进行改造:

命题 2. 设$(M,g)$是渐进平坦流形, $p>\frac{n-2}{2},$ $q>n.$ 假设$R(g)\ge 0,$ 则$\,\forall\,\varepsilon>0,$ $\,\exists\,\overline{g}$使得无穷远处$(M,\overline{g})$仍是渐进平坦的, 且还是共形平坦的, 同时$R(\overline{g})\equiv 0,$ $E(\overline{g})\le E(g)+\varepsilon.$

证: 首先可以设$R(g)\equiv 0,$ 考虑共形Laplace方程$Lu=0,$ 取解$u=1+A|x|^{2-n}+O(|x|^{1-n}),$ $A\le 0.$ 这样在无穷远处, $u\in(0,1).$ 此时度量$u^{\frac{4}{n-2} }g$的数量曲率为零, 且总能量由$E(g)+A\le E(g)$给出. 因此用该度量替代$g$即可.

接下来我们将$g$在无穷远处变为欧氏度量. 取截断函数$\Psi_\sigma(x)$满足如下性质: $$ \left\{\begin{aligned} &\Psi_\sigma(x)=1,\quad |x|\le \sigma,\\ &\text{单调递减, 且} \sigma |\Psi_\sigma'|+\sigma^2|\Psi''_\sigma|\le c,\\ &\Psi_\sigma(x)=0,\quad |x|\ge 2\sigma. \end{aligned}\right. $$ 考虑度量$^{(\sigma)}g:=\Psi_\sigma g+(1-\Psi_\sigma)\delta,$ $\delta=\delta_{ij}dx^idx^j$为欧氏度量. 注意到$\sigma$充分大时, 对$\sigma$一致地有${}^{(\sigma)}g=\delta+O(|x|^{-p}),$ 且$R\left({}^{(\sigma)}g\right)=O(|x|^{-2-p}).$ 特别地, 我们有:

$$ \int_M \left|R\left({}^{(\sigma)}g\right)\right|^{\frac{n}{2} }d\omega_g=O(\sigma^{-\frac{np}{2} }). $$

因此对充分大的$\sigma,$ 有关于方程$L_\sigma u_\sigma=0,$ $u_\sigma>0,$ $u_\sigma\rightarrow 1$的唯一解. 从而度量${}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g}=u_\sigma^{\frac{4}{n-2} }{}^{(\sigma)}g$是数量平坦的, 且在无穷远处共形平坦.

最后说明$\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow \infty}E({}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g})=E(g)$即可, 这样取足够大的$\sigma$即可满足命题要求. 由对$u_\sigma,{}^{(\sigma)}g$的一致衰减估计, 给定$\varepsilon>0,$ 有不依赖于$\sigma$的$r_0$使得

$$ \left|E\left({}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g}\right)-\frac{1}{4(n-1)\omega_{n-1} }\int_{S_{r_0} }\sum_{i,j}{}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g}_{ij,i}\nu_j-{}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g}_{ii,j}\nu_jd\xi\right|\le \frac{\varepsilon}{3} $$

$$ \left|E(g)-\frac{1}{4(n-1)\omega_{n-1} }\int_{S_{r_0} }\sum_{i,j}g_{ij,i}\nu_j-g_{ii,j}\nu_jd\xi\right|\le \frac{\varepsilon}{3} $$

由于我们已经将$g$改造为数量曲率为零的度量, 在紧子集上, $\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow\infty}u_\sigma=1.$ 从而上面两式中的积分项是逼近的. 因此对充分大的$\sigma,$ $|E({}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g})-E(g)|<\varepsilon.$ 这就完成了命题的证明.

若$(M,g)$是渐进平坦, 且无穷远处共形平坦的, 则$g_{ij}=h^{\frac{4}{n-2} }(x)\delta_{ij},$ $|x|$充分大, 且$h(x)\rightarrow 1.$ 若$R(g)\equiv 0,$ 回忆共形Laplace的定义, 得知$h$为在$|x|$充分大时为调和函数, 具体形式为$h=1+E|x|^{2-n}+O(|x|^{1-n}).$ 这里我们将能量标准化, 使得$E$恰为度量$h^{\frac{4}{n-2} }\delta$的总能量. 由此我们总可以假设上述命题中的度量为这种形式, 不再用形如$g=\delta+O(|x|^{-p})$的方式表示.

极小曲面方法

定理 3 (正质量定理). 设$(M,g)$渐进平坦, $p>\frac{n-2}{2},$ $q>n,$ $R(g)\ge 0,$ 则$E(g)\ge 0,$ 且$E(g)=0$当且仅当$(M,g)$等距于$(\mathbb{R}^n,\delta).$

我们给出$n\le 7$时的证明. 对$n\ge 8$的情形, 可通过额外的技术克服极小曲面奇点的问题. $E(g)=0$的情形暂时不做讨论.

证: 反证, 假设$E(g)<0,$ 则由前小节命题, 可设$R(g)\equiv 0,$ $g_{ij}=h^{\frac{4}{n-2} }\delta_{ij},$ $h(x)=1+E|x|^{2-n}+O(|x|^{1-n}).$ 现在改造$R(g)>0$比较方便. 我们取正光滑函数$v$充分小, 且在无穷远处快速衰减. 取解$Lu=v,$ $u=1+\delta|x|^{2-n}+O(|x|^{1-n}),$ $\delta$充分小. 将度量替换为$u^{\frac{4}{n-2} }g$即可. 这样还保证了能量的负性.

我们来计算单位向量场$\eta=h^{-\frac{2}{n-2} }\partial_n$关于$g$的散度. $$ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{div}_g(\eta)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g} }\partial_i\left(\sqrt{g}\eta^i\right)=h^{-\frac{2n}{n-2} }\partial_n\left(h^{\frac{2n}{n-2} }h^{-\frac{2}{n-2} }\right)\\ &=\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}E\partial_n\left(|x|^{2-n}\right)+O\left(|x|^{-n}\right)\\ &=-2(n-1)E\frac{x^n}{|x|^n}+O\left(|x|^{-n}\right) \end{aligned} $$

特别地, 我们看到$\,\exists\,a_0\in \mathbb{R},$ $\operatorname{div}_g(\eta) \begin{cases} >0,&x^n\ge a_0\\<0,&x^n\le -a_0 \end{cases}.$ 现令$\sigma$充分大, $\Gamma_{\sigma,a}$表示$n-2$维球面

$$ \Gamma_{\sigma,a}:=\{x=(x',x^n):|x'|=\sigma,x^n=a\}. $$

$C_\sigma$表示$n-1$维柱面

$$ C_\sigma:=\{(x',x_n):|x'|=\sigma\}. $$

将$\Gamma_{\sigma,a}$作为$C_\sigma$中在它下方的圆柱部分的边界定向. 取$\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$为$n-1$维极小曲面, $\partial{\Sigma_{\sigma,a} }=\Gamma_{\sigma,a}.$ $C_\sigma$框住了内部区域$\Omega_\sigma,$ $\Sigma_{\sigma,a}\subset \Omega_\sigma.$ $n\le 7$时, 极小曲面$\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$上没有奇点.

$\,\forall\,\sigma,$ 定义

$$ V(\sigma)=\min\{\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{\sigma,a}):a\in [-a_0,a_0]\}, $$

这里$a\mapsto\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{\sigma,a})$为连续函数. 断言存在$a=a(\sigma)\in (-a_0,a_0),$ $\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{\sigma,a})=V(\sigma).$ 为了说明$a(\sigma)<a_0,$ 将$\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$写成$(\partial\Omega_{\sigma,a})\cap \Omega_\sigma$的形式, $\Omega_{\sigma,a}$为$\Omega_\sigma$在$\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$下方的部分. 令

$$ U_{\sigma,a}=\{(x',x^n)\in\Omega_{\sigma,a}:x^n>a_0-\delta\}, $$

$\delta$充分小, 使得仍有$\operatorname{div}_g(\eta)>0,$ $x^n>a_0-\sigma.$ 我们来说明$U_{\sigma,a}=\varnothing.$ 若$U_{\sigma,a}\neq \varnothing,$ $a>a_0-\delta.$ 由分部积分公式, 且由于$\eta$与$C_\sigma$纵向相切,

$$ \int_{U_{\sigma,a} }\operatorname{div}_g(\eta)d\mathcal{H}^n=\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma,a}\cap \{x^n\ge a_0-\delta\} }\left<{}\eta,\nu\right>_gd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{\sigma,a}\cap\{x^n=a_0-\delta\})>0, $$

其中$\nu$为$\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$单位法向. 由Schwartz不等式,

$$ \operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{\sigma,a}\cap \{x^n=a_0-\delta\})<\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{\sigma,a}\cap \{x^n\ge a_0-\delta\}). $$

这样, $\Sigma:=\partial(\Omega_{\sigma,a}\cap \{x^n<a_0-\delta\})\cap \Omega_\sigma$有更小的面积, $\partial\Sigma=\Gamma_{\sigma,a_0-\delta}.$ 这与$\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$极小性矛盾, 因此$a(\sigma)\le a_0-\delta,$ 类似地, $a(\sigma)\ge -a_0+\delta.$

取$\Sigma_{\sigma}=\Sigma_{\sigma,a(\sigma)},$ 使得$V(\sigma)$达到极小. 取$X_1$为$M$上向量场, 在紧集外与$\partial_n$恒同. 令$X_0$为具紧支撑的向量场, $X=X_0+\alpha X_1,$ $\alpha\in \mathbb{R}.$ 令$F_t$为由$X$生成的单参数变换群, 若$\sigma$充分大, $X_0$的紧支集包含在$\Omega_\sigma$内, 则$X$给出了$\Sigma_\sigma$的正常变分: $$ \left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\operatorname{Vol}(F_t(\Sigma_\sigma))\right|_{t=0}=0,\quad \left.\frac{d {}^2}{d {}t^2}\operatorname{Vol}(F_t(\Sigma_\sigma))\right|_{t=0}\ge 0. $$ 它的体积第二变分为$F_{X,\sigma}$的积分,

$$ F_{X,\sigma}(P)=\left.\frac{d {}^2}{d {}t^2}\left\Vert dF_t(T_P\Sigma_\sigma)\right\Vert_g\right|_{t=0}. $$

对充分大的$|x|,$ 对$\sigma$一致地有$F_{X,\sigma}(x)=O(|x|^{-n}).$ 正则性理论表明在紧集外, $\Sigma_\sigma$是函数$f_{\sigma}(x')$的图像, 且其梯度有界. 选取一系列$\sigma_i\rightarrow \infty,$ $\{\Sigma_{\sigma_i}\}$收敛到$\Sigma\subset M.$ 由于$F_{X,\sigma_i}$的一致衰减条件,

We all know that there is universal gravitation, and objects in space will exert a force on surrounding objects. This force field is called gravitational field. The field itself should have energy, which is called ADM quality, Defined by three physicists and named after their initials.

Note that after twisting this cloth, it forms a two-dimensional non-trivial surface. In order to explore the energy of an isolated system, assume that there is only this one object in the space, Then its gravitational field should have almost no effect at infinite distance, which is called physically Asymptotically flat space-time. Still taking the above cloth as an example, it is close to the plane at infinity, Therefore, it is also consistent mathematically with space asymptotically flat definition.

The expression of the positive mass theorem in physics is: If the material distribution of an isolated system satisfies the main energy condition, Then its ADM quality is non-negative. Mathematically, the connection between physical quantities and geometric quantities can be established. Under certain circumstances, The main energy condition can deduce the space Quantity curvature is non-negative The condition of . Here $S_\sigma=\{|x|=\sigma\}.$ comma represents partial derivative, Such as $g_{ij,k}:=\partial_kg_{ij}.$ $\nu=\sigma^{-1}x$ is the external normal vector of $S_\sigma$ unit. asymptotically flat manifold

Take the Riemannian manifold $(M^n,g),$ and call it asymptotically flat, if there is a compact set $K\subset M,$ So that $\Phi:M\setminus K\approx \mathbb{R}^n\setminus \overline{B}_1$ is a diffeomorphism. At the same time, in the coordinate system determined by $\Phi$, $g_{ij}(x)=\delta_{ij}+O(|x|^{-p}),$ $p>\frac{n-2}{2};$ $|R(g)|=O(|x|^{-q}),$ $q>n.$ Also required:

$$ |x||g_{ij,k}(x)|+|x|^2|g_{ij,kl}(x)|=O(|x|^{-p}). $$

The comma here refers to the partial guide. This defines the total energy of $M$. Recall the quantity curvature expression: $$ \begin{aligned} R(g)&=g^{ij}R_{ij}=g^{ij}g^{kl}R_{ikjl}\\ &=g^{ij}g^{kl}\left<{}D_{\partial_k}D_{\partial_i}\partial_j-D_{\partial_i}D_{\partial_k}\partial_j,\partial_l\right>\\ &=g^{ij}(\Gamma_{ij,k}^k-\Gamma_{kj,i}^k+\Gamma_{kl}^k\Gamma_{ij}^l-\Gamma_{il}^k\Gamma_{kj}^l) \end{aligned} $$ Contact coefficient expression:

$$ \Gamma_{ij}^k=\frac{1}{2}g^{km}(g_{im,j}+g_{jm,i}-g_{ij,m})=O(|x|^{-p-1}), $$

Note here that the estimate of the inverse matrix is also $g^{ij}(x)=\delta^{ij}+O(|x|^{-r}).$ The derivation of the inverse matrix has the following formula:

$$ \partial_kg^{ij}=-g^{ir}g_{rs,k}g^{sl}=O(|x|^{-p-1}), $$

When the terms containing $g^{ij}$ are summed over $i,j$, only $i=j$ accounts for the main part, so the following estimation is true: $$ \begin{aligned} g^{ij}(\Gamma_{ij,k}^k-\Gamma_{kj,i}^k)&=\frac{g^{ij}g^{km} }{2}(g_{im,jk}+g_{jm,ik}-g_{ij,mk}-g_{km,ji}-g_{jm,ki}+g_{kj,mi})+O(|x|^{-2p-2})\\ &=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,k}(g_{ik,ik}-g_{ii,kk}-g_{kk,ii}+g_{ki,ki})+O(|x|^{-2p-2})\\ &=\sum_{i,j}(g_{ij,ij}-g_{ii,jj})+O(|x|^{-2p-2}) \end{aligned} $$ Combine$\Gamma_{kl}^k\Gamma_{ij}^l-\Gamma_{il}^k\Gamma_{kj}^l=O(|x|^{-2p-2}),$ We get $R(g)=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij,ij}-g_{ii,jj}+O(|x|^{-2p-2}).$ Since the remainder of $2p+2>n,$ is absolutely integrable at infinity. And $R(g)$ itself is integrable, Therefore $\sum_{i,j}g_{ij,ij}-g_{ii,jj}$ is integrable. Therefore, by integration by parts, the following limit exists:

$$ \lim_{\sigma\rightarrow \infty}\int_{S_\sigma}\sum_{i,j}(g_{ij,i}\nu_j-g_{ii,j}\nu_j)d\xi. $$

Here $S_\sigma=\{|x|=\sigma\},$ $\nu=\sigma^{-1}x$ is the external normal vector of $S_\sigma$ unit. Define the total energy $E=E(M,g),$ according to the following formula

$$ E=\frac{1}{4(n-1)\omega_{n-1} }\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow \infty}\int_{S_\sigma}\sum_{i,j}(g_{ij,i}\nu_j-g_{ii,j}\nu_j)d\xi. $$

The basic content of the positive mass theorem is that if there is $R(g)\ge 0,$ everywhere, then $E\ge 0.$ at the same time, $E=0$ if and only if $(M,g)$ is isomorphic to Euclidean space.

Yamabe problem

conformal class

Define the metric $g,$ conformal class:

$$ [g]=\{e^ug,u\in C^\infty(M)\}. $$

1960, Yamabe claims to have proved the following proposition: Given a compact Riemannian manifold $(M,g),$ $n\ge 3,$ There is a metric conformal to $g$, with constant curvature. However, a bigger problem arises in the proof, The proposition thus became a problem, called Yamabe's problem. Twenty-four years later, Schoen gave a proof.

If $\widetilde{g}=e^ug,$ we calculate the change of the geometric quantity. First it is easy to get:

$$ \widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^m=\Gamma_{ij}^m+\frac{1}{2}(\delta_j^m \partial_i u+\delta_i^m\partial_ju-g^{mk}g_{ij}\partial_k u). $$

Take the normal coordinate system at point $x$ under metric $g$, recall

$$ \widetilde{R}_{ijkl}=\widetilde{g}_{lm}(\partial_j \widetilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^m-\partial_i\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^m+\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^r\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jr}^m-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^r\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ir}^m). $$

Due to $\widetilde{g}(x)_{lm}=e^{u(x)}\delta_{lm},$ $\partial_k g_{ij}(x)=0,$ $$ \begin{aligned} \delta_{lm}(\partial_j \widetilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^m(x)-\partial_i\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^m(x))=&\delta_{lm}(\partial_j\Gamma_{ik}^m(x)-\partial_i\Gamma_{jk}^m(x))\\ &+\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{il}\partial_{jk}u(x)+\delta_{jk}\partial_{il}u(x)-\delta_{jl}\partial_{ik}u(x)-\delta_{ik}\partial_{jl}u(x)) \end{aligned} $$

$$ \begin{aligned} \delta_{lm}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^r\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jr}^m-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^r\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ir}^m)=&\frac{\delta_{lm} }{4}\left\{(\delta_{i}^r\partial_k u+\delta_k^r\partial_iu-\delta^{rs}\delta_{ik}\partial_su)(\delta_{j}^m\partial_r u+\delta_r^m\partial_ju-\delta^{mt}\delta_{jr}\partial_tu)\right.\\ &\left.-(\delta_{j}^r\partial_k u+\delta_k^r\partial_ju-\delta^{rs}\delta_{jk}\partial_su)(\delta_{i}^m\partial_r u+\delta_r^m\partial_iu-\delta^{mt}\delta_{ir}\partial_tu)\right\}\\ =&\frac{1}{4}\left\{(\delta_{i}^r\partial_k u+\delta_k^r\partial_iu-\delta_{ik}\partial^ru)(\delta_{lj}\partial_r u+\delta_{lr}\partial_ju-\delta_{jr}\partial_lu)\right.\\ &\left.-(\delta_{j}^r\partial_k u+\delta_k^r\partial_ju-\delta_{jk}\partial^ru)(\delta_{li}\partial_r u+\delta_{lr}\partial_iu-\delta_{ir}\partial_lu)\right\}\\ =&\frac{1}{4}\left\{(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}-\delta_{jl}\delta_{ik})\partial_ru\partial^ru+(\delta_{jl}\partial_ku\partial_iu+\delta_{ik}\partial_ju\partial_lu-\delta_{il}\partial_ku\partial_ju-\delta_{jk}\partial_iu\partial_lu)\right\} \end{aligned} $$ Finally got

$$ \widetilde{R}=e^{-u}\left(R-(n-1)\Delta_gu-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{4}|\nabla u|_g^2\right). $$

When $n=2$, the Yamabe problem is whether there is $\lambda\in \mathbb{R},$ $u\in C^\infty(M),$ makes

$$ -\Delta_gu+R=\lambda e^u. $$

This equation is not particularly difficult. When $n\ge 3$, It is a good problem for nonlinear analysis on manifolds. Let $e^u=v^{\frac{4}{n-2} },$ $v$ is a smooth positive function, then the equation becomes as follows:

$$ -\Delta_g v+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}Rv=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\widetilde{R}v^{2^\ast -1}. $$

Among them $2^\ast =\frac{2n}{n-2}.$ The Yamabe problem is reduced to whether there is a smooth positive function $u,$ on $\lambda\in \mathbb{R}$ and $M$ make

$$ -\Delta_g u+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}Ru=\lambda u^{2^\ast -1}. $$

This turns a purely geometric problem into a purely analytical problem.

Conformal Laplace operator

Inspired by the previous formula, we define Conformal Laplace operator:

$$ L:=-\Delta+c_nR,\quad c_n=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}. $$

It is conformally invariant and can be expressed specifically as the following proposition:

Proposition 1. Let $(M,g)$ be a compact Riemannian manifold. Let $\varphi\in C^\infty(M),$ be $n\ge 3$, for $\widetilde{g}=e^{\varphi}g=\psi^{\frac{4}{n-2} }g,$

$$ L_{\widetilde{g} }u=\psi^{-(2^\ast -1)}L_g(u\psi). $$

Certificate: $$ \begin{aligned} \Delta_{\widetilde{g} }u&=\frac{e^{-\frac{n\varphi}{2} } }{\sqrt{g} }\partial_i(e^{\frac{n-2}{2}\varphi}g^{ij}\sqrt{g}\partial_ju)\\ &=e^{-\varphi}\left(\Delta_gu+\frac{n-2}{2}\partial_i\varphi g^{ij}\partial_ju\right) \end{aligned} $$ Among them $\partial_i\varphi g^{ij} \partial_j u=\left<{}D\varphi,Du\right>_g.$ Thus, $$ \Delta_{\widetilde{g} }u=\psi^{-\frac{4}{n-2} }\left(\Delta_gu+2\frac{\left<{}D\psi,Du\right>_g}{\psi}\right)=\psi^{-(2^*-1)}(\psi \Delta_g u+2\left<{}D\psi,Du\right>_g) . $$ Thus, $$ \begin{aligned} L_{\widetilde{g} }u&=\psi^{-(2^*-1)}\left(-\psi\Delta_gu-2\left<{}D\psi,Du\right>_g+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}Ru\psi -u\Delta_g\psi\right)\\ &=\psi^{-(2^*-1)}(-\Delta_g(u\psi)+c_nRu\psi)=\psi^{-(2^*-1)}L_g(u\psi).\\ \end{aligned} $$

Next we define the concept of conformally flat. We call the Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ conformally flat, If $\,\forall\,x\in M,$ exists an open neighborhood $V,$ $u\in C^\infty(M),$ of $x$ Make $e^ug$ flat on $V$.

Metric transformation

With these preparations in place, the proof of the positive mass theorem can begin. First we transform the measurement:

Proposition 2. Assume $(M,g)$ is an asymptotically flat manifold, $p>\frac{n-2}{2},$ $q>n.$ Assume $R(g)\ge 0,$ then $\,\forall\,\varepsilon>0,$ $\,\exists\,\overline{g}$ makes $(M,\overline{g})$ at infinity still asymptotically flat and conformally flat, and at the same time $R(\overline{g})\equiv 0,$ $E(\overline{g})\le E(g)+\varepsilon.$

Certificate: First, we can set $R(g)\equiv 0,$ and consider the conformal Laplace equation $Lu=0,$ Solve $u=1+A|x|^{2-n}+O(|x|^{1-n}),$ $A\le 0.$ so that at infinity, $u\in(0,1).$ At this time, the quantitative curvature of measure $u^{\frac{4}{n-2} }g$ is zero, And the total energy is given by $E(g)+A\le E(g)$. Therefore, this metric can be used instead of $g$.

Next we turn $g$ into a Euclidean metric at infinity. Take the truncation function $\Psi_\sigma(x)$ to satisfy the following properties: $$ \left\{\begin{aligned} &\Psi_\sigma(x)=1,\quad |x|\le \sigma,\\ &\text{单调递减, 且} \sigma |\Psi_\sigma'|+\sigma^2|\Psi''_\sigma|\le c,\\ &\Psi_\sigma(x)=0,\quad |x|\ge 2\sigma. \end{aligned}\right. $$ Consider Metrics$^{(\sigma)}g:=\Psi_\sigma g+(1-\Psi_\sigma)\delta,$ $\delta=\delta_{ij}dx^idx^j$ is the Euclidean metric. Note that when $\sigma$ is sufficiently large, There is ${}^{(\sigma)}g=\delta+O(|x|^{-p}),$ consistent with $\sigma$ And $R\left({}^{(\sigma)}g\right)=O(|x|^{-2-p}).$ In particular, we have:

$$ \int_M \left|R\left({}^{(\sigma)}g\right)\right|^{\frac{n}{2} }d\omega_g=O(\sigma^{-\frac{np}{2} }). $$

Therefore for sufficiently large $\sigma,$ we have the equation $L_\sigma u_\sigma=0,$ $u_\sigma>0,$ The only solution to $u_\sigma\rightarrow 1$. Thus the metric ${}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g}=u_\sigma^{\frac{4}{n-2} }{}^{(\sigma)}g$ is quantitatively flat, And it is conformally flat at infinity.

Finally, just state $\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow \infty}E({}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g})=E(g)$, In this way, a sufficiently large $\sigma$ can satisfy the proposition requirements. Estimated from the uniform attenuation of $u_\sigma,{}^{(\sigma)}g$, given $\varepsilon>0,$ There is $r_0$ that does not depend on $\sigma$ such that

$$ \left|E\left({}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g}\right)-\frac{1}{4(n-1)\omega_{n-1} }\int_{S_{r_0} }\sum_{i,j}{}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g}_{ij,i}\nu_j-{}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g}_{ii,j}\nu_jd\xi\right|\le \frac{\varepsilon}{3} $$

$$ \left|E(g)-\frac{1}{4(n-1)\omega_{n-1} }\int_{S_{r_0} }\sum_{i,j}g_{ij,i}\nu_j-g_{ii,j}\nu_jd\xi\right|\le \frac{\varepsilon}{3} $$

Since we have transformed $g$ into a metric with zero curvature, on the compact subset, $\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow\infty}u_\sigma=1.$ Therefore, the integral terms in the above two equations are approximate. Therefore, for sufficiently large $\sigma,$ $|E({}^{(\sigma)}\overline{g})-E(g)|<\varepsilon.$ This completes the proof of the proposition.

If $(M,g)$ is asymptotically flat and conformally flat at infinity, Then $g_{ij}=h^{\frac{4}{n-2} }(x)\delta_{ij},$ $|x|$ is sufficiently large, And $h(x)\rightarrow 1.$ if $R(g)\equiv 0,$ recall the definition of conformal Laplace, It is known that $h$ is a harmonic function when $|x|$ is sufficiently large, The specific form is $h=1+E|x|^{2-n}+O(|x|^{1-n}).$ where we normalize the energy, Make $E$ exactly measure the total energy of $h^{\frac{4}{n-2} }\delta$. From this we can always assume that the measure in the above proposition is of this form, It is no longer expressed in the form $g=\delta+O(|x|^{-p})$.

minimal surface method

Let's calculate the divergence of the unit vector field $\eta=h^{-\frac{2}{n-2} }\partial_n$ with respect to $g$. $$ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{div}_g(\eta)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g} }\partial_i\left(\sqrt{g}\eta^i\right)=h^{-\frac{2n}{n-2} }\partial_n\left(h^{\frac{2n}{n-2} }h^{-\frac{2}{n-2} }\right)\\ &=\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}E\partial_n\left(|x|^{2-n}\right)+O\left(|x|^{-n}\right)\\ &=-2(n-1)E\frac{x^n}{|x|^n}+O\left(|x|^{-n}\right) \end{aligned} $$

In particular, we see that $\,\exists\,a_0\in \mathbb{R},$ $\operatorname{div}_g(\eta) \begin{cases} >0,&x^n\ge a_0\\<0,&x^n\le -a_0 \end{cases}.$ Now let $\sigma$ be sufficiently large, $\Gamma_{\sigma,a}$ means $n-2$ dimensional sphere

$$ \Gamma_{\sigma,a}:=\{x=(x',x^n):|x'|=\sigma,x^n=a\}. $$

$C_\sigma$ means $n-1$ dimensional cylinder

$$ C_\sigma:=\{(x',x_n):|x'|=\sigma\}. $$

Orient $\Gamma_{\sigma,a}$ as the boundary of the cylindrical portion below it in $C_\sigma$. Let $\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$ be the $n-1$-dimensional minimal surface, $\partial{\Sigma_{\sigma,a} }=\Gamma_{\sigma,a}.$ $C_\sigma$ frames the inner area $\Omega_\sigma,$ $\Sigma_{\sigma,a}\subset \Omega_\sigma.$ $n\le 7$, There are no singular points on the minimal surface $\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$.

$\,\forall\,\sigma,$ Definition

$$ V(\sigma)=\min\{\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{\sigma,a}):a\in [-a_0,a_0]\}, $$

Here $a\mapsto\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{\sigma,a})$ is a continuous function. Assert existence$a=a(\sigma)\in (-a_0,a_0),$ $\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{\sigma,a})=V(\sigma).$ To illustrate $a(\sigma)<a_0,$ Write $\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$ in the form of $(\partial\Omega_{\sigma,a})\cap \Omega_\sigma$, $\Omega_{\sigma,a}$ is the part of $\Omega_\sigma$ below $\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$. Let

$$ U_{\sigma,a}=\{(x',x^n)\in\Omega_{\sigma,a}:x^n>a_0-\delta\}, $$

$\delta$ is small enough that there is still $\operatorname{div}_g(\eta)>0,$ $x^n>a_0-\sigma.$ Let’s explain $U_{\sigma,a}=\varnothing.$ If $U_{\sigma,a}\neq \varnothing,$ $a>a_0-\delta.$ is given by the integral by parts formula, And since $\eta$ and $C_\sigma$ are longitudinally tangent,

$$ \int_{U_{\sigma,a} }\operatorname{div}_g(\eta)d\mathcal{H}^n=\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma,a}\cap \{x^n\ge a_0-\delta\} }\left<{}\eta,\nu\right>_gd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{\sigma,a}\cap\{x^n=a_0-\delta\})>0, $$

Where $\nu$ is the unit normal direction of $\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$. According to Schwartz inequality,

$$ \operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{\sigma,a}\cap \{x^n=a_0-\delta\})<\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{\sigma,a}\cap \{x^n\ge a_0-\delta\}). $$

In this way, $\Sigma:=\partial(\Omega_{\sigma,a}\cap \{x^n<a_0-\delta\})\cap \Omega_\sigma$ has a smaller area, $\partial\Sigma=\Gamma_{\sigma,a_0-\delta}.$ This contradicts the minimality of $\Sigma_{\sigma,a}$, so $a(\sigma)\le a_0-\delta,$ Similarly, $a(\sigma)\ge -a_0+\delta.$

Take $\Sigma_{\sigma}=\Sigma_{\sigma,a(\sigma)},$ to make $V(\sigma)$ minimum. Let $X_1$ be the vector field on $M$, which is identical to $\partial_n$ outside the compact set. Let $X_0$ be a vector field with compact support, $X=X_0+\alpha X_1,$ $\alpha\in \mathbb{R}.$ Let $F_t$ be the single-parameter transformation group generated by $X$. If $\sigma$ is sufficiently large, The compact support set of $X_0$ is contained in $\Omega_\sigma$, Then $X$ gives the normal variation of $\Sigma_\sigma$: $$ \left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\operatorname{Vol}(F_t(\Sigma_\sigma))\right|_{t=0}=0,\quad \left.\frac{d {}^2}{d {}t^2}\operatorname{Vol}(F_t(\Sigma_\sigma))\right|_{t=0}\ge 0. $$ The second change of its volume is the integral of $F_{X,\sigma}$,

$$ F_{X,\sigma}(P)=\left.\frac{d {}^2}{d {}t^2}\left\Vert dF_t(T_P\Sigma_\sigma)\right\Vert_g\right|_{t=0}. $$

To $|x|,$ which is large enough, there is $F_{X,\sigma}(x)=O(|x|^{-n}).$ to $\sigma$ uniformly Regularity theory shows that outside the compact set, $\Sigma_\sigma$ is the image of function $f_{\sigma}(x')$, And its gradient is bounded. Select a series of $\sigma_i\rightarrow \infty,$ $\{\Sigma_{\sigma_i}\}$ converges to $\Sigma\subset M.$ Due to the consistent attenuation condition of $F_{X,\sigma_i}$,

$$ \int_{\Sigma}F_Xd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\ge 0,\quad F_X(P)=\frac{d {}^2}{d {}t^2}\Vert dF_t(T_P\Sigma)\Vert|_{t=0}. $$

Outside the compact set, $\Sigma$ is also the image of $f(x')$, with bounded gradient. In fact, Since $|f(x')|\le a_0,$ is determined by the regularity theory $|\partial f|(x)=O(|x'|^{-1}).$, in addition, $f$ Satisfies the minimal surface equation:

$$ \sum_{i,j}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{\partial_if\partial_jf}{1+|\nabla f|^2}\right)\partial_{ij}f+\sqrt{1+|\nabla f|^2}\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}\nu_0}\log h=0,\quad \nu_0=\frac{(-\nabla f,1)}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f|^2} }. $$

From linear theory, $f(x')=a+O(|x'|^{3-n}),$ $n\ge 4,$ and $f(x')=a+O(|x'|^{-1}),$ $n=3,$ $a\in \mathbb{R}.$ $F_X$ can be represented by the geometry of $\Sigma$:

$$ F_X=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left<{}R_{Xe_i}X,e_i\right>+\operatorname{div}_M Z+(\operatorname{div}_MX)^2+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|(D_{e_i}X)^{\perp}|^2-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\left<{}e_i,D_{e_j}X\right>\left<{}e_j,D_{e_i}X\right>. $$

Where $Z=D_{\partial_t}\frac{\partial {}F_t}{\partial {}t}$ is the changing acceleration vector field. Among them

$$ \operatorname{div}_MX=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left<{}D_{e_i}X,e_i\right>, $$

$\{e_i\}$ is the orthonormal basis of the tangent space of $\Sigma$.

Do orthogonal decomposition $X=\widetilde{X}+\varphi\nu,$ $Z=\widetilde{Z}+\psi\nu.$ Since $\Sigma$ is extremely small, $\operatorname{div}_M \chi\nu=0,$ For any function $\chi.$ we have:

$$ F_X=-\varphi^2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)-\varphi^2\Vert B\Vert^2+\Vert\nabla \varphi\Vert^2+G, $$

$$ \begin{aligned} G=&-2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\varphi\left<{}R(\widehat{X},e_i)\nu,e_i\right>-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\varphi\left<{}R(\widehat{X},e_i)\widehat{X},e_i\right>+\operatorname{div}_M\widehat{Z}+(\operatorname{div}_M\widehat{X})^2\\ &-2B(\nabla \varphi,\widehat{X})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}B(e_i,\widehat{X})^2-2\varphi\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}b_{ij}\widehat{X}_{i;j}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\widehat{X}_{i;j}\widehat{X}_{j;i}. \end{aligned} $$ We discuss the above formulas under an orthogonal frame. Where $B(-,-)$ represents the second basic type, which is $B(V,W)=\left<{}D_VW,\nu\right>.$ The semicolon refers to finding covariant derivatives, and the metric is the induced metric. The quantity related to $Z,\widehat{X}$ needs to be expressed as a bounded quantity. If $D\subset \Sigma$ is a bounded area,

$$ \int_DGd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial D}\left\{(\operatorname{div}\widehat{X})\left<{}\widehat{X},\eta\right>-\sum_{i,j}\widehat{X}_{i;j}\widehat{X}_j\eta_i-2\varphi\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}\widehat{X}_i\eta_j+\left<{}\widehat{Z},\eta\right>\right\}d\mathcal{H}^{n-2} $$

$\eta$ is the unit outer normal direction of $\partial D$ within $\Sigma$. The middle term disappears because of the Gauss-Codazzi equation and Ricci formula. For $\sigma>0,$ Let $D_\sigma=\Omega_\sigma\cap \Sigma.$ be given the attenuation condition of $f,h$, Each boundary term decays faster than $\sigma^{2-n}$, so the boundary term tends to zero, When $\sigma\rightarrow\infty$. Thus

$$ \int_\Sigma (\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+\Vert B\Vert^2)\varphi^2d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\le \int_\Sigma \Vert\nabla \varphi\Vert^2d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}. $$

Here $\varphi=\left<{}X,\nu\right>.$ since $X$ can be chosen arbitrarily within the compact set, $\varphi$ is only fixed outside a compact set as

$$ \varphi=\alpha\left<{}\partial_n,\nu\right>=\alpha h^{\frac{2}{n-2} }(1+|\nabla f|^2)^{-\frac{1}{2} }. $$

Since $\varphi-\alpha=O(|x'|^{2-n}),$ it has finite energy. Therefore, $\varphi$ can be chosen arbitrarily as long as $\varphi-\alpha$ has compact support or finite energy. From the Gauss equation,

$$ \operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+\Vert B\Vert^2=\frac{1}{2}R_M-\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\Vert B\Vert^2. $$

$R_{\Sigma}$ is the quantitative curvature of $\Sigma$ under the induced metric.

To complete the proof, first assume that $n=3,$ $\varphi\equiv 1.$ Like this$\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}R_{\Sigma}d\mathcal{H}^2>0.$ Now $\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}$ is the Gauss curvature of $\Sigma$, The attenuation estimate of $f,h$ shows that the geodesic curvature of $\partial D_{\sigma}$ converges to $2\pi.$ Apply the Gauss-Bonnet formula to $D_\sigma$, let $\sigma\rightarrow \infty,$

$$ \frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}R_{\Sigma}d\mathcal{H}^2=2\pi \chi(\Sigma)-2\pi. $$

Since $\Sigma$ is an open surface, the right term of $\chi(\Sigma)\le 1,$ is not positive. This is contradictory to the aforementioned inequality, so the proposition $n=3$ is established.

For the case of $n\ge 4$, since the induced metric $\overline{g}$ satisfies:

$$ \overline{g}_{ij}=h(x',f(x'))^{\frac{4}{n-2} }(\delta_{ij}+\partial_if\partial_jf)=\delta_{ij}+O(|x|^{2-n}), $$

$(\Sigma,\overline{g})$ is asymptotically flat and has zero energy. From the previous inequality and Gauss equation, and $R_M\ge 0,$ It shows that the minimum eigenvalue of $L_{\overline{g} }$ is positive in the compact region of $\Sigma$, Because $c(n)=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}<\frac{1}{2},$ $n\ge 3.$ Linear theory allows us to solve $L_{\overline{g} }u=0,$ $u>0,$ on $\Sigma$ Infinity $u\rightarrow 1.$ At the same time, $u$ has an expansion

$$ u(x')=1+E_0|x'|^{3-n}+O(|x'|^{2-n}). $$

In particular, $u-1$ has finite energy on $\Sigma$, take $\varphi=u.$ from $R_M>0,$

$$ -\int_{\Sigma}R_{\Sigma}u^2d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}<2\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^2d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\le c(n)^{-1}\int_\Sigma |\nabla u|^2d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}. $$

Next, $$ \begin{aligned} \int_\Sigma|\nabla u|^2d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&=\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow\infty}\int_{D_\sigma}|\nabla u|^2d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\ &=-c(n)\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow \infty} \int_{D_{\sigma} }R_{\Sigma}u^2d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial D_{\sigma} }u\frac{\partial {}u}{\partial {}\eta}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1} \end{aligned} $$

From the expansion of $u$, $E_0<0.$ Therefore $(\Sigma,u^{\frac{4}{n-3} }\overline{g})$ is asymptotically flat, with zero amount of curvature, and manifolds with negative energy. In summary, this creates a contradiction in $n=3$. Therefore we have completed the proof of $E\ge 0$.
