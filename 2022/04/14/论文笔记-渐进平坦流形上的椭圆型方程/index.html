The Mass of an Asymptotically Flat Manifold - Robert Bartnik Powered Sobolev Space --------------- We first discuss in $\mathbb{R}^n,$ $n\ge 3$. Note $r=|x|,$ $\sigma=\sqrt{1+r^2},$ respectively in $L_{\mathrm{loc} }^p(\mathbb{R}^n),$ The weighted Lebesgue space is defined in $L_{\mathrm{loc} }^p(\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\})$: $$ L_\delta^p:\quad \Vert u\Vert_{p,\delta}= \left\{ \begin{aligned} &\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}|u|^p\sigma^{-\delta p-n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p} },&p<\infty,\\ &\operatorname{ess}\operatorname{sup}_{\mathbb{R}^n}(\sigma^{-\delta}|u|), &p=\infty, \end{aligned} \right. $$ $$ {L'}_\delta^p:\quad \Vert u\Vert'_{p,\delta}= \left\{ \begin{aligned} &\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\} }|u|^pr^{-\delta p-n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p} },&p<\infty,\\ &\operatorname{ess}\operatorname{sup}_{\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\} }(r^{-\delta}|u|), &p=\infty, \end{aligned} \right. $$ Next, define the weighted Sobolev space: $$ W_\delta^{k,p}:\quad \Vert u\Vert_{k,p,\delta}=\sum_{j=0}^k\Vert D^ju\Vert_{p,\delta-j}, $$ $$ {W'}_\delta^{k,p}:\quad \Vert u\Vert'_{k,p,\delta}=\sum_{j=0}^k\Vert D^ju\Vert'_{p,\delta-j}, $$ Note that the difference in weight here makes the dimensions of the addition terms basically the same. When $p\in [1,\infty)$, $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n),$ $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\})$ is dense among them respectively. The weights are selected such that for the function $u_R(x)=u(Rx),$ $$ \Vert u\Vert'_{k,p,\delta}=R^{-\delta}\Vert u_R\Vert'_{k,p,\delta}, $$ $$ \Vert u\Vert_{k,p,\delta;A_R}\approx R^{-\delta}\Vert u_R\Vert_{k,p,\delta;A_1}, \quad R\ge 1. $$ Here $A_R:=B_{2R}\setminus B_R,$ $B_R$ is a closed sphere with radius $R$ at the origin. "$\approx$" means that the two can be controlled by a constant multiple independent of $R$, just consider controlling $\sigma$. Next we enumerate the following properties without proof: **Theorem 1**. *For $1\le p\le q\le \infty,$ $\delta_2<\delta_1,$ $u\in L_{\delta_2}^q,$ there is $\Vert u\Vert_{p,\delta_1}\le c\Vert u\Vert_{q,\delta_2}, $ Therefore $L_{\delta_2}^q\subset L_{\delta_1}^p.$ That is, the higher $p$ is, the smaller $\delta$ is, the better the properties of $L_\delta^p$ are, and the higher the function requirements are.* **Theorem 2** (Hölder's inequality). *$u\in L_{\delta_1}^q,$ $v\in L_{\delta_2}^r,$ $\delta=\delta_1+\delta_2,$ $1\le p,q,r\le \infty,$ $\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r},$ then* $$ \Vert uv\Vert_{p,\delta}\le \Vert u\Vert_{q,\delta_1}\Vert v\Vert_{r,\delta_2}. $$ **Theorem 3** (Interpolation Inequality). *$\,\forall\,\varepsilon>0,$ $\,\exists\,C(\varepsilon)>0,$ $\,\forall\,u\in W_{\delta}^{2,p},$ $p\in [1,\infty],$* $$ \Vert u\Vert_{1,p,\delta}\le \varepsilon\Vert u\Vert_{2,p,\delta}+C(\varepsilon)\Vert u\Vert_{0,p,\delta}. $$ **Theorem 4** (Sobolev’s inequality). *If $u\in W_{\delta}^{k,p},$ then* $$ \Vert u\Vert_{q,\delta}\le C\Vert u\Vert_{k,p,\delta},\quad 0<\frac{n}{p}-k\le \frac{n}{q}\le \frac{n}{p}; $$ $$ \Vert u\Vert_{\infty,\delta}\le C\Vert u\Vert_{k,p,\delta},\quad \frac{n}{p}-k<0,\quad \text{且} |u(x)|=o(r^{\delta}). $$ Define the weighted Hölder module: $$ \Vert u\Vert_{C_\delta^{0,\alpha} }:=\sup_{x\in \mathbb{R}^n}\left(\sigma^{-\delta+\alpha}(x)\sup_{4|x-y|\le \sigma(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha} }\right)+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^n}\sigma^{-\delta}(x)|u(x)|. $$ **Theorem 5** (Negative exponential Sobolev inequality). *If $u\in W_{\delta}^{k,p},$ $0<\alpha\le k-\frac{n}{p}\le 1,$ then* $$ \Vert u\Vert_{C_{\delta}^{0,\alpha} }\le C\Vert u\Vert_{k,p,\delta},\quad \Vert u\Vert_{C_{\delta}^{0,\alpha}(A_R)}=o(1). $$ **Theorem 6** (Poincaré inequality). *$\,\forall\,u\in {W'}_{\delta}^{1,p},$ $1\le p<\infty,$ $\delta\neq 0,$* $$ \Vert u\Vert_{p,\delta}'\le |\delta|^{-1}\Vert u_r\Vert'_{p,\delta-1}\le |\delta|^{-1}\Vert Du\Vert'_{p,\delta-1},\quad u_r=\partial_ru=r^{-1}x\cdot Du. $$ $$ \Vert u\Vert_{p,\delta}\le C\Vert u_r\Vert_{p,\delta-1},\quad \delta<0,\quad u\in W_{\delta}^{1,p}. $$ **Lemma 7**. *$k>j,$ $\delta<\varepsilon,$ $1\le p<\infty,$ $W_\delta^{k,p}\Subset W_{\varepsilon}^{j,p}$ is a compact embedding.* Asymptotic elliptic operator ---------- ### Inequality estimation **Definition 8**. *Operator $Pu:=a^{ij}(x)\partial_{ij}^2u+b^i(x)\partial_iu+c(x)u$ is called (asymptotic)** to $\Delta$ (at rate $\tau$), if $\,\exists\,n}{|x||y|},$ $P_j^\lambda$ is an ultraspherical function, which appears in the Taylor expansion: $$ |x-y|^{2-n}=|x|^{2-n}\sum_0^\infty P_j^\lambda(\mu)\left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\right)^j,\quad |y|<|x|. $$ First, let's verify that the convolution kernel $K$ indeed defines the bounded operator of ${W'}_{\delta-2}^{0,p}\rightarrow {W'}_{\delta}^{0,p}$. Taking the case of $k\ge 0$ as an example, we have an estimate $$ |K(x,y)|\le c(n,k)|x-y|^{2-n} \left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{k+1} &\frac{|x|}{|y|}<\frac{1}{2},\\ &\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{n+k-2} & \frac{|x|}{|y|}\ge \frac{1}{2}. \end{aligned}\right. $$ We have the following lemma: **Lemma 11**. *Fix $p\in (1,\infty),$ $\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p'}=1,$ $a,b\in \mathbb{R}$ and $a+b>0.$ There is a kernel $$ K'(x,y)=|x|^{-a}|x-y|^{2-n}|y|^{-b},\quad x\neq y. $$ * *For $u\in L^p(\mathbb{R}^n),$ define $K'u(x)=\int K'(x,y)u(y)dy,$ then $K':L^p\rightarrow L^p$ is a bounded operator if and only if $a<\frac{n}{p},$ $b<\frac{n}{p'}.$* Let $K_1,K_2$ be the accounting operators $|x-y|^{2-n}\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^\alpha,$ Corresponding to $\alpha=k+1,$ $\alpha=n+k-2$ respectively. The above lemma shows that when $3-n+k<\delta1.$ in the asymptotic condition, then the kernel of operator $P:W_\delta^{2,p}\rightarrow W_{\delta-2}^{0,p}$ is finite-dimensional, has a closed range, and for any $u\in W_{\delta}^{2,p},$ has constant $C, R$ is controlled by $P,\delta,n,p,q$, such that* $$ \Vert u\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\le C(\Vert Pu\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+\Vert u\Vert_{L^p(B_R)}). $$ **Proof:** Take the operator module $$ \Vert P-\Delta\Vert_{op}:=\sup\{\Vert(P-\Delta)u\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}:u\in W_{\delta}^{2,p},\Vert u\Vert_{2,p,\delta}=1\}. $$ Similarly define $\Vert\cdot\Vert_{op,R},$ The selected function $u$ is required to have a support set in $E_R=\mathbb{R}^n\setminus B_R$, That is, zero is taken in $B_R$. Then if $\operatorname{supp}(u)\subset E_R,$ is given by $q>n,$ $$ \begin{aligned} \Vert(P-\Delta)u\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}\le& \sup_{|x|>R}\{|a^{ij}(x)-\delta_{ij}|\}\Vert D^2u\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}\\ &+C\Vert b\Vert_{0,q,-1;E_R}\Vert Du\Vert_{1,p,\delta-1}+C\Vert c\Vert_{0,\frac{q}{2},-2;E_R}\Vert u\Vert_{2,p,\delta} \end{aligned} $$ Since $P$ is asymptotic to $\Delta,$ $$ \Vert P-\Delta\Vert_{op,R}=o(1),\quad R\rightarrow \infty. $$ Take $\chi\in C_c^\infty(B_2),$ $\chi\in [0,1],$ $\chi|_{B_1}=1.$ Take the undetermined constant $R,$ and let $\chi_R(x)=\chi(\frac{x}{R}),$ $u_0=\chi_Ru,$ $u_\infty=(1-\chi_R)u,$ $u=u_0+u_\infty.$ From the previous theorem, we have: $$ \begin{aligned} \Vert u_\infty\Vert_{2,p,\delta}&\le C\Vert\Delta u_{\infty}\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}=C\Vert(\Delta-P+P) u_{\infty}\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}\\ &\le C(\Vert Pu_{\infty}\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+\Vert P-\Delta\Vert_{op,R}\Vert u_{\infty}\Vert_{2,p,\delta}) \end{aligned} $$ Since $\Vert P-\Delta\Vert_{op,R}=o(1),$ has for sufficiently large $R,$ $$ \Vert u_{\infty}\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\le C\Vert Pu_\infty\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}. $$ To further estimate, there are $$ \begin{aligned} \Vert Pu_\infty\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}&\le \Vert Pu\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+\Vert 2a^{ij}u_i\partial_j\chi_R+(a^{ij}\partial^2_{ij}\chi_R+b^i\partial_i \chi_R)u\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2;A_R}\\ &\le \Vert Pu\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+C\Vert u\Vert_{1,p,\delta;A_R} \end{aligned} $$ Then use the interpolation inequality to further transform the $\Vert\cdot\Vert_{1,p,\delta}$ term, And $L^p$ estimate, that is, we have $$ \Vert u_\infty\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\le C(\Vert Pu\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+\Vert u\Vert_{0,p,\delta;A_R}). $$ At the same time, it is estimated by $L^p$, for $u_0$ $$ \Vert u_0\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\le C(\Vert Pu_0\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+\Vert u_0\Vert_{0,p,\delta})\le C(\Vert Pu\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+\Vert Pu_{\infty}\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+\Vert u\Vert_{0,p,\delta;{B_{2R} } }). $$ Using the previous estimates about $Pu_\infty$ again, we get the inequalities required by the theorem: $$ \Vert u\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\le \Vert u_0\Vert_{2,p,\delta}+\Vert u_\infty\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\le C(\Vert Pu\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}+\Vert u\Vert_{L^p(B_R)}). $$ Next, we discuss the finiteness of the kernel dimension. Let $\{u_k\}$ be the function sequence in $\ker P$, Satisfies $\Vert u_k\Vert_{2,p,\delta}=1.$ By the compact embedding theorem, It may be assumed that $\{u_k\}$ itself converges strongly in $L^p(B_R)$. At this time, since $Pu_k\equiv 0,$ The inequality just proved shows that $\{u_k\}$ is the Cauchy column in $W_\delta^{2,p}$. Therefore, the unit sphere in $\ker P$ is compact, so its dimension is limited. Since the dimension of $\ker P$ is limited, there is a decomposition of $W_\delta^{2,p}=\ker P\oplus Z, and $ $Z$ is a closed set. Assert that there exists a constant $C$ such that $$ \Vert u\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\le C\Vert Pu\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2},\quad \,\forall\,u\in Z. $$ Otherwise, there exists $\{u_k\},$ $\Vert u_k\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\equiv 1,$ And $\Vert Pu_k\Vert_{0,p,\delta-2}\rightarrow 0.$ At this time, according to the compact embedding theorem, Using the inequality in the theorem, we once again obtain that $\{u_k\}$ is the Cauchy column in $W_{\delta}^{2,p}$. Due to the closure of $Z$, it converges to $u_0\in Z.$ However, due to the continuity of $P$, $Pu_0=0,$ $u_0\in \ker P.$ thus $u_0=0,$ But this is contradictory to $\Vert u_k\Vert_{2,p,\delta}\equiv 1$, Because $u_k\rightarrow u_0.$ then the assertion is true, immediately indicating that $P$ has a closed range. ### Semi-Fredholm nature Next, we further consider the dimension of the kernel of $P$. Note $$ N(P,\delta)=\dim\ker (P:W_\delta^{2,p}\rightarrow W_{\delta-2}^{0,p}), \quad 10, $ has an estimate* $$ \Vert u-\ker P\Vert_{X}=\Vert u\Vert_{X/\ker P}\le C\Vert Pu\Vert_Y. $$ *At the same time, there*$\varepsilon>0,$ * Such that for any other semi-Fredholm operator *$P',$ that satisfies *$\Vert P-P'\Vert_{op}<\varepsilon$* $$ \dim \ker P'\le \dim \ker P. $$ **Proof:** It is known that $\operatorname{Im}P$ is closed, then it is a Banach space, Therefore the first estimate is obtained immediately from the inverse operator theorem. Now let $\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2C},$ Assuming that the kernel dimension inequality does not hold, then there exists $u\in \ker P',$ $\Vert u\Vert_X=1,$ $\Vert u-\ker P\Vert_X>\frac{1}{2}.$ at this time $$ \frac{1}{2}<\Vert u-\ker P\Vert_{X}\le C\Vert Pu\Vert_{Y}\le C\Vert P-P'\Vert_{op}\Vert u\Vert_X0, $ makes* $$ \Vert u-\ker P'\Vert_{X}\le C\Vert P'u\Vert_Y,\quad \,\forall\,u\in X,P'\in \mathcal{U}, \Vert P-P'\Vert_{op}\le \varepsilon. $$ **Proof:** Otherwise, there exists the sequence $P_k\rightarrow P\in \mathcal{U},$ $\{u_k\}\subset X,$ satisfies $\Vert u_k\Vert_X=1,$ $\Vert u_k-\ker P_k\Vert_{X}>\frac{1}{2},$ and $$ \Vert u_k-\ker P_k\Vert_{X}\ge k\Vert P_ku_k\Vert_Y. $$ This shows that $P_ku_k\rightarrow 0.$ At this time, we can continue to use the tight embedding method in the previous theorem to obtain the subsequence $u_k\rightarrow u,$ Satisfy $Pu=0,$ $\Vert u\Vert_X=1,$ And for sufficiently large $k$, we have $\Vert u-\ker P_k\Vert\ge \frac{1}{4}.$ Similarly, any $v_k\in \ker P_k,$ $\Vert v_k\Vert_X=1,$ In the same way, it also has sub-columns that converge to $v\in \ker P.$ This shows that the $N$-dimensional $\ker P_k$ converges to the $N$-dimensional subspace in $\ker P$. However, according to the estimation in the previous paragraph, $u$ is not in such a subspace, but $u\in \ker P.$ This shows that $\dim \ker P\ge N+1,$ is contradictory.
