《黎曼几何初步》笔记(6)-弧长第一与第二变分公式 "Preliminary Riemannian Geometry" notes (6) - the first and second variational formulas of arc length

first variation formula ---------- Let's study the extremely short length of the $\gamma$ curve. Take the single-parameter curve family $\{\gamma_u\}_{-\varepsilon\le u\le \varepsilon},$ Satisfies $\gamma_0=\gamma,$ $\gamma_u(a)=\gamma(a),$ $\gamma_u(b)=\gamma(b).$ Let $L(u):[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be the length of curve $\gamma_u$. Then when $L'(0)=0,$ $L''(0)>0$, there is a minimum value at $L(0)$; If this holds true for all single-parameter curve families $\{\gamma_u\}$ that satisfy the conditions, Then $\gamma$ locally obtains a very short length. Let’s first cancel the assumptions $\gamma_u(a)=\gamma(a),$ $\gamma_u(b)=\gamma(b)$, Let $U$ be the transverse vector field of $\{\gamma_u\}$, $T$ is tangent to all curves $\gamma_u(t).$ Let $t$ be the arc length parameter of the base curve $\gamma$, $|\dot\gamma|=1.$ then, $$ L(u)=\int_a^b |\dot\gamma_u(t)|dt,\quad L'(u)=\int_a^b\frac{d {} }{d {}u}\sqrt{\left<{}\dot\gamma_u(t),\dot\gamma_u(t)\right>}dt, $$ The integral term can be written as: $$ U\sqrt{\left<{}T,T\right>}=\frac{1}{|T|}\left<{}T,D_UT\right>=\frac{1}{|T|}\left<{}T,D_TU\right>=\frac{1}{|T|}\left(T\left<{}T,U\right>-\left<{}D_TT,U\right>\right), $$ In this way we get the first variation formula of arc length: $$ L'(0)=\int_a^b\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\left<{}\dot\gamma,U\right>-\left<{}D_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma,U\right>dt=\left<{}\dot\gamma(t),U(t)\right>|_a^b-\int_a^b\left<{}\ddot\gamma(t),U(t)\right>. $$ Call $\{\gamma_u\}$ the **normal variation** of $\gamma$, if $\gamma_u(a)=\gamma(a),$ $\gamma_u(b)=\gamma(b),$ $\gamma_0=\gamma.$ For normal variation, $U(a)=U(b)=0,$ Therefore the first term in the first variation formula disappears. Now we can see another equivalent description of the geodesic: **Lemma 1**. *The necessary and sufficient condition for curve $\gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow M$ to be a geodesic is that it is the critical point of the length function with respect to all normal variations.* Let $N$ be a closed submanifold in $M$, $x\notin N,$ Then $\,\exists\,y\in N$ makes $d(x,y)=d(x,N).$ Take the shortest regular geodesic $\gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow M$ to connect $x,y.$ **Lemma 2**. *$\gamma\perp N,$ is $\dot\gamma(b)\perp N_y.$* **Proof:** Let $Y\in N_y,$ take $\xi:[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\rightarrow N,$ $\xi(0)=y,$ $\dot\xi(0)=Y.$ Then we can get the single-parameter curve family $\{\gamma_u\},$ $\gamma_0=\gamma,$ $\gamma_u(a)=\gamma(a)=x,$ $\gamma_u(b)=\xi(u).$ In this way, the transverse vector field $U$ satisfies $U(x)=0,$ $U(y)=Y,$ thus: $$ L'(0)=\left<{}\dot\gamma(b),U(b)\right>=\left<{}\dot\gamma(b),Y\right>=0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \gamma\perp N. $$ second variation formula ---------- Generally, it is meaningful to calculate $L''(0)$ when $L'(0)=0$. Therefore, assuming $\gamma$ is a regular geodesic, then: $$ L''(u)=\int_a^b\frac{d {} }{d {}u}\left(\frac{1}{|T|}\left<{}T,D_TU\right>\right)dt, $$ The integral term can be written as: $$ \begin{aligned} U\left(\frac{1}{|T|}\left<{}T,D_TU\right>\right)&=-\frac{1}{|T|^3}\left<{}T,D_TU\right>^2+\frac{1}{|T|}\left<{}D_TU,D_TU\right>+\frac{1}{|T|}\left<{}T,D_UD_TU\right>\\ &=-\frac{1}{|T|^3}\left(T\left<{}T,U\right>-\left<{}D_TT,U\right>\right)^2+\frac{1}{|T|}|D_TU|^2+\frac{1}{|T|}\left(-\left<{}T,R_{UT}U\right>+\left<{}D_TD_UU,T\right>\right). \end{aligned} $$ When brought to $u=0$, note that $\gamma$ is a regular geodesic at this time, Therefore: $$ \begin{aligned} L''(0)&=\int_a^b -\left(\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\left<{}\dot\gamma,U\right>\right)^2+|\dot U(t)|^2-\left<{}\dot \gamma,R_{U\dot\gamma}U\right>+\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\left<{}D_UU,\dot\gamma\right>dt\\ &=\left<{}D_UU,\dot\gamma\right>|^b_a+\int_a^b\left(|\dot U(t)|^2-\left<{}R_{\dot\gamma U}\dot\gamma,U\right>-\left(\left<{}\dot\gamma,U\right>'\right)^2\right)dt. \end{aligned} $$ This is the **arc length second variation formula**, the determining term is the curvature term, Call $\left<{}D_UU,\dot\gamma\right>|^b_a$ as boundary term, The boundary term disappears when $\{\gamma_u\}$ is a normal variation. Since the tangential component of $U(t)$ along $\dot\gamma$ does not change the length of $\gamma$, We only need to consider the orthogonal components $U^\perp(t),$ That is, suppose $\left<{}U^\perp,\dot\gamma\right>=0,$ $U=U^\perp+f\dot\gamma.$ Then $$ |\dot U(t)|^2-\left(\left<{}\dot\gamma(t),U(t)\right>'\right)^2=|\dot U^\perp (t)|^2+f'(t)^2-f'(t)^2=|\dot U^\perp(t)|^2, $$ $$ \left<{}R_{\dot\gamma U}\dot\gamma,U\right>=\left<{}R_{\dot\gamma U^\perp}\dot\gamma, U^\perp\right>. $$ Therefore, the second variation formula of arc length can also be written as: $$ L''(0)=\left<{}D_UU,\dot\gamma\right>|^b_a+\int_a^b \left(|\dot U^\perp(t)|^2-\left<{}R_{\dot\gamma U^\perp}\dot\gamma,U^\perp\right>\right)dt. $$ Later applications will be added in the future.
