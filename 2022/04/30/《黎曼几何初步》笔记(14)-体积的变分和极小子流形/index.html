first variation of volume ---------- This section considers the generalization of geodesics: $k$-dimensional **minimal submanifold**. Let an immersed compact submanifold with boundaries be given in $\widetilde{M}$: $$ f:M\rightarrow \widetilde{M}, \quad\dim M=n<\widetilde{n}=\dim\widetilde{M}. $$ Let $C^\infty$ map $F:M\times [0,\varepsilon]\rightarrow\widetilde{M},$ $f_t(x):=F(x,t).$ If $f_0=f,$ $f_t|_{\partial M}=f|_{\partial M},$ $f_t$ is immersion, then $\{M_t\}$ is said to be the **normal variation** of $M$, and $M_t$ is the image of $f_t$. The **variation vector field** along a given immersion $f$ is $W=dF\left(\left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\right|_{t=0}\right).$ Volume $V(t):=V(M_t)$ refers to the volume of $M$ under $f_t^\ast \widetilde{g}$, then when $V'=0$, $M$ is said to be **steady-state** with respect to $\widetilde{M}$. If for each directed compact subregion $D with $C^\infty$ boundary $\partial D$ in $M$, If $D$ is stationary with respect to every normal variation, then $M$ is said to be **minimum**. Since $f:M\rightarrow\widetilde{M}$ is an immersion, locally $f$ is an embedding, Therefore, $M$ and $f(M)$ can be identified, The average curvature normal vector field $\eta$ of $f(M)$ is called the **average curvature normal vector field** of $M$ in $\widetilde{M}$, The **average curvature** of $M$ is $|\eta|.$ **Lemma 1**. *The immersed submanifold $f:M\rightarrow \widetilde{M}$ is extremely small, and the necessary and sufficient condition is that its average curvature is zero.* In fact it is a direct corollary of the following more precise theorem. **Proposition 2** (First variation of volume). *Suppose $M$ is a directional compact manifold with boundaries immersed in $\widetilde{M}$, and $\{M_t\}$ is its normal variation, then* $$ V'(0)=\int_M\left<{}\eta,W\right>dv. $$ **Proof:** Let $\Omega_t$ be the volume element measuring $f_t^\ast \widetilde{g}$ on $M$. Let us prove $$ \left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\right|_{t=0}\Omega_t=(\operatorname{div}\top W)\Omega_0+\left<{}\eta,W\right>\Omega_0. $$ Once the formula is established, then according to Green's theorem and $W|_{\partial M}=0,$ we have: $$ V'(0)=\int_M\left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\right|_{t=0}\Omega_t=\int_{\partial M}\left<{}\top W,n\right>dv+\int_M\left<{}\eta,W\right>\Omega_0=\int_M\left<{}\eta,W\right>\Omega_0. $$ For volume elements, we have: $$ \Omega_t=\sqrt{G_t}{}dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx^{ {}n}. $$ Remember $f_t^\ast \widetilde{g}=(g_t)_{ij}dx^idx^j=\left<{}-,-\right>_t,$ $G_t=\det[(g_t)_{ij}],$ $\Omega_{\mathbb{R} }={}dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{}dx^{ {}n},$ Then $$ \left<{}\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^1}\wedge\cdots\wedge \frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^n},\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^1}\wedge\cdots\wedge \frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^n}\right>_t=\det\left[\left<{}\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^i},\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^j}\right>_t\right]=\det[(g_t)_{ij}]=G_t. $$ So we have: $$ \left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\right|_{t=0}\Omega_t=\left(\left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\right|_{t=0}\sqrt{G_t}\right)\Omega_{\mathbb{R} }=\frac{1}{\sqrt{G_0} }\left<{}\widetilde{D}_W\left(\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^n}\right),\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^1}\wedge\cdots\wedge \frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^n}\right>_0\Omega_{\mathbb{R} }. $$ Note $\perp W=\nu,$ $\top W=\tau,$ then: $$ \begin{aligned} \widetilde{D}_W\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}{}x^{1} }\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}{}x^{ {}n} }=&\sum_i\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^1}\wedge\cdots\wedge \widetilde{D}_W\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^i}\wedge \cdots\wedge \frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^n}.\\ =&\sum_i\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^1}\wedge\cdots\wedge \widetilde{D}_{\frac{\partial \frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}x^n}.\\ =&\left(\operatorname{tr}(X\mapsto \widetilde{D}_X\tau)+\operatorname{tr}A_\nu\right)\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}{}x^{1} }\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}{}x^{ {}n} }\\ =&(\operatorname{div}\tau+\left<{}\eta,\nu\right>)\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}{}x^{1} }\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}{}x^{ {}n} }\\ =&(\operatorname{div}\top W+\left<{}\eta,W\right>)\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}{}x^{1} }\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{\partial {} }{\partial {}{}x^{ {}n} } \end{aligned} $$ Thus, $$ \left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\right|_{t=0}\Omega_t=(\operatorname{div}\top W+\left<{}\eta,W\right>)\sqrt{G_0}\,\Omega_{\mathbb{R} }=(\operatorname{div}\top W)\Omega_0+\left<{}\eta,W\right>\Omega_0. $$ Assume $M$ is the immersion hypersurface in $\widetilde{M}$, then the average curvature of $M$ is exactly the sum of the principal curvatures, So we have the following inference: **Corollary 3**. *The necessary and sufficient condition for a hypersurface to be a minimal submanifold is that the sum of its principal curvatures at every point is zero.* Using the Gauss equation, we can get the following results. **Corollary 4**. *The minimal submanifold of a manifold with non-positive cross-section curvature has non-positive Ricci curvature.* By establishing the relationship between the Laplace operator $\Delta$ and $\widetilde{D}^2f$ on $M$, We also have the following conclusions: **Corollary 5**. *There is no compact minimal submanifold in the simply connected complete manifold $\widetilde{M}$ with non-positive cross-section curvature.* In fact, after taking a fixed coordinate system locally, the average curvature $|\eta|=0$ can be represented by $f$, This will be a **quasilinear** elliptical system of equations, Therefore, the study of minimal submanifolds is closely related to partial differential equations. In particular, When $\widetilde{M}=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}$, $M$ is the minimum image if and only if $$ \sum_{i,j} g^{ij}\frac{\partial^2 {}f^A}{\partial {}y^i\partial {}y^j}=0,\quad A=n+1,\cdots,n+k, $$ Where $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\sum_A\frac{\partial {}f^A}{\partial {}y^i}\frac{\partial {}f^A}{\partial {}y^j}.$ second variation of volume ---------- The same assumption as above, and we only discuss the situation based on $V'(0)=0$, because then the second variation is meaningful. From the first variation of the volume, the tangential component $\top W$ does not play a role, Therefore, we might as well always assume that $\{M_t\}$ is **normal normal variation**, that is, $W\perp f(M).$ Let $M$ be a minimal submanifold immersed in $\widetilde{M}$, If for each orientable compact subdomain $D with $C^\infty$ boundary $\partial D$, Each of its normal variations has a non-negative volume second variation, then $M$ is said to be **stable**. That is to say, the stable minimal submanifold has normal variation for all normal variations with compact supports, All have non-negative second variations of volume. For any $D,$ with the same conditions as above If any $C^\infty$ submanifold $\overline{D},$ in $\widetilde{M}$ has the same boundary as $D$, There is always $V(\overline{D})\ge V(D),$, then the minimal submanifold is said to be **(overall) minimized**. Let $\mathscr{N}(M)$ be the **method bundle** of $M$, define mapping for each $X\in \mathscr{T}(M),$ $$ \nabla_X:\mathscr{N}(M)\rightarrow \mathscr{N}(M), \quad \nabla_X\nu=\perp\widetilde{D}_X\nu. $$ It is easy to verify that $\nabla$ has all the properties of contact, Call it an **induced contact** on the $M$ method bundle $\mathscr{N}(M)$. Similarly, Arbitrarily choosing the unitary frame field $\{E_i\},$ in $M$, we define the Laplace operator of $\nabla$: $$ \overline{\Delta}\nu:=\operatorname{tr}\nabla^2\nu=\sum_{i=1}^n \nabla_{E_i}\nabla_{E_i}\nu-\nabla_{D_{E_i}E_i}\nu,\quad \,\forall\,\nu\in \mathscr{N}(M). $$ For any standard field $\{E_i\},$ We then introduce the **Ricci tensor** on $\mathscr{N}(M)$$\overline{\operatorname{Ric} }:$ $$ \overline{\operatorname{Ric} }:\mathscr{N}(M)\rightarrow\mathscr{N}(M),\quad \overline{\operatorname{Ric} }(\nu)=\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde{R}_{E_i\nu}E_i. $$ For the second basic form $A_\nu:M_x\rightarrow M_x,$ Define the operator norm for any standard orthonormal basis $\{e_i\},$ in any $M_x$: $$ |A_\nu|^2:=\sum_{i,j}\left<{}A_\nu(e_i),e_j\right>^2=\sum_{i,j}\left<{}S(e_i,e_j),\nu\right>^2. $$ Let $S^t$ represent the transpose of $S:M_x\otimes M_x\rightarrow M_x^\perp$, That is $S^t:M_x^\perp\rightarrow M_x\otimes M_x,$ such that $$ \left<{}S^t(\mu),V\otimes W\right>=\left<{}\mu,S(V,W)\right>,\quad \,\forall\,\mu\in M_x^\perp, \: V,W\in M_x. $$ Then based on linear algebra knowledge ($\Vert A\Vert^2=\operatorname{tr}(A^TA)=\operatorname{tr}(AA^T)$), $$ |A_\nu|^2=\left<{}(S\circ S^t)(\nu),\nu\right>. $$ **Proposition 6** (Second variation of volume). *Suppose $M$ is a boundary-oriented compact manifold minimally immersed in $\widetilde{M}$, $f:M\rightarrow\widetilde{M},$ and $\{M_t\}$ is the normal normal variation of $M$, and the variation vector field is $\nu\in \mathscr{N}(M),$ $V(t)=V(M_t).$ Then,* $$ V''(0)=\int_M\left<{}-\overline{\Delta}\nu-\overline{\operatorname{Ric} }(\nu)-(S\circ S^t)(\nu),\nu\right>dv $$ **Proof:** First, from the first variation of volume, we can get: $$ V'(t)=\int_M\left<{}\eta_t,\nu_t\right>\Omega_t, $$ Among them, locally $\eta_t$ is the average curvature normal vector field of $f_t(M)\subset \widetilde{M}$, $\nu_t$ is the normal variation vector field on $f_t(M)$. Further, $$ V''(0)=\int_M\left<{}\widetilde{D}_\nu\eta,\nu\right>\Omega+\left<{}\eta,\widetilde{D}_\nu\nu\right>\Omega+\left<{}\eta,\nu\right>\left.\frac{d {} }{d {}t}\right|_{t=0}\Omega_t. $$ Since $\eta=0,$ through direct calculation, we can get: $$ V''(0)=\int_M\left<{}\widetilde{D}_\nu\eta,\nu\right>\Omega=\int_M\left(\left<{}-\overline{\Delta}\nu-\overline{\operatorname{Ric} }(\nu)-(S\circ S^t)(\nu),\nu\right>+f\right)\Omega, $$ Among them, $f$ satisfies $\int_Mf\Omega=0.$ and thus we get the conclusion. The specific calculation process is skipped here. When $M$ is a minimal hypersurface in $\widetilde{M}$, and $\widetilde{M}$ can be oriented, Take the unit normal vector field $N\in\mathscr{N}(M),$ then $\nu=uN.$ At this time $\nabla_XN=0,$ Therefore $\overline{\Delta} \nu=(\Delta u)N.$ Secondly, $$ \left<{}\overline{\operatorname{Ric} }(\nu),\nu\right>=u^2\sum_{i=1}^n\left<{}\widetilde{\operatorname{Ric} }_{E_iN}E_i,N\right>=u^2\widetilde{\operatorname{Ric} }(N,N). $$ Finally, $$ \left<{}(S\circ S^t)(\nu),\nu\right>=u^2|A_N|^2=u^2\sum_i p_i^2, $$ $\{p_i\}$ is the principal curvature of $M\subset \widetilde{M}$. Let $A_N$ be $A,$ Then the second variation formula at this time is: $$ V''(0)=\int_M(-u\Delta u-u^2(\widetilde{\operatorname{Ric} }(N,N)+|A|^2))dv. $$ From this formula, we can derive the following proposition: **Proposition 7**. *Assume $M,\widetilde{M}$ can be oriented, $M$ is a hypersurface that is minimally immersed in $\widetilde{M}$, then the necessary and sufficient conditions for $M$ to be stable are:* $$ \int_M u^2(\widetilde{\operatorname{Ric} }(N,N)+|A|^2)dv\le \int_M |du|^2 dv,\quad \,\forall\,u\in C_c^\infty(M,\mathbb{R}). $$ **Theorem 8** (Schoen-Yau theorem). *Suppose $\widetilde{M}$ is a compact directed $3$-dimensional manifold with positive curvature, then there is no compact, stable minimal immersed $2$-dimensional directed submanifold with positive genus in $\widetilde{M}$.* **Proof:** Proof by contradiction, suppose $M$ is such a submanifold. Since $M$ itself is compact, Let $u\equiv 1,$ in the second variation formula be $$ \int_M(\widetilde{\operatorname{Ric} }(N,N)+|A|^2)dv\le 0. $$ Furthermore, it can be proven that: $$ \widetilde{\operatorname{Ric} }(N,N)+|A|^2=\frac{1}{2}\sigma-K+\frac{1}{2}|A|^2,\eqno{(\ast )} $$ where $\sigma$ is the limit of the quantity curvature of $\widetilde{M}$ on $M$, $K$ is the Gauss curvature of the induced metric on $M$. Integration gives: $$ 0\ge \frac{1}{2}\int_M\sigma dv-\int_MKdv+\frac{1}{2}\int_M|A|^2dv. $$ By assumption, $\int_M\sigma dv>0;$ By Gauss-Bonnet theorem, $$ \int_MKdv=2\pi \chi(M)=4\pi(1-g)\le 0. $$ Finally, the contradiction can be deduced by combining $\int_M|A|^2dv\ge 0$. So we only need to prove the $(\ast )$ formula. Take a set of standard orthonormal bases $\{e_1,e_2\},$ of $M_x$ Make the second fundamental form $A$ diagonal, that is $$ A(e_1)=\lambda e_1,\quad A(e_2)=\mu e_2. $$ Since $M$ is extremely small, $\operatorname{tr}A=0,$ $\lambda=-\mu.$ Therefore $|A|^2=2\lambda^2.$ Let $N=e_3,$ Then $\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}$ constitutes a set of orthonormal basis of $\widetilde{M}_x$. Remember $\widetilde{K}(\operatorname{span}\{e_i,e_j\})=\widetilde{K}_{ij},$ then $$ \sigma=\sum_{i,j}\widetilde{K}_{ij}=2(\widetilde{\operatorname{Ric} }(N,N)+\widetilde{K}_{12}). $$ According to the Gauss formula, $\widetilde{K}_{12}=K-\det(A)=K+\lambda^2,$ Therefore, the formula $(\ast )$ can be proved: $$ \widetilde{\operatorname{Ric} }(N,N)+|A|^2=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sigma-\widetilde{K}_{12}\right)+2\lambda^2=\frac{1}{2}\sigma-K+\lambda^2=\frac{1}{2}\sigma-K+\frac{1}{2}|A|^2. $$ This theorem is the key to the following conclusion, which is closely related to the **Positive Mass Theorem**: **Theorem 9**. *If the Riemannian metric in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is flat outside the compact set $K$ and has non-negative quantity curvature everywhere, then it must be flat.* Finally, we look at the results of minimal hypersurfaces in Euclidean space. **Lemma 10**. *Suppose $M$ is a minimal hypersurface in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.
